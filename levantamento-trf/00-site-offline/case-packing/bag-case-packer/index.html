What is a bag case packer?

A bag case packer is a secondary packaging machine that takes filled and sealed flexible bags from the bagger, groups them into the count the case requires and loads them into the case from above. It sits between the bagging line and case sealing, conveying and palletizing.

TRF uses three mechanisms for bags. Controlled gravity loading collates bags above the case and drops them along a guided path, which suits repetitive formats. Robotic pick-and-place lifts each bag or group and sets it in position, which suits variable positions, many SKUs and patterns where orientation matters. An integrated machine erects the case, loads it robotically and seals it, which suits lower volumes that want fewer machines. TRF supplies all three as top-load systems; the top load case packer page explains the format.

Bags are hard to pack because no two are exactly the same shape:

  • Slack fill and settling. Product settles after bagging, so the same bag can present a different profile at the packer.
  • Trapped air. Air in the bag changes its height and stability in the case and how many bags fit the pattern.
  • Seal geometry and film. Fin seals and gussets stack differently, and some films stick to each other or to guides.
  • Temperature. Frozen bags are stiff and may carry frost, while warm product can make film tacky.

That is why TRF validates every application with real bags at production condition.

How does gravity drop packing work for flexible bags?

Gravity drop packing collates bags above the open case and releases them through a controlled drop, so the bags land in the pattern the case requires without a robot picking each one. TRFDroppack is built on this controlled drop process, which keeps operation simple.

The advantages come from the design. Fewer complex components mean less to maintain, and with no robotic end-of-arm tooling there is no gripper to change when the bag changes. Vertical product movement can also give a compact packing architecture, which helps when space between the bagger and the case sealer is limited.

The limits are equally clear. The product has to tolerate a controlled drop, and the pack pattern has to repeat so collation can build it. Bags that must lie in a specific orientation, overlap precisely or be handled with extra care are usually a robotic application. TRF evaluates the drop with your actual bag, because fill level, seal quality and product density change how a bag behaves on the way down.

The pack pattern ties the bag, the case and the pallet together: bags per case, layers, orientation and how the case closes. A case that is easy to pack but unstable on the pallet moves the problem downstream, so TRF validates the pattern with the case and the palletizing step together.

Which bags can a bag case packer handle?

TRFDroppack packs VFFS and other flexible bags, IQF and frozen food bags, pet food, rice and grains, and grated cheese bags, plus pillow bags and pouches. TRFRapidpack also packs pouches and pillow bags, and TRFOnepack packs pillow bags. Each bag type below has its own engineering questions.

IQF and Frozen Food Bags

IQF product bags and frozen food bags fit gravity packing with TRFDroppack when formats repeat, contents flow freely and the pattern is standardized. Cold packing areas change how film behaves and add frost, so TRF validates film behavior and pattern stability with real frozen product. Downstream, TRFSubzero palletizes in areas below 32°F. See frozen food and ice cream automation.

Pet Food Bags

TRFDroppack packs pet food bags. Retail pet food bags packed into shipping cases are a case packing application, and the case format is defined together with the palletizing pattern because bag size and case weight change both. See pet food packaging automation.

Rice, Grains and Free-Flowing Products

TRFDroppack packs rice, grains and similar free-flowing packaged products. These bags settle into a stable shape, which is what gravity packing needs, so the engineering questions are about stability in the case: bags per case, layers, flat or on edge, and how the case closes. See food processing automation.

Grated Cheese Bags

TRFDroppack packs grated cheese bags. The bag is soft, the contents shift easily and the product is usually handled cold, so the pattern has to hold without crushing the bags below. Repetitive formats favor TRFDroppack. See dairy and cheese automation.

Which bag case packer fits your line setup and speed?

Choose by line setup and speed. The three TRF case packing platforms form one family, and the right model is defined mainly by how repetitive your bags and patterns are, how bags arrive, whether the case must be erected and sealed in the same machine and the products per minute you run.

TRFDroppack is the gravity drop packer for repetitive flexible bags on lines that need medium to low throughput without robotic complexity. It can run as a standalone case packer or as part of a complete TRF end-of-line system.

TRFRapidpack fits high-mix lines where bag position, orientation or product mix defeat mechanical collation. Changeovers are recipe-based, and the same platform also covers pouches and pillow bags on mixed lines.

TRFOnepack fits plants automating manual or semi-automatic packing at lower volume that want one machine instead of three: case erecting, robotic loading and sealing on one controls platform, with a smaller footprint.

Sealed cases then travel on TRFIntraflow to a TRF palletizer. The case packing page compares the three platforms side by side.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What Is the Throughput Range of a Gravity Case Packer?

TRFDroppack is designed for applications of about 20 to 500 products per minute, for lines that need gravity loading without robotic complexity. TRF publishes application ranges, not machine speeds: actual capacity depends on the bag, the collation, the drop path, the pack pattern and the case, and TRF engineering validates it with your real bags before the design is closed.

Does a Case Packer Need Vision-Guided Robotics for High-Mix Production?

Only when bag position varies or the line runs many formats. TRFRapidpack picks with vision-guided delta robots when bags reach the packer in a random flow, and with indexed picking when they arrive in fixed lanes. Repetitive bags in standardized patterns usually need neither, which is where TRFDroppack and its controlled gravity loading fit.

Do All Bags Need to Be Packed in Cases Before Palletizing?

No. Many large bags and sacks ship directly on pallets without a shipping case, which makes them a palletizing application rather than a case packing one. TRF covers both: case packing with TRFDroppack, TRFRapidpack or TRFOnepack, and direct palletizing on the bag palletizer page, with the gripper and pallet pattern engineered for each bag.

What Is the Difference Between a Case Packer and a Case Erector?

A case erector forms an open case from a flat blank, and a case packer loads products into that case. TRF supplies case erecting only as part of an integrated solution: TRFOnepack erects, packs and seals in one machine, and end-of-line projects include case forming and closing, so one partner answers for the flow from flat case to sealed case.

How Fast Can a Case Packer Change Over Between SKUs?

It depends on how different the bags, patterns and cases are. On TRFRapidpack, product and pattern changes are recipe-based with reduced mechanical adjustments. On TRFDroppack, there is no robotic tooling to change, and the changeover plan for the formats the line runs is set during engineering. Count how many changeovers you run per week, because that number shapes the choice.

Can Case Packing Equipment Integrate With My Existing Palletizer and Conveyors?

Yes. Each TRF case packing platform can run standalone or integrate with conveyors, case handling, palletizing and intralogistics as one end-of-line system. TRFIntraflow moves and accumulates packed cases to a TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed palletizer, and interfaces with your bagger and existing equipment are defined during project engineering. See end-of-line integration.

What Is the Typical ROI of Switching From Manual to Robotic Case Packing?

There is no typical figure, because payback depends on your plant. The estimate compares the installed cost of the system with the annual savings: packers per shift, shifts per day, local labor cost, the output the bagger gains when the packing table stops limiting it, damaged bags and rejected cases avoided, and time saved at changeovers. A TRF Sales Engineer builds it with your data.
