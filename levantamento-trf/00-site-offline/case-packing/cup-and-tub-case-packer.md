## What Is a Cup and Tub Case Packer?

A cup and tub case packer is a secondary packaging machine that collates filled, sealed cups or tubs and loads them into a shipping case from above, in the count and pattern the case requires. It sits after the filler and sealer and before case sealing, conveying and palletizing.

The machine does three things in sequence. It receives the containers, usually in lanes from the filling and sealing line. It groups them into the layout the case needs, in one or more layers. Then it places the group into an open case, by robotic pick-and-place or inside an integrated machine that also erects and seals the case. All TRF case packers load from the top; the [top load case packer](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/top-load-case-packer/) page explains the format across the family.

Cups and tubs arrive at the end of the line in high counts and frequent format changes: single cups, multipacks, promotional packs and private label versions of the same product. Manual packing works until volume, SKU count or labor availability makes it the slowest step on the line. Cup and tub lines are common in [dairy and cheese](https://trfautomation.com/industries/dairy-and-cheese/), [frozen food and ice cream](https://trfautomation.com/industries/frozen-food-and-ice-cream/) and [food processing](https://trfautomation.com/industries/food-processing/) plants, including prepared foods.

## How Does a Case Packer Protect Cups, Tubs and Lid Seals?

A case packer protects cups and tubs by picking them on surfaces that can take the load and placing them rather than dropping them. Cups and tubs are rigid enough to pick one by one or in groups, but a machine can only grip the lid, the rim and the sidewall.

- **The lid is the seal.** Picking on a foil or film lid has to hold the container without deforming the seal. Where the lid cannot take the load, the tool picks from the sides or supports the container underneath.
- **Tapered walls.** Most cups and tubs taper, so side gripping has to account for the angle and for how containers sit against each other in the case.
- **Cold and condensation.** Refrigerated and frozen containers can arrive wet or frosted, which changes how tooling grips them.
- **Multipacks and sleeves.** Banded, shrink-wrapped or sleeved multipacks behave differently from single cups and are treated as a separate format in the recipe.
- **Label orientation.** Retail-ready cases may need labels facing one way, which adds an orientation step to the pick and place.

TRFRapidpack is designed for controlled handling of delicate, flexible or irregularly shaped products. TRF validates the tooling against your real containers, including cold product, before the machine is built.

## How Are Cups and Tubs Packed Into Cases?

Cups and tubs are packed upright in a pattern defined by container size, case size, count per case and the number of layers. Retail-ready cases, club-store formats and e-commerce cases can use the same container with different patterns, and that is where changeover time becomes a real cost.

TRFRapidpack changes products and patterns by recipe, with reduced mechanical adjustments, so switching from one cup format to another is mostly a recipe selection plus any tooling change the containers require. TRFOnepack runs multiple products, recipes and packing patterns through robotic loading within the validated application. Each format is validated during project engineering.

Settle a few questions before the pattern is fixed: which counts per case you actually ship, whether pads or dividers go between layers, how the case is closed and whether a specific face must be visible on the shelf.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a Cup and Tub Case Packer Need a Vision System?

Only when product position varies. When containers arrive in controlled lanes from the sealer, indexed picking is enough. When the flow is variable or several SKUs share the infeed, TRFRapidpack can pack with vision-guided delta robots that locate each container before the pick. The choice depends on your infeed and on how many formats the machine runs.

### What Is the Difference Between an Integrated Case Packer and Separate Erecting, Packing and Sealing Machines?

An integrated case packer erects the case, loads it and seals it in one coordinated machine, with one controls platform. Separate machines each need their own interfaces, controls and floor space. TRFOnepack is the integrated option, for applications of about 20 to 50 products per minute, and it can start standalone and later join a complete TRF end-of-line system.

### What Floor Space Does an Integrated Case Packing Machine Require?

It depends on the case size, the pack pattern, the infeed and how operators reach the machine. What changes the answer most is consolidation: TRFOnepack combines case erecting, robotic packing and case sealing in one machine architecture, which takes less floor space than three machines in series. TRF places the machine in your floor plan, with infeed and discharge, during the application review.

### How Many Products per Minute Can a Robotic Case Packer Run?

TRFRapidpack is designed for applications of about 100 to 200 products per minute. TRF publishes application ranges, not machine speeds: actual capacity depends on the container, the pick tool, the pack pattern and how cups and tubs leave the sealer, and TRF engineering validates it for each line before the design is closed.

### Can Case Packing Equipment Integrate With My Existing Palletizer and Conveyors?

Yes. Each TRF case packing platform can run standalone or integrate with conveyors, case handling, palletizing and intralogistics as one end-of-line system. The signals and buffers between your filler and the case packer, and the handoff to a TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed palletizer, are defined during project engineering. See [end-of-line integration](https://trfautomation.com/conveyor-systems/end-of-line-integration/).

### What Is the Typical ROI of Switching From Manual to Robotic Case Packing?

There is no typical figure, because payback depends on your plant. The estimate compares the installed cost of the system with the annual savings: packers per shift, shifts per day, local labor cost, cases the line can ship once manual packing stops limiting it, damaged lids and dropped containers avoided, and time saved at changeovers. A TRF Sales Engineer builds it with your data.
