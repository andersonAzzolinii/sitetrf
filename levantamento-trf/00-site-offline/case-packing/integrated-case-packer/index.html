Why do manufacturers choose an integrated case packer?

Small and mid-sized food and CPG manufacturers choose TRFOnepack to automate secondary packaging in one step. These are the six reasons.

Integration

Case erecting, robotic packing and sealing are managed as one coordinated system, not as three machines to connect.

Labor Reduction

Manual work across several secondary packaging operations moves into one machine, freeing operators for other tasks.

Simplicity

Fewer equipment interfaces, controls and integration points make the project easier to specify, install and run.

Small Footprint

One machine architecture takes the place of separate erecting, packing and sealing stations, so the case step needs less floor space.

Flexibility

Robotic loading handles multiple products, recipes and packing patterns within the validated application envelope.

Reliability

An integrated architecture reduces handoffs between machines and is designed around continuous production flow.

What is an integrated case packing machine, and how is it different from separate erecting, packing and sealing machines?

An integrated case packing machine erects the case from a flat blank, loads the products into it and seals it inside one coordinated system. It covers the whole secondary packaging step, from flat case to sealed case, ready for conveying and palletizing. Form, pack, seal: three processes, one solution.

The alternative is a line of separate equipment: a case erector, then a case packer machine, then a case sealer, each with its own frame, controls and interfaces. That arrangement works, but it multiplies the interfaces to specify, install, connect and support, and it takes more floor space.

Inside the machine, product loading is robotic, so integration does not lock the line to a single format. The trade-off is scope and speed: lines that need higher rates, vision-guided picking or a very wide range of formats belong with a dedicated robotic cell such as TRFRapidpack.

Which applications fit an integrated case packer?

TRFOnepack fits small and mid-sized food and CPG manufacturers that want to replace manual or semi-automatic secondary packaging with one machine that erects, packs and seals, at low to medium throughput. It packs these formats from the top:

  • Pillow bags
  • Thermoformed packages
  • Trays
  • Cartons
  • Cups
  • Small packaged products, validated per application

Typical plants are in food processing, including prepared foods, and in consumer packaged goods, where the case step still depends on manual or semi-automatic work.

Is your line ready for an integrated case packing machine?

Your line is a strong candidate for TRFOnepack when most of these statements are true:

  • Operators erect, pack and seal cases by hand, or on semi-automatic equipment.
  • Packing rates fall inside the TRFOnepack application range.
  • The products are pillow bags, thermoformed packages, trays, cartons, cups or other small packaged products.
  • Floor space for three separate machines is limited.
  • You want fewer equipment interfaces and one partner for the whole case step.
  • The line runs more than one product or packing pattern.

Before quoting, TRF validates five factors with your line data: the product (format, weight and how it behaves when picked), the case blank and the range of case formats, the packing pattern, the sealing method and the layout, including the product infeed and the discharge to conveying and palletizing.

How do you choose between TRFRapidpack, TRFOnepack and TRFDroppack?

Choose by rate, product behavior and how much of the case process belongs in one machine. The three TRF case packing platforms form one family, and line setup and speed define the model.

Platform Application range Packing principle Typical fit
TRFRapidpack About 100 to 200 products per minute Delta robots, vision-guided or indexed picking High-mix lines, multiple SKUs, variable product flow
TRFOnepack About 20 to 50 products per minute Case erecting, robotic packing and case sealing in one machine Small and mid-sized plants consolidating secondary packaging
TRFDroppack About 20 to 500 products per minute Controlled gravity loading Repetitive flexible packages and standardized patterns

Two lines at the same rate can land on different platforms. A plant with no case erector on the floor and a standard pattern is a strong TRFOnepack candidate, because the machine brings the missing processes with it. A plant that already erects and seals its cases, and changes formats often, may be better served by a dedicated robotic cell. The case packing page and the top-load case packer guide compare the family in more detail.

Behind the machine, TRFIntraflow conveys the sealed cases and TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed build the pallet, so the whole end of line comes from one partner.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What Floor Space Does an Integrated Case Packing Machine Require?

The space depends on the case formats, the product infeed and how the machine sits in your line. The principle stays the same: TRFOnepack consolidates case erecting, packing and sealing into one machine architecture, so it replaces three separate frames and the conveyors between them. TRF confirms the footprint for your plant with a layout study during the application review.

How Much Does a Case Packing Machine Cost?

The cost depends on your products, case formats, rates and the integration scope around the machine. TRFOnepack combines erecting, packing and sealing in one capital project, so the scope is defined once instead of three times. Payback comes from the manual labor removed across those three steps, the shifts the line runs and the separate machines you no longer need to buy and connect.

How Fast Can a Case Packer Change Over Between SKUs?

It depends on what changes between the SKUs. On TRFOnepack, robotic loading supports multiple products, recipes and packing patterns within the validated application envelope, so a product or pattern change starts with a stored recipe. A new case size also involves the erecting and sealing steps, so TRF defines the case format range and the changeover plan before the design is confirmed.

What Is the Difference Between a Case Packer and a Case Erector?

A case erector forms a flat corrugated blank into an open case; a case packer loads products into that case. TRFOnepack does both and seals the case as well, in one machine. TRF does not sell stand-alone case erectors: case forming exists inside TRFOnepack and inside end-of-line integration projects that TRF engineers around its case packing platforms.

Can Case Packing Equipment Integrate With My Existing Palletizer and Conveyors?

Yes. Because TRFOnepack seals the case, what leaves the machine is a finished shipping unit ready for conveying. TRF engineers the interfaces to conveyors and palletizers already in your plant, and TRFIntraflow covers the integration layer with sensors, PLC and HMI, machine communication and palletizer interfaces. Existing equipment and controls are reviewed with your team before the scope is defined.

Who Supplies Case Packing Equipment for Food and CPG Manufacturers in the US?

TRF supplies top-load case packing equipment to food and CPG manufacturers across the United States: TRFOnepack for integrated erecting, packing and sealing, TRFRapidpack for high-mix robotic packing and TRFDroppack for gravity packing of flexible packages. TRF also supplies the conveying and palletizing around them, so one partner answers for the whole end of line.
