What is a pillow bag case packer?

A pillow bag case packer is secondary packaging equipment that receives filled and sealed pillow bags from the bagger and loads them into an open case in a set count and pattern. The bags enter from above, and the full case moves on to sealing, conveying and palletizing. All TRF case packers work this way; the top load case packer page explains the format across the family.

Snack bags, frozen vegetable bags, candy bags and many other flexible packages share the pillow shape: a tube of film sealed at the top and bottom, with a seal along the back. The shape is simple, but the bag is not:

  • Headspace. Many pillow bags carry air or gas around the product, which cushions contents but makes the bag springy.
  • Fragile contents. Chips, crackers and similar products break if the bag is squeezed, dropped too far or compressed in the case.
  • Shifting contents. Frozen pieces, grains or small items settle to one end, changing the bag's shape and balance.
  • Seal fins and film. Seals add stiff edges, and printed films can be slippery on conveyors and under a pick tool.

That is why automated pillow bag case packing is validated with real bags, not only with bag dimensions. Pillow bag lines are common in bakery and snacks, confectionery and frozen food and ice cream plants.

How are pillow bags packed into cases?

Pillow bags are packed in a pattern that sets how many bags fit in each case and how they sit. Because the bags compress and rebound, the pattern and the case dimensions are defined together.

  • On edge, in rows. Bags stand on their side or bottom seal across the case, common for snack bags.
  • Flat, in layers. Bags lie face down in one or more layers, common for frozen and heavier bags.
  • Alternating or interlocked. Bags rotate from row to row so seal fins and filled ends nest, which can improve case fill.

Robotic placement builds these patterns bag by bag and switches between them by recipe. Gravity loading builds the pattern through collation before the drop, which suits patterns that repeat case after case.

Headspace sets the case size. A case that is too tight compresses the bags when the flaps close and stresses the seals; one that is too loose lets bags move and rub in transport. TRF validates the pattern with real bags at the fill and headspace your bagger produces, so the case closes cleanly and the load stays stable.

How does a pillow bag case packer protect fragile contents?

A pillow bag case packer protects fragile contents by limiting squeeze, impact and compression at every step. TRFRapidpack is designed for controlled handling of delicate, flexible or irregularly shaped products, and TRFDroppack uses a controlled drop, not a free fall, for bags that tolerate gravity loading.

In robotic loading, the end-of-arm tool is selected for the bag: film, headspace, weight and pattern decide how it is held, and TRF validates the tool with your bags during project engineering. In gravity loading, collation, drop path and drop height are engineered to bring bags into the case in pattern while protecting seals and contents.

During validation, TRF checks breakage after loading and case closing, seal condition, bag position and label visibility, and pattern stability when the case is sealed and conveyed.

Which pillow bag case packer fits your line setup and speed?

Choose by line setup and speed. The three TRF case packing platforms form one family, and because all three pack pillow bags, the right model is defined by your SKU mix, the products per minute you run and whether the case should be erected and sealed in the same machine.

Application ranges for commercial screening, not machine speeds. TRF engineering validates final capacity for each application (bag, end-of-arm tool or drop path, pack pattern and case).

TRFRapidpack fits mid-sized and large food and CPG manufacturers that run many flavors or sizes, change patterns often or need controlled handling of fragile contents. Changeovers are recipe-based, and the platform can grow from one robotic module to several.

TRFOnepack fits small and mid-sized manufacturers that pack by hand or semi-automatically and want one machine for the whole case. It erects, loads and seals the case in one machine, with fewer equipment interfaces and a smaller footprint than three separate machines.

TRFDroppack fits lines that run the same pillow bag format for long periods, where the bag tolerates gravity loading and the line needs medium to low throughput without robotic complexity. Reduced mechanical complexity supports consistent operation, and vertical product movement can keep the layout compact.

Each platform can run standalone or as part of a complete TRF end-of-line system. Sealed cases travel on TRFIntraflow to a TRF palletizer, with TRFSubzero for palletizing in frozen areas below 32°F. The case packing page compares the three platforms side by side.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Does a Pillow Bag Case Packer Need Vision-Guided Robotics?

Only when bag positions vary. TRFRapidpack picks with vision-guided delta robots when bags leave the bagger in a random flow, and with indexed picking when they arrive in a controlled position. TRFDroppack collates bags before the drop and does not use robotic picking. TRF defines the approach in the application review, based on your infeed, SKU count and pattern.

What Is the Difference Between a Drop Packer and a Robotic Case Packer?

A robotic case packer places each bag or group of bags in a precise position, which suits high-mix lines and fragile contents. A drop packer collates bags and loads them by controlled gravity, which suits repetitive formats with less mechanical complexity and no robotic tooling to maintain. TRF offers TRFRapidpack as the robotic option and TRFDroppack as the drop packer.

What Is the Difference Between an Integrated Case Packer and Separate Erecting, Packing and Sealing Machines?

An integrated case packer erects the case, loads it and seals it in one coordinated machine, with one controls platform. Separate machines need their own interfaces, controls and floor space. TRFOnepack is the integrated option, for applications of about 20 to 50 products per minute, and it can start standalone and later join a complete TRF end-of-line system.

Can a Case Packer Handle Frozen or IQF Products?

Yes, within the validated application. TRFDroppack packs IQF product bags and frozen food bags, and frozen food is one of the focus industries for TRFRapidpack. TRF validates the bag, the pattern and the operating conditions with real product before closing the design, and the same project can extend to palletizing below 32°F with TRFSubzero.

How Fast Can a Case Packer Change Over Between SKUs?

It depends on how different the bags, patterns and cases are. TRFRapidpack changes products and patterns by recipe, with reduced mechanical adjustments, and TRFOnepack runs multiple recipes through robotic loading. TRFDroppack is designed for standardized patterns. During engineering, TRF defines which changes are made in software and which, if any, require a format part.

Can Case Packing Equipment Integrate With My Existing Palletizer and Conveyors?

Yes. Each TRF case packing platform can run standalone or integrate with conveyors, case handling, palletizing and intralogistics as one end-of-line system. TRFIntraflow carries sealed cases to a TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed palletizer, and interfaces with your bagger and existing equipment are defined during project engineering. See end-of-line integration.

What Is the Typical ROI of Switching From Manual to Robotic Case Packing?

There is no typical figure, because payback depends on your plant. The estimate compares the installed cost of the system with the annual savings: people packing bags by hand per shift, shifts per day, local labor cost, the extra output the bagger gains when packing stops limiting it, broken product avoided and time saved at changeovers. A TRF Sales Engineer builds it with your data.
