What is a robotic case packer, and how is it different from gravity packing?

A robotic case packer uses robots to pick packaged products from a conveyor, arrange them in a pack pattern and place them into shipping cases, replacing repetitive manual picking, grouping and case loading. It sits between primary packaging, where the product is bagged, wrapped, sealed in a tray or filled into a cup, and the end of the line, where cases are closed, conveyed and palletized.

A case packing robot changes what it picks and how it places it through recipes, so a line that runs several SKUs, formats or patterns in the same week does not need a machine rebuilt around each one. TRFRapidpack uses delta robots, parallel-arm robots built for fast, light pick-and-place work from above, which suits top-load case packing.

A gravity drop packer works differently: it collates products above the case and releases them in a controlled drop, with no robot placing each item. Gravity suits repetitive flexible packages in standard patterns. Robotic packing suits lines where products, orientations and patterns vary, which is the job TRFRapidpack was designed for.

Which applications fit robotic case packing?

Robotic case packing fits medium- to high-speed lines with repetitive picking and top-load case packing, where labor dependency, SKU complexity, product orientation or throughput limit production. TRFRapidpack packs these formats:

  • Pouches and pillow bags
  • Flow packs and thermoformed packages
  • Trays and cartons
  • Cups and tubs, plus small packaged products

The platform is built for mid-sized and large food and CPG manufacturers in food processing and prepared foods, frozen food, protein processing, bakery and snacks, confectionery, dairy and consumer packaged goods.

When one line runs several of these formats, each product gets its own recipe for pick logic and pack pattern, and operators switch recipes when the SKU changes instead of rebuilding the process.

Is your line ready for a robotic case packer?

Your line is a strong candidate for TRFRapidpack when most of these statements are true:

  • Products are packed by hand at rates inside the TRFRapidpack application range.
  • The line runs several SKUs, pack patterns or package formats.
  • Products arrive in variable positions or must be oriented in the case.
  • Packages are delicate, flexible or irregularly shaped.
  • Operators spend each shift on repetitive picking, grouping and case loading.
  • You expect volumes or SKU count to grow.

Before quoting, TRF validates five factors with your line data: the product (format, weight and how it behaves when picked), the end-of-arm tool, the pack pattern and case format, the picking strategy and robot count, and the changeover plan for each SKU. When case forming, case closing, conveying or palletizing are part of the project, they are scoped at the same time.

How do you choose between TRFRapidpack, TRFOnepack and TRFDroppack?

Choose by product behavior, SKU mix, rate and how much of the case process belongs in one machine. The three TRF case packing platforms form one family, but they are not small, medium and large versions of the same machine: line setup and speed define the model.

Platform Application range Packing principle Typical fit
TRFRapidpack About 100 to 200 products per minute Delta robots, vision-guided or indexed picking High-mix lines, multiple SKUs, variable product flow
TRFOnepack About 20 to 50 products per minute Case erecting, robotic packing and case sealing in one machine Small and mid-sized plants consolidating secondary packaging
TRFDroppack About 20 to 500 products per minute Controlled gravity loading Repetitive flexible packages and standardized patterns

The ranges overlap, so speed alone rarely decides. If the pattern changes often or the product must be oriented, robotic packing fits. If the package is flexible and the pattern repeats, gravity is the simpler answer. The top-load case packer guide and the case packing page compare the family in more detail.

Every platform runs standalone or as part of a complete TRF end of line: TRFIntraflow conveys and routes the cases, and TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed build the pallet. Projects that span several lines are scoped as end-of-line integration.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How Many Products per Minute Can a Robotic Case Packer Run?

TRFRapidpack is positioned for applications of about 100 to 200 products per minute. That figure is an application range, not a nominal machine speed. The rate a specific line reaches depends on product weight and shape, pack pattern, case size and robot count, and TRF engineering confirms it for each application.

Can a Robotic Case Packer Handle Pouches, Trays and Thermoformed Packages in the Same Line?

Yes, within the validated application envelope. TRFRapidpack handles multiple products, SKUs, pack patterns and packaging formats, including pouches, trays and thermoformed packages, with recipe-based product and pattern changes. Some formats need a different end-of-arm tool, so TRF validates every product in your mix before confirming the design. See the pouch case packer and tray packer pages.

Does a Case Packer Need Vision-Guided Robotics for High-Mix Production?

Not always. Vision-guided picking is needed when products arrive in random positions or orientations; when they arrive spaced and oriented, indexed picking is simpler and runs on a fixed cycle. TRFRapidpack uses delta robots with either strategy, and high SKU variability or variable product positioning are the usual triggers for vision. TRF reviews your product flow before recommending one.

How Fast Can a Case Packer Change Over Between SKUs?

It depends on how different the two SKUs are. On TRFRapidpack, product and pattern changes are recipe-based with reduced mechanical adjustments, so a change within the same format starts with selecting a stored recipe. A change that needs another end-of-arm tool or case size takes longer. TRF maps these changes before the design is confirmed, so the changeover plan is part of the project.

How Does a Case Packer Protect Delicate or Irregularly Shaped Products?

A robotic case packer protects products through controlled handling. TRFRapidpack picks each package with an end-of-arm tool selected for that product and places it into the case from above, instead of pushing or dropping it. Controlled handling of delicate, flexible or irregularly shaped products is one of the design drivers of the platform, and it keeps the pack pattern consistent case after case.

What Is the Typical ROI of Switching From Manual to Robotic Case Packing?

Return depends on the labor hours replaced, shifts per day, local labor cost, throughput gained and the installed cost of the system. TRFRapidpack combines labor savings, higher throughput and flexible production in one scalable platform. Because each system is configured for your products, robot count and integration scope, the cost and the payback estimate come from a scoped proposal built on your line data.

What Is the Difference Between a Case Packer and a Case Erector?

A case erector forms a flat corrugated blank into an open case; a case packer loads products into that case. TRF offers case erecting only inside integrated solutions: in TRFOnepack, which erects, packs and seals in one machine, or within an end-of-line project around TRFRapidpack. TRF does not sell stand-alone case erectors.

Can Case Packing Equipment Integrate With My Existing Palletizer and Conveyors?

Yes. TRF engineers the interfaces between the case packer and the equipment around it, including conveyors and palletizers already installed in your plant. TRFIntraflow covers the integration layer with sensors, PLC and HMI, barcode and vision interfaces, machine communication and palletizer interfaces. Your existing equipment and controls are reviewed with your team before the scope is defined.
