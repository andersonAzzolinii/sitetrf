## What Is a Tray Packer?

A tray packer, in secondary packaging, is the machine that takes filled trays from the packaging line and loads them into shipping cases so they can be sealed, palletized and shipped. It does the job operators do by hand today: collect trays as they leave the sealer or wrapper, count them, orient them and lower them into an open case without disturbing the product.

In the US market, "tray packer" also names machines that build a corrugated tray from a flat blank and place product inside it. That is tray forming, a different category outside what TRF supplies in the United States. On this page, a tray packer loads finished trays into shipping cases.

Trays reach the case packer in many forms: trays with a sealed film lid, overwrapped trays and rigid trays for bakery items or ready meals, some with a shelf-ready label on top. Each is a separate application, validated with your real trays before a platform and tooling are confirmed. Because TRFRapidpack and TRFOnepack load from above, a tray is lowered into the case rather than pushed, which matters for film lids and delicate contents. The [top load case packer](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/top-load-case-packer/) page explains the format across the family.

Tray lines run in [bakery and snacks](https://trfautomation.com/industries/bakery-and-snacks/), [dairy and cheese](https://trfautomation.com/industries/dairy-and-cheese/), [protein processing](https://trfautomation.com/industries/protein-processing/) and [food processing](https://trfautomation.com/industries/food-processing/) plants, including prepared foods.

## Does a Tray Packer Need a Vision System?

Only when tray position varies. TRFRapidpack packs with vision-guided delta robots for variable product flow, or with indexed picking when trays arrive in a controlled position. The choice depends on how trays reach the packer and how many SKUs the line runs.

A tray sealer that discharges in a fixed index, single file or in neat rows presents trays the robot can pick by position alone. A line that merges lanes, rejects trays at an inspection station, runs an accumulation table or lets trays rotate on transfers presents a variable flow, and vision guidance removes the need to re-orient every tray mechanically. Before the review, document whether trays arrive single file or in lanes, whether they can rotate between sealer and packer, how many tray sizes run and whether any tray must be turned for label position.

## How Are Trays Packed Into Cases?

Trays are packed in a pattern that sets the count, orientation and number of layers in each case, defined per SKU and stored as a recipe. One tray packer can run different tray sizes and pack patterns within the validated application: TRFRapidpack changes products and patterns by recipe with reduced mechanical adjustments, and TRFOnepack runs multiple products, recipes and packing patterns through robotic loading.

Tray patterns raise their own questions. Trays are usually wider than they are tall, so cases often hold few layers rather than a deep stack. Some trays nest when stacked, while others need the case wall or a divider so they do not bear on the product below. Shelf-ready cases may require a specific tray face toward the opening, and retailers may set different counts for the same SKU.

Each tray size and pattern is validated during project engineering. Sharing planned tray formats early is what lets the machine absorb the next format without a rebuild.

**[TRFRapidpack](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/robotic-case-packer/)** is the high-mix tray packer. It suits tray lines where SKU complexity, tray orientation, labor dependency or throughput limit manual packing, handles trays with film lids or soft contents with controlled handling, and can start with one robotic module and add robots as volume grows.

**[TRFOnepack](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/integrated-case-packer/)** erects the case from a flat blank, loads trays robotically and seals the case in one coordinated machine, for small and mid-sized food and CPG manufacturers. Each platform can run standalone or as part of a complete TRF end-of-line system, with [TRFIntraflow](https://trfautomation.com/conveyor-systems/case-conveyor-intralogistics/) moving sealed cases to a TRF palletizer. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is the Difference Between a Tray Packer and a Tray Palletizer?

A tray packer loads trays into shipping cases at the case packing step. A [tray palletizer](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/tray-palletizer/) stacks loaded trays directly onto a pallet at the palletizing step, for plants that ship trays without an outer case. Some plants need both, and TRF can engineer the case packer, the conveying and the palletizer as one project with shared controls.

### Can a Tray Packer Handle Trays With Film Lids or Delicate Contents?

Yes, within the validated application. Top loading lowers each tray into the case instead of pushing it, and TRFRapidpack is designed for controlled handling of delicate, flexible or irregularly shaped products. Where the tool may contact the tray, and how fast it may move, are defined during the application review with your real trays produced under normal line conditions.

### What Is the Difference Between an Integrated Case Packer and Separate Erecting, Packing and Sealing Machines?

An integrated case packer erects the case, loads it and seals it in one coordinated machine, with one controls platform. Separate machines each need their own interfaces, controls and floor space. TRFOnepack is the integrated option, for applications of about 20 to 50 products per minute, and it can start standalone and later join a complete TRF end-of-line system.

### How Many Products per Minute Can a Robotic Case Packer Run?

TRFRapidpack is designed for applications of about 100 to 200 products per minute. TRF publishes application ranges, not machine speeds: actual capacity depends on the tray, the pick tool, the pack pattern and how trays leave the sealer, and TRF engineering validates it for each line before the design is closed.

### Can Case Packing Equipment Integrate With My Existing Palletizer and Conveyors?

Yes. Each TRF case packing platform can run standalone or integrate with conveyors, case handling, palletizing and intralogistics as one end-of-line system. The signals and buffers between your tray sealer and the case packer, and the handoff to a TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed palletizer, are defined during project engineering. See [end-of-line integration](https://trfautomation.com/conveyor-systems/end-of-line-integration/).

### What Is the Difference Between a Case Packer and a Case Erector?

A case erector forms an open case from a flat blank, and a case packer loads products into that case. TRF supplies case erecting only as part of an integrated solution: TRFOnepack erects, packs and seals in one machine, and end-of-line projects with TRFRapidpack include case forming and closing, so one partner answers for the flow from flat case to sealed case.

### What Is the Typical ROI of Switching From Manual to Robotic Case Packing?

There is no typical figure, because payback depends on your plant. The estimate compares the installed cost of the system with the annual savings: operators packing trays per shift, shifts per day, local labor cost, output the tray sealer gains when packing stops limiting it, trays damaged by handling and time saved at changeovers. A TRF Sales Engineer builds it with your data.
