## What Is a Custom Conveyor System, and How Is It Different From Buying Conveyor Sections?

A custom conveyor system is an engineered network of conveyors, transfers, accumulation zones, routing devices and controls that moves packaged goods between automated processes in one specific plant. "Custom" refers to the layout, the flow logic and the interfaces, not to one-off hardware: TRFIntraflow uses standard, repeatable modules, and the engineering goes into arranging and controlling them around your production flow.

This is case intralogistics: once a product is packed, the case has to be transported, accumulated, merged, diverted, sorted, elevated and delivered to a palletizer or a warehouse interface, at the rate of the lines. A packaging conveyor system bought section by section tends to leave gaps: cases pushed by hand at transfers, lines that stop when the palletizer pauses, and controls that do not talk to each other.

| Decision factor | Engineered system (TRFIntraflow) | Sections from several suppliers |
|---|---|---|
| Responsibility | One partner answers for the complete flow, from concept to startup | Each supplier answers for its own section |
| Flow logic | Accumulation, merges and routing designed together for the real line rates | Buffers and merges sized section by section |
| Controls | One control concept with sensors, PLC, HMI and machine communication | Several control concepts that must be made to work together |
| Machine interfaces | Defined in the same engineering as the conveyors | Defined separately by each supplier |
| Startup | One commissioning team for the conveyors and their interfaces | Several teams, coordinated by the plant |

A turnkey conveyor system does not mean giving up standard hardware. TRFIntraflow keeps repeatable engineering and consistent parts, and adds one partner responsible for the result.

TRFIntraflow fits plants where cases are bottlenecked or still moved by hand between automated processes. Typical applications:

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Does Zero-Pressure Accumulation Work on a Case Conveyor?

Zero-pressure accumulation divides the conveyor into zones controlled by sensors. When a downstream machine pauses, each case stops in its own zone instead of pushing against the case ahead, so cases are not compressed and restart in order. In accumulating conveyor systems built with TRFIntraflow, it is a standard flow management module, sized for the stops your line has to absorb.

### What Is the Difference Between a Merge, a Divert and a Sorting Conveyor?

A merge combines cases from two or more conveyors onto one path. A divert sends cases from the main path to another lane, such as a second palletizer or a reject lane. A sorting system uses product data, such as a barcode, to send each case to its assigned destination. TRFIntraflow includes merge, divert, sorting and reject station modules under one control system.
