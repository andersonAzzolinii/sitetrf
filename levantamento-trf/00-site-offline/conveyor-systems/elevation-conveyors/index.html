Conveyor Systems / Elevation

Elevation conveyors for multi-level case flow.

Incline, decline, elevator, lowerator and vertical transfer modules that carry cases between levels inside one engineered line.

Elevation conveyors move cases, cartons, trays and totes between floors and levels of a plant, using incline and decline conveyors, elevators, lowerators and vertical transfers. TRF Industrial Automation engineers them as modules of the TRFIntraflow platform, integrated into the customer's line and controlled together with the machines around them.

What are elevation conveyors, and when does a line need them?

Elevation conveyors are the part of a conveyor system that changes the height of the product path, so cases can climb to a higher level, descend to a lower one or cross a space a flat conveyor cannot use. In TRFIntraflow, the elevation family has three standard modules: incline and decline conveyors, elevator and lowerator, and vertical transfer.

Plants usually need elevation because the building was laid out around other things first: the packaging hall is on a mezzanine and the palletizer sits at floor level, or a forklift aisle separates the end of the line from the pallet area. Without elevation, the alternative is people carrying cases, which consumes labor hours and puts repetitive lifting on your team.

A line is a good candidate for elevation modules when cases change level between machines, when floor space between packaging and palletizing is limited or blocked by aisles, utilities and existing equipment, or when cases are still carried by hand at a level change.

Which elevation module fits: Incline, elevator, lowerator or vertical transfer?

The right elevation module matches the unit, the height change, the footprint and the flow around it. TRF selects among them during layout engineering, not from a catalog.

Module What it does When it fits
Incline conveyor Carries units continuously upward along a sloped path The height change can be spread over a longer horizontal run
Decline conveyor Carries units continuously downward along a sloped path Cases come down from a mezzanine or raised packaging area to floor level
Elevator Lifts units to a higher level in a compact vertical footprint Little floor space is available for the height change
Lowerator Lowers units to a lower level in a compact vertical footprint Cases descend to a lower level and the horizontal run is short
Vertical transfer Moves units vertically between two conveyor levels at a defined point Two flows at different levels must exchange product at one location

Incline and Decline Conveyors

An incline or decline conveyor is a sloped section that moves cases continuously between two heights, with no stop at the level change. It is the simplest way to change level when the plant has enough horizontal distance. A sealed corrugated case, an overwrapped tray and a reusable tote behave differently on a slope, so the conveying surface is selected for the unit and the slope is confirmed against the case data. The section belongs to the same straight conveying architecture as the rest of the line.

Elevator and Lowerator

An elevator lifts cases to a higher level and a lowerator brings them down, both in a compact vertical footprint. They are the choice when floor space is the scarce resource, because they use the height of the building instead of a long horizontal run. The flow at an elevator is grouped rather than continuous, so the module is paced against the accumulation and flow control that feeds and follows it.

Vertical Transfer

A vertical transfer moves units between two conveyor levels at a defined point. It fits where two flows at different heights have to exchange product, and it usually connects with merge, divert and sorting modules so cases from different levels reach the right destination.

Where does an elevation conveyor go in the line?

An elevation conveyor goes wherever the product path has to change level, and its position matters as much as the module. The typical positions are these:

  • Right after the case packer. Cases leave a packing cell on a raised platform or mezzanine and descend to floor level on a decline or lowerator.
  • Over an aisle or around equipment. A path blocked at floor level by a forklift aisle, a utility corridor or an existing machine rises above it and comes back down on the other side, with an incline and a decline, or an elevator and a lowerator, as the pair.
  • Before the palletizer. When the palletizer sits at a different height than the line that feeds it, the last modules adjust the level, then meter and orient the cases for the pick position.
  • At the warehouse boundary. Where the plant hands product to a warehouse interface at a different height, elevation modules adjust the level and the controls exchange data with the warehouse system where the project requires it.

Around every level change, spacing is set before the module, curves and transfers re-orient cases after it, and a buffer absorbs pauses so the module keeps working when the palletizer stops for a pallet change. The elevation module does not run on its own timer: it knows when the zone ahead is full and when the zone behind has product waiting.

Can an elevation conveyor integrate with my palletizer and case packer?

Yes. Elevation modules connect to case packers and palletizers through the same system integration modules as the rest of TRFIntraflow: sensors, PLC and HMI, machine communication and palletizer interfaces. Each interface is defined during project engineering, so the machines and the conveyors agree on spacing, orientation, signals and what happens when one side stops.

  • Upstream, case packing. Cases can come from the TRFRapidpack robotic case packer, from the TRFOnepack integrated case packer, which erects, packs and seals in one machine, from the TRFDroppack drop packer, or from equipment already installed.
  • Downstream, palletizing. Cases are presented to the TRFCobot collaborative palletizer, the TRFCompact compact palletizer, the TRFSpeed high-speed palletizer or a custom palletizer. Which one fits depends on your line setup and speed.

Elevation touches these machines in three ways: spacing, because the level change must not disturb the palletizer cycle; timing, because the buffer around the module absorbs a pallet change so the case packer keeps running; and information, because barcode or vision identification stays attached to each case until the routing or palletizing decision. The Avon case study covers conveying, case handling and vertical transport.

Elevation for cases belongs to TRFIntraflow. For exposed food or hygienic zones, see TRFCleanflow; both platforms are compared on the conveyor systems page. Extended to case packing and palletizing, the same approach becomes end-of-line integration.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What Is the Difference Between an Incline Conveyor and an Elevator?

An incline conveyor carries cases continuously along a sloped path and needs enough horizontal distance to gain height. An elevator lifts cases in a compact vertical footprint and suits plants where floor space is the constraint. TRF selects between them during layout engineering, based on the unit, the height, the footprint and the rate of the line.

What Is a Lowerator Conveyor?

A lowerator is the module that brings cases down to a lower level in a compact vertical footprint, the counterpart of an elevator. It is typically used when cases leave a packing area on a raised platform or mezzanine and must reach floor level. In TRFIntraflow, it is paired with accumulation and metering so the flow stays paced after the level change.

Can Elevation Conveyors Keep Barcode or Vision Data Attached to Each Case?

Yes, where the project requires it. TRFIntraflow includes barcode and vision interfaces, and the controls keep the identification of each case as it moves through the modules, including a level change. That allows sorting, rejecting and routing decisions to be made after the elevation module, with the data each case must carry defined during project engineering.

Which Industries Use Elevation Conveyors?

Manufacturers that run packaging and end-of-line processes on more than one level, or in plants where floor space is limited. TRFIntraflow serves mid-sized and large manufacturers in food processing, beverage, CPG, protein, frozen food, pet food, personal care and industrial sectors.
