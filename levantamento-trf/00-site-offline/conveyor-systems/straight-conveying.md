## What Is Straight Conveying in a Case Handling Line?

Straight conveying is the family of standard modules that move product in a straight line from one point of the plant to the next. In TRFIntraflow it includes three module types: powered roller, belt conveyor and modular belt, each chosen for the case, carton, tray or tote it will carry.

It is easy to underestimate this family, because a straight run looks like the simplest part of a conveyor system. In practice it defines the character of the whole line. The surface under the case decides how the case behaves at a transfer, how it is held during accumulation and how it presents itself to a sensor or a palletizer. The wrong surface causes trouble that is hard to correct downstream.

Straight sections also join the other TRFIntraflow functions into one continuous path. [Curves and transfers](https://trfautomation.com/conveyor-systems/curves-and-transfers/) change direction. [Accumulation and flow control](https://trfautomation.com/conveyor-systems/accumulation-and-flow-control/) buffers and spaces the product. [Merge, divert and sorting](https://trfautomation.com/conveyor-systems/merge-divert-and-sorting/) decides where each case goes. [Elevation conveyors](https://trfautomation.com/conveyor-systems/elevation-conveyors/) move product between levels. Every run is part of a system whose layout, controls and interfaces are engineered together, never a component shipped alone.

## Which Straight Conveyor Fits Your Product: Powered Roller, Belt or Modular Belt?

The right straight conveyor depends on the unit being conveyed, the path it follows and the function of that segment, and a single line often uses more than one type. TRF selects the module for each segment and confirms the choice with you during engineering.

### Powered Roller Conveyor

A powered roller conveyor carries product on driven rollers and suits rigid units with a firm, flat bottom, such as corrugated cases, trays and totes. Because driven roller sections can be divided into independent zones, they are a natural base for zero-pressure accumulation, where each case waits in its own zone instead of pushing against the case ahead. Inside a TRFIntraflow line, a roller conveyor system suits case packer discharge, buffering runs and palletizer infeeds, where control over the position of each case matters.

### Belt Conveyor

A belt conveyor carries product on a continuous surface and suits packs that benefit from support across the full underside, including packs whose bottom is less rigid than a corrugated case. The continuous surface holds the unit in place through a long run or into an incline. Its start, stop and speed follow the flow logic of the whole line rather than reacting on their own.

### Modular Belt

A modular belt conveyor uses a belt built from interlocking modules, giving the line a surface that can follow the path the layout requires. TRF specifies modular belt where a run must adapt to direction changes or where the belt surface itself needs to match the packaged product being carried.

## Can a Conveyor System Integrate With My Palletizer and Case Packer?

Yes. Straight conveying connects a case packer to a palletizer through a defined interface at each end, so the two machines run at their own rates while the conveyor absorbs the difference. Both interfaces are engineered in the same project as the conveyors.

- **At the case packer.** Cases come from case packing platforms such as [TRFRapidpack, the robotic case packer](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/robotic-case-packer/), [TRFOnepack, which erects, packs and seals cases in one machine](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/integrated-case-packer/), and [TRFDroppack, the gravity case packer](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/drop-packer/), or from equipment already installed. The first straight section accepts the case at the discharge height, speed and orientation of that machine.
- **Along the run.** Straight sections carry cases past scanners, checkweighers and labelers at a steady spacing, form buffer runs with zoned powered roller sections and bring the output of several lines toward a merge.
- **At the palletizer.** The last sections present cases at the spacing and orientation the palletizer expects, whether it is the [TRFCobot collaborative palletizer](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/cobot-palletizer/), the [TRFCompact compact palletizing cell](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/compact-palletizer/), the [TRFSpeed high-speed palletizer](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/high-speed-palletizer/) or a [custom palletizer](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/custom-palletizer/). The right palletizer is defined by line setup and speed.

The controls make this work. Sensors detect each case where the line needs to know it is there, the PLC decides when each zone starts, stops and releases a case, the HMI gives operators a view of the line, and machine communication links the conveyors to case packers and palletizers. Barcode, vision and WMS interfaces are added where the project requires them. A straight section therefore behaves as part of the line, not as a stand-alone conveyor.

## Can a Conveyor System Be Customized to My Exact Plant Layout?

Yes. TRFIntraflow is custom engineered around the actual production flow, layout and operational requirements of each plant, using standard modules. Straight runs are laid out by working through these questions with your team:

1. **What exactly is on the conveyor?** Case, carton, tray or tote, with dimensions, weight range and bottom construction. The bottom of the unit is the first input to the choice between roller, belt and modular belt.
2. **How many SKUs and sizes share the line?** A line with many sizes needs modules and guides that adapt without adjustment by hand.
3. **What are the line rates?** Rates at the case packer and the palletizer define spacing, section speed and the buffer between them.
4. **How much space is available?** Bay width, ceiling height, columns, aisles and existing equipment set the path and whether elevation modules are needed.
5. **What has to happen along the run?** Scanning, weighing, labeling, inspection and rejection each add requirements to the section that serves them.

The answers become the basis of the layout and of the quote for the complete system.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can Roller, Belt and Modular Belt Conveyors Be Combined in One Line?

Yes. Many lines use more than one type, because each segment has a different job. TRF selects the module for each position and connects all of them to one control concept, so speed, start and stop follow the state of the whole line. The plant receives a single engineered system rather than a mix of independent sections from different suppliers.

### Can a Single Supplier Engineer, Build and Install My Entire Conveyor System?

Yes. With TRFIntraflow, engineering, manufacturing, controls, assembly, integration and commissioning are managed within one coordinated TRF delivery model, from concept to startup. The same partner can also supply the case packers and palletizers at both ends of the conveyors, so the interfaces between them are defined once, in one project.

### How Does Straight Conveying Reduce Manual Case Handling?

Straight conveying carries cases under control from the case packer to the palletizer, so people do not have to move, turn or clear cases by hand between machines. Sensors and controls keep spacing and speed matched to both ends of the line, which supports steady throughput and less repetitive lifting.
