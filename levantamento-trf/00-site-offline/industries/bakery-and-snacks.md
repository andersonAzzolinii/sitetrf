## How Does a Case Packer Protect Delicate or Irregularly Shaped Products?

A case packer protects delicate products by picking without crushing, orienting without tearing film and placing packages in a pattern that protects them in transit. Snack cakes, cookies, crackers and chips break easily, and air-filled pillow bags behave very differently from rigid cartons.

TRFRapidpack is designed for controlled handling of delicate, flexible or irregularly shaped products, with vision-guided robotic handling where products arrive in variable positions. Each product is validated with its real package before the end-of-arm tool and pack pattern are fixed.

Protection also depends on flow. Wrappers and baggers rarely deliver product at a steady rate: stops, restarts and rejects create gaps and surges. Accumulation, metering and gapping on TRFIntraflow conveyors between machines let each machine run at its own pace, so the case packer receives a steadier flow instead of catching up.

## How Fast Can a Case Packer Change Over Between SKUs?

When the change is recipe-only, it is a selection at the operator panel: the case packer and palletizer load the stored pick pattern, case count and pallet pattern for the new SKU. TRFRapidpack is designed for recipe-based product and pattern changes with reduced mechanical adjustments, and TRFCobot supports multiple recipes, pallet patterns and SKU changes.

Some changes still involve hardware. A new case size may need guide adjustments on the case conveyor, and a very different product may need another end-of-arm tool. During the application review, list every product, case and pallet combination you run or plan to run, and confirm which changes are recipe-only. A plant with many changeovers a week benefits from robotic handling; a plant that runs one product per shift may get more from a simpler gravity solution.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a Robotic Case Packer Handle Flow-Wrapped Bakery Products and Pillow Bags?

### How Many Products per Minute Can a Robotic Case Packer Run?

TRFRapidpack is designed for applications of about 100 to 200 products per minute on medium- to high-speed lines. It can expand from a single robotic module to multiple robots as production grows, so packing capacity can follow new SKUs and higher volumes. The final rate is validated for each product, pack pattern and case.

### How Do You Choose Between Robotic, Integrated and Gravity Case Packing for Snacks?

Choose by product behavior, SKU variety and rate: robotic case packing with TRFRapidpack fits many SKUs, variable positioning and delicate packs. Integrated case packing with TRFOnepack fits smaller lines that want to erect, fill and seal cases in one machine. Gravity case packing with TRFDroppack fits repetitive bagged snacks that tolerate a controlled drop. TRF confirms the fit during the application review.

### How Do You Choose Between TRFCobot, TRFCompact and TRFSpeed?

Choose by line speed, case weight and floor space: TRFCobot fits lower-speed bakery lines with many SKUs and little space at the end of the line. TRFCompact adds industrial duty cycle and speed in a compact cell. TRFSpeed fits high-volume bakeries and snack plants where several lines feed one palletizing area. TRF validates the choice with your real cases and pallet patterns.

### How Is Payback Estimated for Bakery Automation?

Payback depends on the labor hours the system replaces, shifts per day, local labor cost and the installed cost of the system. The estimate divides the system cost, including maintenance, by the annual net savings: the labor cost of manual palletizing minus the electricity the robot uses.
