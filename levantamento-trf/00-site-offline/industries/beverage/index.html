What do beverage plants convey and palletize?

Beverage plants convey and palletize sealed shipping cases, shrink-wrapped bundles, tray packs and boxes stacked on tall pallets. Each unit has its own palletizing page.

How does zero-pressure accumulation work on a case conveyor?

Zero-pressure accumulation holds units in separate zones so they wait without pushing against each other, then releases them at the rate the next machine wants. On a beverage line, it keeps a pallet change, a wrapper fault or a label roll change from backing up the line.

TRFIntraflow includes accumulation and zero-pressure accumulation modules, plus metering and gapping. Sizing the buffer is an engineering calculation made in the layout phase: how long the downstream machine can stop before the line backs up, at the line rate, sets the accumulation length. Adding buffer later is harder than designing it in, and TRFIntraflow is always engineered as a complete system, never sold as loose sections.

How do you select a beverage palletizer?

A beverage palletizer is selected on three factors: units per minute, unit weight and the pallet pattern the load needs to stay stable. Rate comes first, especially in plants that consolidate several filling lines into one palletizing area, which points to TRFSpeed. Weight then filters the shortlist, using the measured weight of every unit rather than an estimate.

Pallet stability is the factor most often left for later. Layer patterns, interlocking, slip sheets, load height and the handoff to the stretch wrapper decide whether the pallet arrives intact. Share the pallet specification your distribution centers require, including height and overhang limits, before the layout is fixed.

Frequently asked questions

What Is the Difference Between a Merge, a Divert and a Sorting Conveyor?

A merge combines units from two or more conveyors into one flow, for example several filling lines feeding one palletizing area. A divert sends selected units to another path, such as a reject station. A sorting conveyor separates units by SKU or destination, often after reading a barcode. TRFIntraflow includes all three as standard modules, engineered into one system.

What Is the Difference Between a Dedicated Palletizing Cell and a Centralized Multi-Line System?

A dedicated cell palletizes one line and is simpler to control and expand line by line. A centralized system receives units from several lines and builds their pallets in one area, saving floor space but needing merging, sorting and buffering logic. TRFSpeed scales from a dedicated cell to a centralized multi-line system, and TRFIntraflow provides the routing between them.

Can a Palletizer Be Integrated With My Existing Conveyor and Wrapping Line?

Yes. TRF defines the interfaces with the machines already installed, from the bundler or case packer discharge to the stretch wrapper, labeling and warehouse systems. TRFIntraflow carries the integration layer: sensors, PLC and HMI, barcode and vision interfaces, machine communication, palletizer interfaces and warehouse interfaces where applicable. Who owns each signal is agreed during project engineering.

How Do You Choose Between TRFCobot, TRFCompact and TRFSpeed?

Choose by line speed, unit weight and floor space: TRFSpeed fits high-rate beverage lines and several lines feeding one area, for units within its weight range. TRFCompact fits secondary lines and brownfield rooms where floor space is limited. TRFCobot fits smaller producers replacing manual stacking at lower rates. TRF validates the choice with the real unit weight and pallet pattern.

Can One Automation Partner Supply Palletizing, Case Packing and Conveyors for a Beverage Plant?

Yes. TRF supplies conveying and palletizing as one integrated end-of-line solution, with TRFIntraflow at the core and TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed building the pallet. One partner is responsible from layout to startup, and Parts and Service supports TRF-supplied equipment afterward.
