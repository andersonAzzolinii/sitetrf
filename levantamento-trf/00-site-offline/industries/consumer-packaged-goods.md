## End-of-Line Challenges in CPG Plants

A growing catalog, promotional packs and rising volumes reach the end of the line before they reach the budget for more people. Four challenges define the end of the line in consumer goods plants.

### SKU Proliferation and Frequent Changeovers

Consumer goods multiply: sizes, scents, flavors, club packs, value packs and retailer-exclusive configurations. Each one can bring its own case, count and pallet pattern, and every promotion or launch adds a changeover. When runs are short, changeover time is a capacity number, so recipes and tooling validated for the full product list matter more than peak speed.

### Labor Across Shifts

Manual case packing and palletizing are among the hardest positions to keep staffed, and turnover is expensive when every new operator has to learn several products. Automation reduces dependence on repetitive manual work and moves operators to supervision, quality checks and changeovers.

### Floor Space Shared Between Lines

Many CPG plants grew by adding lines to buildings laid out for something else. Compact cells, and machines that consolidate case erecting, packing and sealing, save floor space where every square foot is already assigned.

### Multi-Plant Rollout

Brand owners run several plants and lines at once. A standardized platform reduces engineering complexity and lead time compared with a fully custom cell, and a first successful installation becomes the template for the next plant.

## How Much Automation Does a CPG Plant Really Need?

Automate the repetitive work without over-automating the process. A plant with a stable portfolio does not always need the highest-rate machine; it needs the machine that changes formats quickly, handles the widest product list and keeps running on every shift.

When more than one platform could technically work, TRF recommends the one that matches your required throughput, production intensity and five-year growth path, not simply the lowest initial investment. For a brand owner, that five-year path is the product plan. A platform that can be extended when a launch succeeds, such as TRFRapidpack adding robots or TRFSpeed growing into a multi-line system, can spare you a second project.

Standardizing on one family of platforms also helps across plants: the same cell, the same routines and the same partner make training and maintenance easier from one site to the next. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who Supplies Case Packing Equipment for Food and CPG Manufacturers in the US?

TRF supplies three top-load case packing platforms for food and CPG manufacturers across the United States: TRFRapidpack for flexible robotic packing, TRFOnepack to erect, pack and seal in one machine, and TRFDroppack for controlled gravity loading of flexible packages. Each is engineered for the customer's application and can connect to TRF conveying and palletizing.

### How Many Products per Minute Can a Robotic Case Packer Run?

It depends on the product and the platform. TRFRapidpack is positioned for applications of about 100 to 200 products per minute, TRFOnepack for about 20 to 50 and TRFDroppack for about 20 to 500. These are application ranges for screening, not machine ratings: TRF confirms the rate with your products and pack patterns.

### How Fast Can a Case Packer Change Over Between SKUs?

It depends on how much of the change is software. On high-mix case packing lines, TRFRapidpack changes products and patterns through recipes with reduced mechanical adjustments, so SKUs that share tooling change from the operator screen. SKUs that need a different case size or tool add a physical step. The application review lists which SKUs share tooling, so you can plan real changeover time.

### Can Case Packing Equipment Integrate With My Existing Palletizer and Conveyors?

Yes. Each TRF case packer can run as a standalone machine or as part of a complete TRF end of line with case forming, case closing, conveyors and palletizing. Interfaces with a palletizer or conveyors already installed are defined during project engineering, so the new case packer exchanges the signals the rest of the line expects.

### Can a Robotic Palletizer Operate Below Freezing?

Yes, with a palletizer built for it. TRFSubzero palletizes in frozen areas below 32°F, for frozen food and ice cream. The Custom Foods case study documents robotic palletizing below freezing. TRF validates the layout and the equipment for each cold application before quoting.

### How Should a CPG Brand Owner Calculate Payback on Packaging Automation?

Start with the labor hours the equipment replaces, shifts per day, local labor cost and the installed cost of the system, including electricity and maintenance. Then calculate against the hours the line actually runs across the product plan, and count changeover time recovered separately.
