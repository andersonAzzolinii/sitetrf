A list price is a starting point, not a project total. Installation, line integration, the end-of-arm tool for your case, conveyors and options are quoted with your application, because two plants that order "a robotic palletizer" rarely need the same scope. Part of the investment also happens inside your own plant, such as electrical work, floor preparation and production time lost during installation.

What are you actually buying?

A robotic palletizing cell is a system, and each part of it carries cost. Use this list as the skeleton of your budget and as the checklist for every proposal you receive.

  • Robot and cell structure. Payload and reach set the robot class, which drives a large share of the investment.
  • End-of-arm tool. Vacuum, clamp, fork and multipick tools have different costs, and the tool is what makes the cell work with your product.
  • Infeed and conveying. Infeed, accumulation and metering deliver cases in the right orientation, spacing and rate.
  • Pallet handling. Empty pallet supply, slip sheets and discharge of full loads. Manual pallet handling lowers the investment and keeps an operator at the cell.
  • Safety concept. Guarding and sensing defined for the complete cell.
  • Controls and integration. PLC, HMI and interfaces with upstream packaging equipment, wrapping and labeling.
  • Recipes and pallet patterns. The number of SKUs and patterns affects engineering hours and changeovers.
  • Installation, startup and training. Installation, commissioning, production trials and training for operators and maintenance.

Two quotes that look far apart often differ in what each supplier assumed you would provide. Writing this list into your request for quotation keeps proposals comparable.

Which factors move the investment up or down?

Factor Effect on the investment
Line speed Faster and multi-line systems cost more than an entry-level cell
Case weight Heavier cases require a larger robot class and a stronger end-of-arm tool
Product type Cases, bags, pails, trays and bundles each need a different gripper and handling logic
Number of infeeds Each line added to a central palletizing cell adds conveying, controls and pattern logic
Pallet handling Automatic pallet dispensing, slip sheets and load discharge add scope and reduce operator work
SKU and pattern variety More recipes mean more engineering, validation and training
Operating environment Areas below 32°F, washdown areas and dusty areas add engineering requirements

Two factors are often underestimated. Palletizing in a freezer is a different engineering problem, which is why TRF offers TRFSubzero for areas below 32°F. And in a brownfield plant, getting the cell in, connected and commissioned without stopping production is a significant part of the work.

Size the system for the throughput you will actually run, including the growth you can defend, not for the maximum the plant might one day reach.

What is total cost of ownership for a palletizer?

Total cost of ownership is what the system costs to buy, install and run over its useful life. A capital request built only on the purchase order tends to be challenged by finance, while one built on total cost of ownership is easier to defend.

Category What it includes
Acquisition Equipment, end-of-arm tool, controls and options, from the quote or list price
Project and installation Electrical, air, civil work, rigging, removal of old equipment, internal engineering hours
Production impact Reduced output during installation and startup
Operation Electricity, compressed air, consumables such as slip sheets
Maintenance and spare parts Preventive routines, wear parts, recommended spares
Changeovers Time lost when switching SKUs and pallet patterns

Two items are easy to forget: production impact during installation and changeover time. Both are measurable in your own records.

What is the typical payback period for a robotic palletizer?

There is no typical figure that fits every plant: payback depends on the labor hours the palletizer replaces, shifts per day, local labor cost and the cost of the system. The estimate follows the method of the A3 (Association for Advancing Automation) robot ROI calculator: the system cost, including 20 years of maintenance at 5% per year, divided by the annual net savings, which is the labor cost of manual palletizing minus the electricity the robot uses.

Break-even = (system price + 20 years of maintenance at 5% per year) ÷ (manual labor per year minus the robot's electricity per year)

Build the estimate in four steps:

  1. Count the labor. People dedicated to palletizing per shift, hours per shift, number of shifts and days per week, including the overtime that palletizing generates today.
  2. Apply a labor rate. Use your own rate when you have it. The calculator fills an average production wage for your state from public U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics data, and you can replace it.
  3. Build the system cost. The system price plus maintenance at 5% per year over the 20-year life. Electricity (a 20 kW reference draw at your state's rate) is subtracted from the yearly labor savings.
  4. Divide. The result is the break-even in years. Multiply by 12 for months. The 20-year labor savings is the manual labor over 20 years minus the robotic system over the same period.

Run it twice: once with today's shift pattern and once with the pattern you expect as volume grows. The equipment is paid once, while the labor it replaces repeats on every shift.

The palletizing ROI calculator applies this method. Choose a platform to load its reference list price, or enter the investment you want to test, then add your state, people per shift, hours per shift, shifts per day and days per week. The result is an initial estimate that leaves out several costs, such as installation and integration, and a TRF Sales Engineer completes the calculation with the costs of your project.

What Does Payback Not Capture?

Payback is a screening tool, not the whole business case.

  • Ergonomics. Repetitive lifting, bending and twisting at the end of the line is the work automation takes off your team.
  • Labor availability. Some plants automate palletizing because the position is hard to fill and keep, not only because of the hourly rate.
  • Consistency. A robot builds each pallet to the stored pattern, which supports load stability and the customers who receive your pallets.
  • Operator reassignment. People move from repetitive lifting to supervision, quality checks and changeovers.

What should you ask a robotic palletizer supplier before buying?

Ask questions that make every supplier price the same scope.

  • Which platform do you recommend for our speed, case weight and product, and why?
  • How will you validate the end-of-arm tool with our actual product, including the hardest case?
  • How many recipes and pallet patterns are included, and what does adding more cost later?
  • Are infeed conveyors, pallet dispensing, slip sheets and discharge conveyors in the quote, or assumed to be ours?
  • Who owns the interfaces with our packaging equipment, labeling and wrapping?
  • What do installation, startup and training include, and how much production time should we plan to lose?
  • How do remote support, spare parts and field service work after startup?

A higher quote often includes work the lower one leaves to your team. For TRF-supplied equipment, Parts and Service explains how support and spare parts are organized.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What Is a Palletizer?

A palletizer is a machine that stacks cases, bags, trays or other packed units onto a pallet in a defined pattern at the end of a production line. A robotic palletizer does this with a robot arm and an end-of-arm tool, which makes it flexible across products, SKUs and pallet patterns. TRF Industrial Automation supplies collaborative, compact industrial and high-speed robotic palletizing platforms.

What Is Palletization?

Palletization is the process of arranging packed units into a stable load on a pallet so it can be wrapped, stored, moved by forklift and shipped. It covers the pattern of each layer, the number of layers, the use of slip sheets and how the finished load leaves the line. Robotic palletizing automates that process at the end of the line.

How Do I Choose a Palletizing System?

Choose by line speed, case weight and floor space first, then by product type, number of lines and operating environment. Those variables point to a platform, and the end-of-arm tool and pallet pattern are validated per application. Send your line data, and a TRF Sales Engineer confirms which platform fits and what the scope of the quote should include.

How Many Cases per Minute Can a Robotic Palletizer Handle?

It depends on the platform. TRFCobot covers about 1 to 10 cases per minute, TRFCompact about 5 to 15 and TRFSpeed about 10 to 35. These are typical ranges for commercial screening, and TRF confirms the final rate for your product, end-of-arm tool, pallet pattern and reach. Applications outside these ranges are engineered as a custom palletizer.

What Payload Does a Palletizing Robot Need for My Product?

The robot must carry the case weight plus the end-of-arm tool, and for multi-case picks, the combined weight of the cases. TRFCobot handles cases up to about 22 lb, TRFCompact up to about 44 lb and TRFSpeed up to about 66 lb. Payload also sets the robot class, which is one of the main cost factors of the cell.

What Is the Difference Between a Cobot Palletizer and a Traditional Robotic Palletizer?

A cobot palletizer uses a collaborative robot, designed for shared workspaces and moderate speeds, in a compact standardized cell with a lower entry cost. A traditional robotic palletizer uses an industrial robot that moves faster, carries heavier loads and runs a higher duty cycle. That is why the TRFCobot Dual has the lowest of the three TRF reference list prices.

What Maintenance Does a Robotic Palletizer Require to Avoid Downtime?

A robotic palletizer needs periodic inspection of the gripper, sensors and the robot itself, following the manufacturer's schedule, and that yearly cost belongs in your payback estimate. Keep recommended spare parts for wear items on hand. For TRF-supplied equipment, Parts and Service covers technical support, spare parts and the TRFCare preventive maintenance program.

Turn the cost question into a specification

The useful version of "how much does a robotic palletizer cost" is "what does my palletizing project include, and what does it return". Start from the list price of the platform that fits, build the scope list and run the payback estimate with your own shifts and labor cost. At that point the budget number stops being a guess and becomes a document your finance team can approve.
