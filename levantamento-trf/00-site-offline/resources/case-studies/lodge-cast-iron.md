## The Challenge: Packing Lines That Backed Up

Before automation, the packing lines were very labor intensive and easily backed up with the volume of product coming down the line, according to the customer. The company wanted a solution that made the job easier, put less stress on employees, worked more efficiently and reduced the workload of the people doing it.

Two problems sit inside that description, and they are common to any plant packing heavy product by hand:

- **Rate is capped by people.** A manual station has a sustainable pace, and when the process upstream runs faster, the line backs up.
- **The physical load accumulates.** Taping cases and lifting heavy boxes onto pallets by hand, shift after shift, is the work behind shoulder and lower back complaints.

## The Solution: Fewer Touches, From Packing Station to Pallet

The customer's design brief was clear: the employee should touch the product as few times as possible. That required automated palletizing along with automated packaging, and a partner able to provide a full end-of-line solution rather than one machine.

TRF delivered a tailor-made ergonomic manual case packing system together with a fully automatic robotic palletizing system. The operator places the product in the box at an ergonomic station, and from there the robot takes the case away. As the customer describes, the team no longer struggles with tape machines or lifts heavy boxes onto the pallet.

## What Changed on the Floor

The results are about people first. The customer reports less stress on employees and lines that can be operated with fewer people. It also reports fewer complaints of shoulder and lower back strain: once the product is in the box, the robot takes it away, and operators hold the weight for less time.

The customer also describes people who are not as worn out at the end of the day.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a Plant Automate Palletizing and Keep Manual Case Packing?

Yes, and it can be the right scope. An ergonomic manual packing station keeps the flexibility of hand packing, while the automatic palletizing cell removes the heaviest, most repetitive lifting. That is the combination installed at Lodge Cast Iron, where the robot takes each case away once the product is in the box.

### Can Case Packing Equipment Integrate With My Existing Palletizer and Conveyors?

Yes, when packing, conveying and palletizing are engineered together. Case conveying, accumulation and routing connect the packing area to the palletizing cell, and the controls coordinate the flow. When the whole path from packing to pallet is one scope, it falls under end-of-line integration, with one partner responsible for how the machines work together.

### Does End-of-Line Automation Reduce Headcount?

The customer says the lines can be operated with fewer people. The same case stresses the physical side: operators hold heavy cases for less time, and the most strenuous positions no longer depend on lifting. That makes automation a labor availability argument as much as a labor cost argument.
