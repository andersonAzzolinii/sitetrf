TRF Industrial Automation builds carton palletizers on TRFCobot, TRFCompact and TRFSpeed, or as a custom palletizer, and the right model is defined by your line setup and speed.

What is a carton palletizer?

A carton palletizer takes cartons from the end of a packaging line and builds them into a pallet load that can be wrapped, labeled and shipped without being touched by hand. The robot picks each carton, or a group of cartons, from the infeed conveyor and places it according to a recipe that defines the layer pattern, the orientation of every carton, the number of layers and the use of slip sheets.

Cartons behave differently from heavy shipping cases. They are often lighter, they arrive in larger numbers per minute, their surfaces carry retail graphics or lot codes, and paperboard loses strength quickly when corners are misaligned or the load overhangs the pallet edge. A carton palletizer is therefore engineered for gentle, precise placement as much as for rate, with the gripper, the pattern and the line connections designed together.

Carton lines are common in personal care and cosmetics, pharmaceutical, confectionery and dairy and cheese plants. The business case starts with labor and ergonomics: fewer people dedicated to repetitive pallet building, and consistent patterns on every shift.

Can a robot palletize several cartons at once?

Yes, when the application supports it. Light cartons often arrive faster than one robot move per carton allows, so engineering looks at two numbers: the carton rate from your line and the pick rate the robot needs. When the tool picks two or more cartons per cycle, the pick rate drops while the carton rate stays the same.

Multipick is part of the TRFSpeed scope, together with automatic pallet handling and slip sheets. A grouping or row-forming section in front of the robot prepares the cartons, and the pick count per cycle is set during validation with your production cartons, since it also changes the tool weight and the pattern.

How does a carton palletizer avoid crushing cartons?

It combines a gentle end-of-arm tool, a pattern suited to carton strength and good load practice. The robot sets cartons down rather than dropping them, keeps them inside the pallet edge and follows a pattern chosen for the carton and the load height.

The tool. The end-of-arm tool is selected for your carton type, size, weight and surface. Coatings, porous board and glossy print affect a vacuum grip, and side pressure can dent panels or scuff graphics, so the tool is tested with production cartons.

The pattern. Carton patterns trade compression strength against load stability:

Pattern Strength of the stack Stability in transit Typical use for cartons
Column Higher, walls aligned Lower without help from sheets and wrap Tall loads, lighter cartons, long storage
Interlocking Lower, walls offset Higher, layers tie together Shorter loads, stronger cartons, rough handling
Column with interlocked top layers or tier sheets Balanced Balanced Loads that need both strength and stability

Print and labels. Labels, barcodes and lot codes usually face outward so the load can be read without breaking it down, and the recipe controls the rotation of each carton. A slip sheet on the pallet deck protects cartons from gaps between boards, and wrap settings suited to cartons complete the protection.

How does a robotic palletizer handle carton changeovers?

A changeover becomes a recipe selection, plus any tool change the application requires. Each recipe stores the carton size, pattern, orientation, layer count and sheet plan, so operators do not have to rebuild a pattern from memory on every shift. Three questions shape the changeover strategy:

  1. How many carton sizes run on the line, and how different are they? A narrow range may run on one tool; a wide range may need an adjustable or interchangeable tool.
  2. How often does the SKU change? Frequent SKU and pattern changes are part of the TRFCobot and TRFCompact profiles.
  3. Do customers specify their own pallet patterns? Co-packers often build to customer rules, so the number of stored patterns grows over time.

How do you choose a carton palletizer?

Choose by line setup and speed: the carton rate the palletizer must sustain, how many cartoning lines feed it and how much floor space it can use. Carton weight, picks per cycle and pattern then confirm the choice.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can a Carton Palletizer Fit a Plant With Limited Floor Space?

Yes. TRFCobot has a small footprint for areas where a traditional robotic cell is hard to install, and TRFCompact brings an industrial robot into a compact, standardized cell for brownfield plants and secondary lines. The area for the robot, the infeed pick point, the pallet positions and operator access to full pallets is confirmed with your layout, together with the robot reach.

What Payload Does a Palletizing Robot Need for Cartons?

The robot must carry the carton plus the end-of-arm tool, and for multipick, the combined weight of all cartons in the pick. Published case weights are up to about 22 lb for TRFCobot, 44 lb for TRFCompact and 66 lb for TRFSpeed. TRF validates the final payload with your cartons, tool, pick count and pallet pattern.

Can a Carton Palletizer Be Integrated With My Cartoner, Conveyors and Wrapper?

Yes. The palletizer needs cartons closed, square and spaced, with glue set so flaps do not open under the tool. TRFIntraflow provides accumulation, metering, gapping, merging and routing between the cartoner and the robot, and end-of-line integration ties cartoning, palletizing, wrapping and labeling into one project with one partner.

Can a Robotic Palletizer Operate Below Freezing for Frozen Food Cartons?

Yes, with TRFSubzero, the TRF palletizer for areas below 32°F, built for frozen food, ice cream and other below-freezing areas. Rate, payload and end-of-arm tool for frozen cartons are validated per application, and the frozen food and ice cream page shows how palletizing fits a cold-chain end of line.

How Much Does a Carton Palletizer Cost?

Cost depends on the platform, carton rate, picks per cycle, end-of-arm tool, pallet and sheet handling and conveyor integration. As a reference, list prices are US$ 120,000 for the TRFCobot Dual, US$ 180,000 for the TRFCompact Dual and US$ 350,000 for the TRFSpeed 100, EX Works Lenexa, Kansas, installation not included. The robotic palletizer cost page explains each factor.

What Is the Typical Payback Period for a Robotic Palletizer?

Payback depends on the labor hours the palletizer replaces, shifts per day, local labor cost and the installed cost of the system. The palletizing ROI calculator applies this method to your location, your shifts and the people who palletize by hand today. On carton lines, also note the effects it does not measure directly, such as gentler handling and consistent patterns.

What Information Should I Prepare to Size a Carton Palletizer?

Bring your carton sizes and weights, board type, print and label locations, carton rate at peak, SKU list and changeover frequency, shifts per day, pallet size and target height, and a drawing of the end of the line. A TRF Sales Engineer uses this data to screen TRFCobot, TRFCompact, TRFSpeed or a custom palletizer and validate the fit.
