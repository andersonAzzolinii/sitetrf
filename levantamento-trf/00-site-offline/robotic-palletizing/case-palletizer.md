## What Is a Case Palletizer?

A case palletizer builds pallet loads from the sealed shipping cases that leave a packaging line. Cases arrive on a conveyor at the rate the case packer sets. The palletizer picks one case or a group of cases, places them according to the pattern recipe for that SKU, adds layer sheets where required, and releases the finished pallet for wrapping and shipping.

Two things make case palletizing more predictable than most palletized products: the case is rigid and dimensionally consistent, and one SKU usually means one case size and one pattern. That is also why TRF publishes its palletizing ranges in cases per minute and pounds per case. The business case is the same in every plant: fewer people dedicated to repetitive stacking, no more repeated lifting at the end of the line, and a palletizer that keeps pace with the packing line.

Case palletizing runs at the end of most packaged-goods lines, in [food processing](https://trfautomation.com/industries/food-processing/), [dairy and cheese](https://trfautomation.com/industries/dairy-and-cheese/), [bakery and snacks](https://trfautomation.com/industries/bakery-and-snacks/), [CPG](https://trfautomation.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/) and [co-packers](https://trfautomation.com/industries/co-packers/). Film-wrapped multipacks without a case go on the [bundle palletizer](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/bundle-palletizer/) page.

### Why the Case Packer Defines the Case Palletizer

A case palletizer is sized backward from the packing line, because the case packer decides how many cases reach the palletizer and how consistent they are.

- **Rate.** TRF case packing platforms are positioned by application range: [TRFRapidpack](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/robotic-case-packer/) for applications of about 100 to 200 products per minute, [TRFOnepack](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/integrated-case-packer/) for applications of about 20 to 50 products per minute and [TRFDroppack](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/drop-packer/) for applications of about 20 to 500 products per minute. Those are product rates, so the cases per minute at the palletizer depend on how many products go into each case.
- **Case quality.** A squarely erected, properly sealed case presents a flat top for the gripper. Loose flaps, excess tape or bulging cases change how the robot picks and how the layers sit.
- **Case identification.** When several SKUs run on one line, the palletizer needs to know which pattern applies, from line control or from a barcode or vision interface on the conveyor.

## How Fast Does a Case Palletizer Need to Be?

Size it for the peak rate of the case packer, not the shift average. A palletizer sized for the average stops the line whenever the packing line runs at its peak or catches up after a stop. Four numbers matter:

1. **Peak cases per minute**, the highest sustained rate the case packer produces.
2. **Cases per layer and layers per pallet**, since every pallet change takes time out of the cycle.
3. **Recovery rate** after a short upstream stop, when the line runs faster to catch up.
4. **Time that is not case picking**, such as pallet exchange, layer sheet replenishment and SKU changes.

Between the case packer and the palletizer, accumulation decides how often a palletizer stop becomes a line stop. Zero-pressure accumulation holds cases without pressure between them and releases them in a controlled flow, which gives the palletizer time to change a pallet. [TRFIntraflow](https://trfautomation.com/conveyor-systems/case-conveyor-intralogistics/) provides accumulation, metering and gapping modules, together with merges, diverts, sorting, reject stations and elevation.

## What Is the Difference Between a Dedicated Palletizing Cell and a Centralized Multi-Line System?

A dedicated cell palletizes the output of one line. A centralized system brings several packing lines into one palletizing area, which plants choose when several lines need palletizing but none of them justifies its own cell. TRFSpeed covers both, scaling from a dedicated palletizing point to a centralized system serving multiple lines.

A centralized system adds these elements:

- **Merging and routing.** Case conveyors from each line merge into the palletizing area, with diverts and sorting sending cases to the correct pallet position.
- **Case identification.** Barcode or vision interfaces identify each case so the system applies the right pattern and the right pallet.
- **Several pallet positions.** One cell can build more than one pallet at a time, within the layout and reach the project validates.
- **Multipick and pallet handling.** Picking more than one case per cycle, automatic pallet handling, slip sheets and pallet discharge keep the cell running with less manual work.

Because the architecture is modular, a plant can start with one line and add others as production grows.

## How Are Sealed Cases Gripped and Stacked?

The end-of-arm tool is selected for your case type, size, weight and surface. A sealed case with closed flaps offers a flat top that is often picked with vacuum, while heavy, tall or weak-top cases may need side clamping or support from below. Every option is validated with your actual cases, sealed the way your line seals them, because tape, glue and board grade all affect the grip.

Case pallet patterns are usually defined per SKU and often by the customer receiving the pallet. A robotic case palletizer stores each one as a recipe, so a retailer that wants a specific layer count or labels facing out does not require a mechanical change. The pattern chooses interlocking or column stacking for case strength and storage time, keeps cases inside the pallet edge and respects racking and trailer height. Slip sheets between layers add friction and spread load, and the palletizer hands a square load to stretch wrapping and pallet labeling.

### Can One Case Palletizer Handle Different Case Sizes and Pallet Patterns?

Yes, within the validated application. Each case size and pallet pattern is stored as a recipe, so the same palletizer can serve several SKUs, and the operator selects the pattern when the line changes over. The limits are the size range of the end-of-arm tool, the reach of the robot and how many pallet positions the layout allows.

### What Is Slip Sheet Palletizing and When Is It Needed?

A slip sheet is a sheet placed on the pallet deck or between layers. It adds friction between smooth case surfaces, spreads the load of the layers above and helps column patterns stay square. It is needed when the case surface, the pattern or the customer's rules call for it. On TRFSpeed, slip sheet handling is part of the integration scope.
