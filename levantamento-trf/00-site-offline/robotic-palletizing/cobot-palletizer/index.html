Why do manufacturers choose collaborative robot palletizing?

Manufacturers choose collaborative robot palletizing to take repetitive manual lifting off the end of the line at a lower entry cost than a full industrial cell. These are the six reasons plants buy TRFCobot.

Labor

TRFCobot reduces dependence on repetitive manual palletizing and lets you reassign operators to line supervision, quality checks or upstream changeovers.

Ergonomics

Manual palletizing means lifting, bending and twisting, case after case, shift after shift. A palletizing cobot takes that motion off your team.

Payback

TRFCobot is a lower-entry-cost path to robotic palletizing, particularly in one- to three-shift operations. Payback depends on your labor hours, shifts and installed cost.

Flexibility

Multiple recipes, pallet patterns and SKU changes are supported without the complexity of a large custom system, so the robot runs every day, including during short runs.

Simplicity

A standardized architecture and intuitive operation lower the barrier to a first automation project. Operators learn one cell and one routine, and maintenance supports one standardized platform.

Small Footprint

TRFCobot fits applications where a traditional robotic cell would be difficult to install: automate the repetitive work without over-automating the process.

What is a cobot palletizer, and how is it different from a traditional robotic palletizer?

A cobot palletizer is a palletizing cell built around a collaborative robot, an arm designed for shared workspaces and moderate speeds, that picks cases at the end of a production line and stacks them on a pallet in a programmed pattern. It replaces manual pallet building with a compact automated cell.

A traditional robotic palletizer uses an industrial robot. Industrial robots move faster, carry heavier loads and run higher duty cycles, usually as a dedicated cell with its own conveyors, pallet handling and controls. That architecture makes sense at higher rates or when several lines feed one palletizing point. For a lower-volume line, it can be more machine, more floor space and more project than the application needs.

Collaborative robot palletizing sits below that level on purpose: a smaller robot, a simpler layout and a lower entry cost. The trade-off is speed and payload, so a cobot palletizer is sized for lower rates and lighter cases.

Which applications fit cobot palletizing?

Cobot palletizing fits lines where manual palletizing still takes one or more operators and the rate stays at about 1 to 10 cases per minute. Five customer profiles fit TRFCobot:

  • Small and mid-sized food manufacturers that still palletize by hand. Many food processing plants run lower-speed lines where a full industrial cell is hard to justify, but lifting every case by hand is no longer sustainable.
  • Pharmaceutical, personal care and similar industries with lower-speed production and frequent product changes. See pharmaceutical packaging automation and personal care and cosmetics lines.
  • Co-packers and contract manufacturers where flexibility, SKU changes and short production runs are critical. See CPG and co-packers.
  • E-commerce and distribution operations with repetitive case handling. See palletizing for e-commerce and distribution.
  • Plants with limited floor space that prefer standardized, fast-to-deploy automation over a custom project.

Each recipe stores what the robot needs to know about one product: case dimensions, pick logic and pallet pattern. On a high-mix line, operators switch recipes when the SKU changes instead of rebuilding the process. The infeed, the pallet positions and the pallet exchange are laid out for your floor and the way your operators work today.

Is your line ready for a cobot palletizer?

Your line is a strong candidate for TRFCobot when most of these statements are true:

  • Manual palletizing runs at about 1 to 10 cases per minute.
  • Cases weigh up to about 22 lb, subject to end-of-arm tool and reach validation.
  • Floor space at the end of the line is limited.
  • The line has a high product mix or frequent SKU and pallet pattern changes.
  • One or more operators are dedicated to repetitive palletizing on each shift.
  • Your priority is simple operation, flexibility and a lower entry cost rather than maximum throughput.

Before quoting, TRF validates five factors with your line data: the product (case dimensions, weight, material and how it behaves when gripped), the end-of-arm tool, the pallet pattern, the reach (the robot must place the farthest case on the top layer of your tallest pallet) and the process (infeed rate, accumulation ahead of the cell and pallet exchange). Floor space is checked at the same time, including operator access to remove full pallets.

Frequently asked questions

How Much Does a Cobot Palletizer Cost?

The TRFCobot Dual has a reference list price of US$ 120,000, EX Works Lenexa, Kansas, installation not included. Installation, line integration, gripper and conveyor interface are quoted with your application, because line speed, case weight, pallet pattern and layout change the scope. Use the payback calculator on this page to estimate what manual palletizing costs you today.

How Many Cases per Minute Can a Robotic Palletizer Handle?

It depends on the platform. TRFCobot covers about 1 to 10 cases per minute, TRFCompact about 5 to 15 and TRFSpeed about 10 to 35. These are typical ranges for commercial screening: TRF confirms the final rate for your product, end-of-arm tool, pallet pattern and reach.

What Payload Does a Palletizing Robot Need for My Product?

The robot must carry the case weight plus the end-of-arm tool, and for multi-case picks, the combined weight of the cases. TRFCobot handles cases up to about 22 lb, TRFCompact up to about 44 lb and TRFSpeed up to about 66 lb. TRF validates the final payload with your product, gripper, pallet pattern and reach.

Can a Cobot Palletizer Handle Multiple SKUs and Pallet Patterns?

Yes. TRFCobot supports multiple recipes, pallet patterns and SKU changes without the complexity of a large custom system, which is why it fits co-packers, contract manufacturers and plants with short runs. Each recipe stores the case data and the pallet pattern for one product, and the end-of-arm tool and reach are validated for every case size in your mix.

What Floor Space and Reach Do I Need to Validate Before Buying a Cobot Palletizer?

Validate the area for the robot, the infeed pick point, one or more pallet positions and operator access to remove full pallets. Then confirm reach: the robot must place the farthest case on the top layer of your tallest pallet. Pallet height and case size change the answer, so TRF validates reach, end-of-arm tool and pallet pattern for each application.

What Is the Typical Payback Period for a Robotic Palletizer?

Payback depends on the labor hours the palletizer replaces, shifts per day, local labor cost and the installed cost of the system. The estimate follows the method of the A3 (Association for Advancing Automation) robot ROI calculator: the system cost, including 20 years of maintenance at 5% per year, divided by the annual net savings, which is the labor cost of manual palletizing minus the electricity the robot uses. The calculator on this page applies this method to your location, your shifts and the people who palletize by hand today, and returns an initial estimate that a TRF Sales Engineer completes with the costs of your project.

What Maintenance Does a Robotic Palletizer Require to Avoid Downtime?

A robotic palletizer needs periodic inspection of the gripper, sensors and the robot itself, following the manufacturer's schedule. For TRF-supplied equipment, Parts and Service covers technical support, spare parts kept in the United States and the TRFCare preventive maintenance program.
