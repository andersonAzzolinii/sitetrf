## When Do You Need a Custom Palletizer?

You need a custom palletizer when the application falls outside what a standard platform was designed to do. In practice, one of the four situations below is usually present, and often more than one at the same time.

### The Product or Load Is Outside the Standard Range

The standard platforms are screened by cases per minute and by the weight of each unit. Many products that are not regular shipping cases still fit a standard platform once the end-of-arm tool and the pallet pattern are validated for them. When the unit weight, size, shape or fragility goes beyond what a standard platform and its tooling can handle, the project moves to a custom palletizer.

The pages by palletized product, such as the [drum palletizer](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/drum-palletizer/) and [bag palletizer](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/bag-palletizer/) pages, explain how the platform is selected for each load.

### The Line Speed or the Number of Lines Goes Beyond One Configured Cell

TRFSpeed can scale from a dedicated palletizing cell to a centralized system that serves several production lines. When the combined flow, the number of infeed lines or the production logic goes beyond what a TRFSpeed configuration covers, the application is evaluated as a custom project, for example when each consolidated line must keep its own pallet pattern and product sequence.

### Pallet Handling Has to Happen Inside the System

Multipick, automatic pallet handling, slip sheets between layers, pallet discharge and interfaces with stretch wrapping, labeling or warehouse systems all change the cell. TRFSpeed already covers many of these functions. When their combination or sequence is specific to one plant, the pallet handling is engineered into a custom palletizing system instead of added around a standard cell.

### The Layout Does Not Fit a Standard Cell

Building columns, existing conveyors, forklift aisles, pallet staging areas and ceiling height all constrain where a palletizer can go. TRFCobot and TRFCompact were designed for tight footprints, and they solve many of these cases. When the only viable position requires a special frame, an unusual infeed direction or a split between picking and pallet building, the cell is laid out as a custom project.

## What Is a Custom Palletizing System, and How Is It Different From a Standard Platform?

A custom palletizing system is engineered as a project around one application: layout, robot selection, tooling, controls and pallet handling are defined for one plant. A standard platform starts from a known architecture, so the project concentrates on what is specific to your line. Standardization reduces engineering complexity and commercial uncertainty compared with a fully custom cell.

That is why the standard options are checked first, even when a buyer asks for a custom palletizer. Several requirements that sound custom are already covered by a standard platform:

- **Multiple recipes and pallet patterns.** The standard platforms run several SKU recipes and pallet patterns, so frequent product changes do not require a special machine.
- **Compact footprints.** TRFCobot fits lines where a traditional robotic cell is difficult to install, and TRFCompact brings an industrial robot into a compact, standardized cell for brownfield plants.
- **Customer-specific layouts at higher speed.** TRFSpeed is a configurable platform that uses modular engineering to reduce project risk while keeping the customization each production flow requires.

A custom palletizer is the right answer when the application still does not fit after these options are considered. You pay for engineering where the application needs it, and you keep standard components where they already work.

### End-of-Arm Tool and Pallet Pattern

In a custom project, the end-of-arm tool and the pallet pattern are engineered together with the layout, not added at the end. The tool has to pick the unit securely at the required rate, and the pattern has to produce a stable load for wrapping, forklift handling and shipping. Changeovers follow the same principle as the standard platforms: operators select a recipe, and the system changes the pattern and the related settings.

## What Information Do You Need to Evaluate a Custom Palletizing System?

You need product, line, pallet and plant data, because payload, cycle rate and reach are validated against the real application. The more complete the first data package, the faster engineering can tell you whether a standard platform or a custom project fits.

| Data group | What to include |
|---|---|
| Product | Dimensions and weight of each unit, packaging type (case, bag, tray, pail, drum, bundle), surface and rigidity, number of SKUs |
| Line | Throughput per line in cases or units per minute, number of lines, peak and average rates, shifts per day |
| Pallet | Pallet type and size, pallet patterns, maximum load height, slip sheet use, mixed or single-SKU pallets |
| Plant | Available floor space, layout drawing, ceiling height, forklift and pallet flow, operating temperature |
| Interfaces | Conveyors, case packers, stretch wrapping, labeling and warehouse systems the palletizer must connect to |
| Goals | Labor to be reassigned, ergonomic issues to remove, capacity targets and planned expansion |

If you do not have every item, start with what you have. Product weight, rate per line and a photo or sketch of the available area are usually enough for a first screening conversation with a Sales Engineer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a Custom Palletizer Serve More Than One Production Line?

Yes, when the application calls for it. Consolidating several lines into one palletizing area is a common reason to move beyond a single cell. TRF first checks whether a TRFSpeed configuration, which can grow from a dedicated cell to a centralized multi-line system, covers the flow. When it does not, the multi-line logic is engineered into a custom project.

### Can a Robotic Palletizer Operate Below Freezing (32°F)?

Yes, with a palletizer built for it. For palletizing areas below 32°F, TRF offers [TRFSubzero](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/subzero-palletizer/), built on the TRFSpeed architecture for frozen food, ice cream and meat processing in below-freezing areas. A custom project is considered only when the cold application also goes beyond the standard ranges in speed, load, pallet handling or layout.

### What Is EOAT and How Does It Affect Palletizer Payload?

EOAT stands for end-of-arm tool, the gripper at the end of the robot that picks each unit. The robot has to carry the unit weight plus the tool, and the combined weight when it picks more than one unit at a time. That is why the platform ranges are always subject to EOAT and reach validation with your real product.

### How Long Does It Take to Install and Commission a Robotic Palletizer?

It depends on the scope: the cell itself, conveyor changes, the pallet flow and how much production time the plant can release for the tie-in. For a custom palletizer, the schedule for engineering, delivery, installation and commissioning is confirmed in the quote, based on the validated scope.

### How Much Does a Custom Palletizer Cost?

A custom palletizer has no list price, because it is quoted from each project's data: robot and tooling, pallet handling, conveyors, controls, integration, installation and training. The [robotic palletizer cost guide](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/robotic-palletizer-cost/) explains each cost factor, and the [palletizing ROI calculator](https://trfautomation.com/tools/palletizing-roi-calculator/) estimates payback with your location, your shifts and the people who palletize by hand today.

### What Maintenance Does a Robotic Palletizer Require to Avoid Downtime?

A robotic palletizer needs periodic inspection of the gripper, sensors, conveyors and the robot itself, following the manufacturer's schedule, plus replacement of wear parts before they fail.
