What is a subzero palletizer, and how is it different from a standard robotic palletizer?

A subzero palletizer is a robotic palletizing system specified for areas below 32°F, so cases are stacked inside or next to the cold area without operators building pallets by hand. A standard robotic palletizer is specified for ambient conditions, and its design starts from the rate.

Below freezing, the application is defined by the environment as much as by the rate. The first questions are concrete: where the palletizer sits, what temperature the area holds during production, and how cases enter and leave the cold area.

Which applications fit subzero palletizing?

The rule of thumb is simple: if the palletizing area operates below 32°F, start with TRFSubzero. Three cold-chain applications fit it:

  • Frozen food production lines, including frozen prepared foods and other packaged food operations that palletize below 32°F. See frozen food and ice cream.
  • Ice cream manufacturing and its end-of-line palletizing.
  • Meat processing in below-freezing areas (below 32°F). See protein processing, where TRF also supplies sanitary conveyors and case conveying for the same end of line.

Custom Foods, in De Soto, Kansas, runs robotic palletizing below freezing. The Custom Foods case study shows the application and the equipment installed.

Is your line ready for a subzero palletizer?

Your line is a strong candidate for TRFSubzero when most of these statements are true:

  • The palletizing area operates below 32°F, or pallets are built inside or next to a freezer.
  • Operators build pallets by hand in the cold today.
  • Rate and case weight can be validated for your application with the end-of-arm tool.
  • You want fewer operator hours in the coldest part of the plant.
  • The palletizer has to connect with conveyors and pallet handling that cross the temperature boundary.

To size the application, a Sales Engineer needs the data that defines the cold:

  • Temperature: what the area holds during production and during a stop.
  • Location and boundary: where the palletizer sits relative to the cold area, and every point where cases or people cross between the cold area and a warmer one.
  • Product and rate: case sizes and weights, cases per minute per line and shifts per day.
  • Pallets: pallet size, the patterns your customers expect and the number of pallet positions at the cell.
  • People: who enters the cell today, how often and for which tasks, such as pallet changes and clearing a stop.
  • Layout: a drawing of the cold zone with pallet routes and access.

With these facts, TRF validates the product, end-of-arm tool, pallet pattern, reach and process, and confirms the construction, the layout and the scope. A rough drawing and an honest description of the cold area are enough to start.

How do you choose between TRFCobot, TRFCompact, TRFSpeed and TRFSubzero?

Start with the temperature of the palletizing area, then choose by line speed, case weight and the number of lines. The TRF palletizing platforms form one family.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can a Robotic Palletizer Operate Below Freezing (32°F)?

Yes, with a palletizer built for it. TRFSubzero is the TRF palletizer for applications operating below 32°F, built on the TRFSpeed architecture. It brings high throughput, industrial reliability, modularity and integration to frozen and cold production environments. The construction and the operating conditions are confirmed for each application during the technical review, together with the layout.

Can Palletizing Equipment Operate Below Freezing for Frozen Meat?

Meat processing in below-freezing areas (below 32°F) is one of the target applications of TRFSubzero. The palletizing area temperature and the position of the cell relative to the cold area are reviewed for each plant, and the configuration is defined from that review before the scope is confirmed. Conveyors that feed the cell can be supplied in the same project.

How Does a Freezer Palletizing System Work?

A freezer palletizing system takes cases from the packaging line, builds pallets with a robot in a stored pattern and sends finished pallets out of the cold area. The layout and the entries into the cell are defined for the temperature of your area, so operators spend less time in the cold.

Who Manufactures Robotic Palletizers for Frozen or Cold-Chain Production?

TRF offers TRFSubzero, a robotic palletizer for areas below 32°F. TRF designs, builds, installs and supports these systems for manufacturers in the United States, and can supply the conveyors and case packing that feed them as part of the same end-of-line project, with one point of contact from layout to startup.

Can a Palletizer Be Integrated With My Existing Conveyor and Wrapping Line?

Yes, when the interfaces are defined in the application study: case infeed rate and orientation, spacing, signals between machines and how full pallets leave the cold area toward wrapping or storage. In a cold-chain plant, the entry and exit points of the cold area are planned with the layout. See end-of-line integration for the full-line approach.

What Is the Typical Payback Period for a Robotic Palletizer?

Payback depends on the labor hours the palletizer replaces, shifts per day, local labor cost and the installed cost of the system. In a freezer, manual pallet building is demanding work to staff, and the labor it removes is part of the calculation. A TRF Sales Engineer can walk through the estimate with your location, your shifts and the people who palletize by hand today.

What Maintenance Does a Robotic Palletizer Require to Avoid Downtime?

A robotic palletizer needs periodic inspection of the robot, gripper, sensors and conveyors, following the manufacturer's schedule, plus replacement of wear parts before they fail. For TRF-supplied equipment, Parts and Service covers technical support, spare parts kept in the United States and the TRFCare preventive maintenance program.
