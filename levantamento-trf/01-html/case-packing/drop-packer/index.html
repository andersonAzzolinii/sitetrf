Why do manufacturers choose a drop packer?

Food manufacturers choose TRFDroppack when the package is flexible, the pattern repeats and simplicity matters more than robotic flexibility. These are the six reasons.

Simplicity

A gravity-based architecture avoids robotic handling when the application does not require it, so the machine stays simple to run.

Reliability

Reduced mechanical complexity supports consistent industrial operation.

Labor Reduction

The machine automates repetitive product grouping and manual case loading, so operators stop counting and loading by hand.

Small Footprint

Vertical product movement can provide a compact packaging architecture where floor space at the packing step is tight.

Low Maintenance

Fewer complex components and no robotic end-of-arm tooling to specify, change and service.

Efficiency

Continuous, controlled case loading combines labor savings and throughput improvement with a less complex automation architecture.

What is a drop packer, and how is it different from a robotic case packer?

A drop packer is a case packing machine that groups packaged products above an open case and releases them into it through a controlled drop, using gravity instead of a robot to place the product. It is also called a gravity case packer, and it suits flexible packages, such as bags and pouches, that settle into the case on their own.

A robotic case packer picks each product and places it in the case. That suits variable products, orientations and patterns, but it adds robots and end-of-arm tooling to specify and maintain. When a product tolerates a controlled drop and the pack pattern repeats every cycle, a flexible bag case packer built on gravity removes that complexity. When patterns change often or packages must be oriented, robotic packing is the better tool.

TRFDroppack brings that logic to lines where the product and the pack pattern do not require the complexity of robotic handling. Simple by design, it runs as a standalone case packer or as one part of a complete TRF end of line.

Which applications fit a drop packer?

Drop packing fits mid-sized and large food manufacturers running repetitive flexible packages, where simplicity and low operational complexity matter more than maximum robotic flexibility. TRFDroppack packs:

  • Pouches and pillow bags
  • VFFS packages and flexible bags, including IQF product bags and frozen food bags
  • Pet food bags, rice and grains, grated cheese bags and similar free-flowing packaged products

Typical plants are in food processing, frozen food and ice cream, dairy and cheese for grated cheese bags, and pet food. The bag case packer page goes deeper into VFFS, IQF, pet food, rice and grains and grated cheese bags.

Is your line ready for a drop packer?

Your line is a strong candidate for TRFDroppack when most of these statements are true:

  • The products are flexible packages, such as bags or pouches.
  • The pack pattern repeats from case to case.
  • Operators group and load bags or pouches into cases by hand today.
  • Packing rates fall inside the TRFDroppack application range.
  • Your team values fewer components to maintain over maximum flexibility.
  • Floor space at the packing step is limited.

Before quoting, TRF validates five factors with your line data: how the package behaves in the drop (stiffness, fill level and seal), the collation pattern, the count per case, the case format, and the flow from the bagger upstream to conveying and palletizing downstream.

How do you choose between TRFRapidpack, TRFOnepack and TRFDroppack?

Choose by how the package behaves, how often patterns change and how much complexity the plant wants to own. The three TRF case packing platforms form one family, and line setup and speed define the model.

Platform Application range Packing principle Typical fit
TRFRapidpack About 100 to 200 products per minute Delta robots, vision-guided or indexed picking High-mix lines, multiple SKUs, variable product flow
TRFOnepack About 20 to 50 products per minute Case erecting, robotic packing and case sealing in one machine Small and mid-sized plants consolidating secondary packaging
TRFDroppack About 20 to 500 products per minute Controlled gravity loading Repetitive flexible packages and standardized patterns

A practical test: list the SKUs the line will run and the pack pattern of each one. If the patterns repeat and the packages are flexible, gravity packing is the simpler answer. If many SKUs need different orientations, mixed formats or careful placement of rigid packages, a robotic cell earns its complexity.

Pouches and pillow bags are also packed by TRFRapidpack, and pillow bags by TRFOnepack, so for those formats the decision depends on line setup and speed rather than on the package alone. The case packing page and the top-load case packer guide compare the family in more detail.

Behind the packer, TRFIntraflow conveys and routes the cases, and TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed build the pallet. Projects that span several lines are scoped as end-of-line integration.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What Is the Throughput Range of a Gravity Case Packer?

TRFDroppack is positioned for applications of about 20 to 500 products per minute, on medium to low throughput lines. The range is an application range, not a nominal machine speed. The rate a given line reaches depends on the package, the collation pattern, the count per case and the equipment upstream and downstream, all validated by TRF engineering.

What Is a Vertical Case Packer and When Is It the Right Fit?

A vertical case packer moves the product downward into the case instead of placing it with a robot arm, which is how a gravity drop packer works. It fits when the package is flexible, the pack pattern repeats and the priority is efficiency with low mechanical complexity. TRFDroppack uses that vertical movement, which can also keep the packing station compact on a crowded floor.

Can a Case Packer Handle Frozen or IQF Products?

Yes. TRFDroppack packs IQF product bags and frozen food bags, along with other flexible bags and pouches. TRF validates how your bag behaves in the drop under your production conditions before confirming the design. For the palletizing step in cold areas, TRFSubzero palletizes below 32°F, in frozen food and refrigerated or frozen areas.

What Is the Difference Between a Case Packer and a Case Erector?

A case erector forms a flat corrugated blank into an open case; a case packer loads product into it. TRF does not sell stand-alone case erectors. TRFOnepack erects, packs and seals inside one machine.

Can Case Packing Equipment Integrate With My Existing Palletizer and Conveyors?

Yes. TRF engineers the interfaces between the packer and equipment already in your plant, and reviews existing equipment and controls before the scope is defined. TRFIntraflow brings sensors, PLC and HMI, machine communication and palletizer interfaces. The bag palletizer page covers the palletizing side of a bag line.

How Much Does a Drop Packer Cost?

The cost depends on the package, the collation pattern, the case format and the rate, plus the integration scope around the machine. Payback comes from labor savings and throughput improvement with a less complex automation architecture, and fewer complex components also mean less to service over the life of the machine. A TRF Sales Engineer quotes each application from your line data.

Who Supplies Case Packing Equipment for Food and CPG Manufacturers in the US?

TRF supplies top-load case packing equipment to food and CPG manufacturers across the United States: TRFDroppack for gravity packing of flexible packages, TRFRapidpack for high-mix robotic packing and TRFOnepack for integrated erecting, packing and sealing. TRF also supplies the conveying and palletizing around them, so one partner answers for the whole end of line, from the bagger discharge to the finished pallet.
