What is a thermoformed package case packer?

A thermoformed package case packer is the machine at the end of a thermoform-fill-seal line that collects finished, sealed packages and loads them into shipping cases, ready for case sealing, coding and palletizing. It takes over the work of operators who pick packs off the discharge conveyor, count them, turn them to the right orientation and set them into an open case by hand.

Thermoformed packages are made inside the packaging machine: a web is formed into cavities, the cavities are filled, a lidding film is sealed over them and the web is cut into individual packs. The result is a package with a rigid or semi-rigid body, a flat sealing flange and a lid that is usually the most sensitive surface of the pack. Sliced meats, cheese portions, snack kits and prepared meals are common examples.

Every TRF case packer loads the case from above, and TRFRapidpack and TRFOnepack place each pack into position instead of pushing it. The top load case packer page explains the format across the whole family. Thermoformed lines are common in protein processing, dairy and cheese and food processing plants, including prepared foods.

How does a case packer protect thermoformed packages?

A case packer protects thermoformed packages by controlling where the tool touches the pack, how fast the pack moves and how it enters the case. TRFRapidpack is designed for controlled handling of delicate, flexible or irregularly shaped products, and top loading sets each pack down in place.

  • Lidding film and seal area. The lid often carries the print the shopper sees. During the application review, TRF defines where the end-of-arm tool may contact the package and where it must not, so the pack reaches the store looking the way it left the thermoformer.
  • Geometry and flange. Cavity depth, flange width and rigidity change how a package can be held and how it sits in the case. Shallow packs of sliced product behave differently from deep-draw portions or ready meals.
  • Product movement. Loose contents can shift when a package accelerates and stops, so the motion profile, pick point and placement height are defined around the product, not only around the package.
  • Chilled surfaces. Moisture on chilled packs affects how any tool grips them, which is why TRF asks for samples taken under production conditions.

Does a thermoformed package case packer need vision-guided robotics?

Only when package position varies. TRFRapidpack packs with vision-guided delta robots for variable product flow, or with indexed picking when packages reach the pick area in a controlled position.

Thermoformers usually release packages in rows, one index at a time, across several lanes. If the discharge conveyor keeps those rows spaced and aligned, indexed picking is the simpler solution. If packages spread, rotate or bunch on the way to the packer, or if lanes merge, vision lets the robot find each pack and pick it where it actually is. Before the review, it helps to document how many lanes leave the thermoformer, the gap between packs at normal speed, what happens when the thermoformer stops or runs a partial index, and where rejected packs leave the line.

How are thermoformed packages packed into cases?

Thermoformed packages are packed in a pattern that sets the count, orientation and layers in each case, defined per SKU and stored as a recipe. Some packs stack lid to base in layers, others stand on edge, and some patterns alternate orientation so the packs nest efficiently. Retail customers often expect packs to face a specific way for display or scanning.

Most thermoformed lines run more than one pattern: the same package may ship in different counts to different customers, or several package sizes may run on the same thermoformer. TRFRapidpack changes products and patterns by recipe, with reduced mechanical adjustments, and TRFOnepack runs multiple products, recipes and packing patterns through robotic loading within the validated application.

Share planned package formats at the start. A case packer that handles today's SKUs but needs long mechanical changes for every new package will limit the plant later, so TRF validates current and planned packages, patterns and cases during project engineering.

Which thermoformed package case packer fits your line setup and speed?

Choose by line setup and speed. The three TRF case packing platforms form one family, and the right model is defined mainly by how packages leave the thermoformer, how many SKUs you run, whether the case must be erected and sealed in the same machine and the products per minute you run.

TRFRapidpack fits medium- to high-speed thermoformer lines where SKU complexity, product orientation, labor dependency or throughput make manual packing a constraint. It can start as a single robotic module and grow to several, so investment follows volume.

TRFOnepack fits lower-volume lines at small and mid-sized food and CPG manufacturers. Instead of a case erector, a packer and a sealer connected in series, the plant installs one machine with fewer equipment interfaces, one controls platform and a smaller footprint.

TRFDroppack packs flexible packages such as bags and pouches, not thermoformed packages.

Each platform can run standalone or as part of a complete TRF end-of-line system. TRFIntraflow moves sealed cases to a TRF palletizer, and upstream of the thermoformer, TRFCleanflow hygienic conveyors connect processing to packaging. The case packing page compares the three platforms side by side.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can a Robotic Case Packer Handle Pouches, Trays and Thermoformed Packages in the Same Line?

Yes, within the validated application. TRFRapidpack packs pouches, pillow bags, flow packs, thermoformed packages, trays, cartons, cups and tubs, and it changes products and patterns by recipe with reduced mechanical adjustments. Each package format, pick tool and pattern is validated during project engineering, so share every format the line will run before the system is specified.

What Is the Difference Between an Integrated Case Packer and Separate Erecting, Packing and Sealing Machines?

An integrated case packer erects the case, loads it and seals it in one coordinated machine, with one controls platform. Separate machines each need their own interfaces, controls and floor space. TRFOnepack is the integrated option, for applications of about 20 to 50 products per minute, and it can start standalone and later join a complete TRF end-of-line system.

What Floor Space Does an Integrated Case Packing Machine Require?

It depends on the case size, the pack pattern, the infeed and how operators reach the machine. What changes the answer most is consolidation: TRFOnepack combines case erecting, robotic packing and case sealing in one machine architecture, which takes less floor space than three machines in series. TRF places the machine in your floor plan, with infeed and discharge, during the application review.

How Many Products per Minute Can a Robotic Case Packer Run?

TRFRapidpack is designed for applications of about 100 to 200 products per minute. TRF publishes application ranges, not machine speeds: actual capacity depends on the package, the pick tool, the pack pattern and how packages leave the thermoformer, and TRF engineering validates it for each line before the design is closed.

Can Case Packing Equipment Integrate With My Existing Palletizer and Conveyors?

Yes. Each TRF case packing platform can run standalone or integrate with conveyors, case handling, palletizing and intralogistics as one end-of-line system. The signals and buffers between your thermoformer and the case packer, and the handoff to a TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed palletizer, are defined during project engineering. See end-of-line integration.

What Is the Difference Between a Case Packer and a Case Erector?

A case erector forms an open case from a flat blank, and a case packer loads products into that case. TRF supplies case erecting only as part of an integrated solution: TRFOnepack erects, packs and seals in one machine, and end-of-line projects with TRFRapidpack include case forming and closing, so one partner answers for the flow from flat case to sealed case.

What Is the Typical ROI of Switching From Manual to Robotic Case Packing?

There is no typical figure, because payback depends on your plant. The estimate compares the installed cost of the system with the annual savings: people packing cases on each shift, shifts per day, local labor cost, output the thermoformer gains when packing stops limiting it, packs damaged by handling and time saved at changeovers. A TRF Sales Engineer builds it with your data.
