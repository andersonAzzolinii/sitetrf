What is the difference between a case packer and a case erector?

A case packer loads products into the shipping case; a case erector forms the case from a flat blank. In a top-load case packer, the open case waits below the loading point while products enter from above, picked and placed by a robot or released by a controlled drop, and the case is then closed and sealed for palletizing.

At TRF, erecting is part of the packing system rather than a separate machine. TRFOnepack erects, packs and seals the case in one machine, and case forming is included in integrated end-of-line projects. TRF does not supply case erecting as a stand-alone machine.

How do you choose between TRFRapidpack, TRFOnepack and TRFDroppack?

Start with product behavior, then speed and line setup. The three platforms are not a ladder of sizes: each one solves a different packing problem.

Platform Packing principle Application range Best fit
TRFRapidpack Robotic pick and place, vision-guided or indexed For applications of about 100 to 200 products per minute High-mix lines, variable product positioning, delicate or irregular products
TRFOnepack Erect, robotic pack and seal in one machine For applications of about 20 to 50 products per minute Replacing manual or semi-automatic secondary packaging with one machine
TRFDroppack Controlled gravity loading For applications of about 20 to 500 products per minute Repetitive flexible packages, medium to low throughput without robotic complexity

Commercial screening criteria; final capacity is validated per application (product, pack pattern, tooling and case format).

TRFRapidpack handles pouches, pillow bags, flow packs, thermoformed packages, trays, cartons, cups and tubs, and can expand from one robotic module to several as production grows. TRFOnepack consolidates three processes, so a Dallas-area plant automates secondary packaging with fewer equipment interfaces and less floor space. TRFDroppack fits pillow bags, pouches, VFFS packages and frozen food bags packed in standardized patterns. All three appear side by side on the top-load case packer page.

Can case packing equipment integrate with your existing palletizer and conveyors?

Yes. Each TRF case packer can run as a stand-alone machine or integrate with conveyors, case handling and palletizing. Sealed cases are conveyed, accumulated and routed before they reach the palletizer.

TRFIntraflow supplies the conveying, accumulation and routing modules, and the robotic palletizing page compares the palletizers for the same line. What to prepare before requesting a technical consultation in Dallas

Bring the details that define the platform, the tooling and the integration scope:

  • Primary package: the product and package type entering the case packer, its dimensions and how it behaves when picked or dropped.
  • Rate: products per minute per line, and how many lines feed the case packer.
  • Pack pattern and case format: count per case, row or layer pattern, case dimensions and how cases are closed.
  • SKU mix and changeover: number of SKUs and how often they change.
  • Floor space: the area available.
  • Existing equipment: any conveyor, palletizer or warehouse system the case packer connects to.

Who Supplies Case Packing Equipment for Food and CPG Manufacturers in Texas?

TRF supplies case packing equipment to food and CPG manufacturers across the United States, including Texas: TRFRapidpack for flexible robotic packing, TRFOnepack for erecting, packing and sealing in one machine, and TRFDroppack for gravity packing. How Many Products per Minute Can a Robotic Case Packer Run?

TRFRapidpack is positioned for applications of about 100 to 200 products per minute, TRFOnepack for about 20 to 50 and TRFDroppack for about 20 to 500. These are application ranges used for commercial screening, not nominal machine speeds; final capacity is validated per application, considering the product, the pack pattern, the tooling and the case format.

What Is a Drop Packer and How Does Gravity Packing Work?

A drop packer groups packages above the case and releases them through a controlled drop instead of picking them with a robot. TRFDroppack uses this principle for repetitive flexible packages such as pillow bags, pouches, VFFS packages and IQF bags. Fewer complex components and no robotic end-of-arm tool keep operation and maintenance simple.

Can a Robotic Case Packer Handle Pouches, Trays and Thermoformed Packages in the Same Line?

Yes, within the validated application envelope. TRFRapidpack is built for high-mix production and handles pouches, pillow bags, flow packs, thermoformed packages, trays, cartons, cups and tubs with recipe-based changeovers. The limits are the tooling and the pack patterns, which TRF engineering validates against the complete SKU list before the machine is specified.

How Fast Can a Case Packer Change Over Between SKUs?

On a robotic case packer, a changeover is mostly a recipe change: TRFRapidpack switches product and pattern by recipe, with reduced mechanical adjustments. How fast it goes in your plant depends on whether the new product fits the same tooling and case format, which is why the full SKU list is part of the application review.

What Is the Typical ROI of Switching From Manual to Robotic Case Packing?

ROI comes from three places: labor no longer spent picking, grouping and loading cases by hand, a steady packing rate on every shift, and, with TRFOnepack, fewer machines and interfaces in one capital project. Each case packing project is quoted for its own scope, so the return is estimated with your shifts, labor cost and line data.
