What is a Custom conveyor system, and how is it different from buying conveyor sections?

A custom conveyor system is an engineered network of conveyors, transfers, accumulation zones, routing devices and controls that moves packaged goods between automated processes in one specific plant. "Custom" refers to the layout, the flow logic and the interfaces, not to one-off hardware: TRFIntraflow uses standard, repeatable modules, and the engineering goes into arranging and controlling them around your production flow.

This is case intralogistics: once a product is packed, the case has to be transported, accumulated, merged, diverted, sorted, elevated and delivered to a palletizer or a warehouse interface, at the rate of the lines. A packaging conveyor system bought section by section tends to leave gaps: cases pushed by hand at transfers, lines that stop when the palletizer pauses, and controls that do not talk to each other.

Which applications fit Custom conveyor systems?

TRFIntraflow fits plants where cases are bottlenecked or still moved by hand between automated processes. Typical applications:

  • Case packer to palletizer, with accumulation that decouples the two machines.
  • Multi-line consolidation, merging several packaging lines into one palletizing area or a centralized system such as TRFSpeed.
  • Line balancing, with metering and buffers that keep upstream equipment running during short downstream stops.
  • Case routing and sorting, sending each SKU to the right palletizing position or warehouse interface.
  • Reject handling, removing cases that fail code reading or inspection before palletizing.
  • Level changes, with inclines, elevators, lowerators and vertical transfers where floor space is limited.
  • Warehouse conveyor systems, exchanging data with the warehouse management system where the project requires it.

The platform serves mid-sized and large manufacturers in food processing, beverage, protein processing, frozen food, pet food, personal care, consumer packaged goods and industrial manufacturing, wherever several packaging or end-of-line processes need reliable case movement.

Is your line ready for a Custom conveyor system?

Your plant is a strong candidate for TRFIntraflow when most of these statements are true:

  • Operators push, lift or straighten cases between machines.
  • A short palletizer stop also stops the case packer upstream.
  • Several packaging lines need to reach one palletizing area.
  • Cases must be routed by SKU, code or destination.
  • Aisles, forklift paths or existing equipment block the shortest route.
  • Conveyors, case packers and palletizers run on controls that do not communicate.

Before quoting, TRF reviews five inputs with your team: the units conveyed (dimensions, weight range and bottom construction for each SKU), the line rates and number of lines, layout drawings with levels, columns, aisles and utilities, the points where the flow breaks today, and the case packers, palletizers, scanners and warehouse systems the conveyors must connect to.

How do you choose between TRFIntraflow and TRFCleanflow?

Choose by what is conveyed and where. Use TRFIntraflow when the item is a case, carton, tray, tote or other packaged unit moving between automation processes. Use TRFCleanflow when the item is exposed food or the conveyor sits in a hygienic or washdown zone, in its Dry or Washdown line.

Frequently asked questions

How Does Zero-Pressure Accumulation Work on a Case Conveyor?

Zero-pressure accumulation divides the conveyor into zones controlled by sensors. When a downstream machine pauses, each case stops in its own zone instead of pushing against the case ahead, so cases are not compressed and restart in order. In accumulating conveyor systems built with TRFIntraflow, it is a standard flow management module, sized for the stops your line has to absorb.

What Is the Difference Between a Merge, a Divert and a Sorting Conveyor?

A merge combines cases from two or more conveyors onto one path. A divert sends cases from the main path to another lane, such as a second palletizer or a reject lane. A sorting system uses product data, such as a barcode, to send each case to its assigned destination. TRFIntraflow includes merge, divert, sorting and reject station modules under one control system.

Can a Conveyor System Integrate With My Palletizer and Case Packer?

Yes. TRFIntraflow includes palletizer interfaces, machine communication, sensors, PLC and HMI as standard integration modules. TRF connects the conveyors to case packers such as TRFOnepack and TRFDroppack, to palletizers such as TRFCobot and TRFCompact, and to equipment already installed in your plant. Each interface is defined during project engineering.

Can a Single Supplier Engineer, Build and Install My Entire Conveyor System?

Yes. TRF takes ownership of TRFIntraflow projects from concept and engineering to manufacturing, controls, assembly, installation and commissioning, within one coordinated delivery model. One partner answers for the complete intralogistics solution, which reduces the integration risk of combining sections, controls and interfaces from several suppliers. The same team can also integrate the case packers and palletizers at both ends.

How Much Does a Custom Conveyor System Cost?

The cost depends on the number of lines served, line rates, case size and weight, the layout and floor space, the number of accumulation, routing and elevation points, and the interfaces with case packers, palletizers and warehouse systems. TRFIntraflow is always quoted as a complete engineered system.

Can Roller, Belt and Modular Belt Conveyors Be Combined in One System?

Yes. TRFIntraflow combines powered roller, belt and modular belt conveying with transfers, curves, turntables, accumulation, merges, diverts and elevation modules in one coordinated system. TRF selects each module for the unit conveyed, the path it follows and the function of that segment, then connects it with sensors, PLC, HMI and machine communication, so the whole line runs under one control logic.
