TRFCleanflow dry conveyor system: Sanitary conveying for dry zones.

Sanitary conveying for dry production zones, where cleanability matters but high-pressure washdown does not apply.

What is a dry conveyor system?

A dry conveyor system is a conveyor engineered for food production zones that are cleaned without water, using dry methods such as vacuuming, brushing and wipe-down, while still handling food or food-contact product. It is a sanitary conveyor, not a general-purpose one: the hygienic logic is the same as in a washdown line, but the cleaning method and the electrical protection are different.

The distinction matters because many food plants run both environments. A plant may wash down the area where raw product is processed and keep the area where dry or low-moisture product is handled free of water on purpose. Using a general industrial conveyor in the dry zone leaves cavities, ledges and fasteners that a crew cannot clean with a vacuum and a cloth.

TRFCleanflow Dry sits between those two mistakes. It is designed for dry and low-moisture food-processing environments where cleanability, product protection and reliable operation are required, applying the same platform philosophy as the washdown line to a zone with a different sanitation routine.

What is the difference between dry-clean and washdown conveyor lines?

A dry-clean line is cleaned with vacuum, brushing and wipe-down, and a washdown line is cleaned with water, chemicals, high pressure and high temperature. Choose the dry conveyor system for industrialized foods with moderate, infrequent cleaning, and the washdown line for animal protein and similar products sanitized daily. The deciding factor is the sanitation program of that specific area, not the plant as a whole.

What makes a conveyor easy to clean in a dry zone?

A conveyor is easy to dry-clean when a crew with a vacuum, a brush and a cloth can reach every surface that matters without dismantling it. These are the design targets of TRFCleanflow Dry:

  • 304 stainless-steel construction, for a surface that tolerates repeated cleaning.
  • Open-frame architecture, so the structure can be seen, reached and vacuumed from more than one side.
  • Reduced horizontal surfaces and contamination points, because in a dry zone flat ledges collect powder, crumbs and fines.
  • No unnecessary cavities or product accumulation points, which removes the places where product builds up out of sight.
  • Easy-access belt and components, so the belt underside and the area below it can be cleaned without dismantling the conveyor.
  • Food-grade belting, selected for the product and for the way the line will be cleaned.
  • Sealed stainless fasteners where appropriate, which keeps threads and gaps out of the product zone.
  • Food-grade lubricants where required, for points that need lubrication inside a food handling area.
  • Easy vacuum, brushing and wipe-down access, designed around the tools a dry-clean crew actually uses.

Dry cleaning has a different failure mode from wet cleaning. In a washdown zone, the risk is water and residue trapped where they cannot drain. In a dry zone, the risk is product that accumulates unnoticed, or a crew that reaches for a hose because dry methods cannot reach a hidden surface. Both come from construction that was never designed to be cleaned, and the features above address that directly.

What electrical protection does a dry conveyor need?

A dry conveyor needs electrical protection matched to a zone that is cleaned without hoses but still has to tolerate dust, wiping and incidental moisture. On TRFCleanflow Dry, the design target is IP65 electrical components as a baseline, with protected electrical and pneumatic routing.

Routing matters as much as the rating. Cables and pneumatic lines are run so they do not create ledges above the product zone, so they can be wiped or vacuumed like the rest of the conveyor, and so they stay protected from damage during production and cleaning.

When does an enclosed product path make sense?

An optional enclosed or covered product path makes sense when the product must be protected from the environment, or the environment from the product. It is a project decision, not a default.

The trade-off is access: any cover has to open or come off for cleaning and inspection. TRF engineering evaluates the covered path together with your sanitation procedure.

TRFCleanflow dry or TRFIntraflow?

Use TRFCleanflow when the conveyed item is exposed food, and TRFIntraflow when it is a case, carton, tray, tote or other packaged unit moving between automation processes. The conveyor systems page compares both platforms.

Both lines belong to the TRFCleanflow sanitary conveyor system. Once the product is packed, TRFIntraflow carries cases to case packing, such as TRFDroppack gravity case packing for repetitive flexible packages or TRFRapidpack robotic case packing for high-mix lines, and on to palletizing. When the whole flow is designed together, the project becomes end-of-line automation engineered as one system.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How Often Does a Dry Conveyor System Need to Be Cleaned?

The plant's sanitation program sets the routine. Dry zones for industrialized foods typically see moderate, infrequent cleaning by vacuum, brushing and wipe-down, with water kept out of the area. TRFCleanflow Dry is designed for dry and low-moisture environments where cleanability is required.

Which Food Plants Use a Dry Conveyor System?

Dry and low-moisture zones exist in many food plants, including the priority industries of TRFCleanflow: frozen food, dairy and cheese, ready-to-eat and food processing, and protein processing plants with dry packaging areas. Your sanitation team defines each zone, and TRF designs the conveyor to match it.

Can a Dry Conveyor System Connect to Packaging and Case Packing Equipment?

Yes. TRFCleanflow Dry is engineered to connect processing, inspection and primary packaging with the downstream automation, so start, stop and product flow are coordinated across the line. After primary packaging, TRFIntraflow carries the packed product to case packing and palletizing, and one partner defines every interface in the same project.
