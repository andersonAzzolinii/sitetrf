What does an industrial conveyor system do on a production line?

An industrial conveyor system does four jobs at once: transport, flow management, routing and integration with the machines at both ends. The difference between a line that flows and a line that stops is usually in the last three.

Transport carries product between machines, across levels and through work areas. Flow management accumulates product when a downstream machine pauses, meters it so a robot picks at a steady rhythm and creates gaps where a scanner or diverter needs them. Routing merges lines, diverts to a second lane, sorts by SKU or destination and rejects product that fails inspection, while integration links the conveyor to the machines through sensors, PLC and HMI, barcode and vision interfaces.

A conveyor that only transports leaves the line exposed. If a palletizer pauses for a pallet change and there is no accumulation, the case packer upstream has to stop. Those are design questions, not catalog questions, and they are why TRF engineers conveying as a system.

Two conveyor platforms, chosen by product and environment

Moving a sealed case through a packaging hall and moving exposed food between processing and packaging are different engineering problems. One is about flow and scalability, the other about hygiene, cleanability and uptime between sanitation cycles, so TRF answers them with two complementary platforms.

TRFIntraflow: Custom Conveyor Systems for Case Intralogistics

TRFIntraflow is the packaged-goods material-flow platform. It connects individual machines into one coordinated production flow using standardized modules, and it scales from one line to complete multi-line case intralogistics systems:

  • Straight conveying: powered roller, belt and modular belt conveying.
  • Curves and transfers: transfers, curves, turntables and pop-up transfers.
  • Accumulation and flow control: accumulation, zero-pressure accumulation, metering and gapping.
  • Merge, divert and sorting: merging, diverting, sorting and reject stations.
  • Elevation: incline and decline, elevators and lowerators, vertical transfers.
  • System integration: sensors, PLC and HMI, barcode and vision interfaces, machine communication, palletizer interfaces and warehouse interfaces where applicable.

Typical users are mid-sized and large food, beverage, CPG, protein, frozen food, pet food, personal care and industrial manufacturers that need reliable case movement, routing, accumulation or line balancing.

TRFCleanflow: Sanitary Conveyor Systems for Exposed Food

TRFCleanflow extends TRF automation upstream, providing hygienic product handling between processing, inspection and packaging. It is designed around sanitation, accessibility, product integrity and uptime rather than transportation alone. The platform has a Dry line and a Washdown line, and its priority industries are beef, pork, poultry, seafood, frozen foods, ready-to-eat products and dairy and cheese.

How do you choose between TRFIntraflow and TRFCleanflow?

Choose by the item being conveyed and the zone it moves through. Use TRFCleanflow when the item is exposed food or the conveyor sits in a hygienic or washdown zone, and TRFIntraflow when the item is a case, carton, tray, tote or other packaged unit moving between automation processes.

Can a single supplier engineer, build and install your entire conveyor system?

Yes. TRF manages engineering, manufacturing, controls, assembly, integration and commissioning within one coordinated delivery model, from concept and layout to installation and startup. Conveyors are never sold as loose sections: every project is an engineered system designed around your production flow and layout.

The reason is practical. A conveyor bought as a component solves a distance problem and leaves the flow problem to you: where accumulation goes, how a merge is metered, how the palletizer knows a case is ready and who answers when a transfer jams. When one partner owns the system, those questions are answered inside one scope of supply.

The same logic extends to the whole end of the line. End-of-line integration puts case packing, conveying and robotic palletizing under one engineering scope, with the interfaces to stretch wrapping, labeling and warehouse systems defined in the same project.

What is the difference between accumulation, merging, diverting and sorting?

Each function solves a different flow problem, and the wrong one in the wrong place creates the jam it was meant to prevent. Accumulation builds a buffer so upstream equipment keeps running while a downstream machine pauses. Zero-pressure accumulation does the same in zones that stop and release individually, so units do not push against each other and release stays predictable.

Metering releases units at a controlled rate and gapping creates space between them, which matters wherever a scanner, a diverter or a robot picks. Merging brings two or more flows into one lane and depends on metering to avoid collisions at the junction.

Diverting transfers units out of the main lane, for a second packing station or a reject line, and sorting routes units by destination, usually from barcode or vision data. A reject station removes flagged units without stopping the flow.

Information to prepare before requesting a conveyor layout

Before a first conversation with a TRF Sales Engineer, it helps to have:

  • Products and units: cases, cartons, trays, totes or exposed food, with dimensions and typical weight.
  • Flow map: where product enters and leaves the system, and which machines sit at each end.
  • Rates and shifts: average rates, peak rates and planned growth.
  • Environment and sanitation: dry, low-moisture or washdown, and how the area is cleaned today.
  • Layout and existing equipment: drawings with floor space, levels, columns and aisles, plus the case packer, palletizer, scanner or warehouse system the conveyor connects to.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can a Conveyor System Be Customized to My Exact Plant Layout?

Yes. TRF conveyor systems are custom engineered around your production flow, layout and operational requirements, using standard modules for conveying, transfers, accumulation, routing and elevation. Levels, columns, aisles and the positions of the machines at both ends are part of the layout study, so the system fits the plant you have instead of a catalog drawing.

Can a Conveyor System Integrate With My Palletizer and Case Packer?

Yes. TRFIntraflow includes system integration modules with sensors, PLC and HMI, barcode and vision interfaces, machine communication and palletizer interfaces. Because TRF also supplies case packing and palletizing platforms, the conveyor can be designed with them as one end-of-line system. Interfaces with equipment already installed are defined during project engineering, within the scope agreed for each project.
