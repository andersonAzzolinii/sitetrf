What do food plants pack and palletize?

Food plants pack pouches, pillow bags, flow-wrapped packs, thermoformed packages, trays, cartons, cups, tubs and flexible bags into shipping cases, then palletize those cases together with bundles, bags and pails. Each format has its own case packing or palletizing page on this site.

What slows down the end of a food processing line?

Five pressures slow the end of a food line, and each one has a TRF answer:

  • Many products and short runs. Pouches in the morning, flow-wrapped bars after lunch, cartons at night. TRFRapidpack changes products and pack patterns by recipe with reduced mechanical adjustments, and TRFCobot and TRFCompact store multiple recipes and pallet patterns.
  • Repetitive manual handling. Automating case loading and pallet building reduces repeated lifting, bending and twisting, and lets operators move to quality checks, line supervision or changeovers.
  • Sanitation boundaries. Exposed food in hygienic or washdown zones points to TRFCleanflow; cases, cartons, trays and totes point to TRFIntraflow. When a project crosses both zones, TRF combines them into one coordinated material-flow system.
  • Floor space in existing plants. TRFCompact is designed for brownfield plants, TRFOnepack consolidates three machines into one and TRFSpeed can consolidate several lines into one palletizing area.
  • Line balancing. TRFIntraflow accumulation, metering and gapping buffer the line, so a changeover at one machine does not have to stop the machine before it.

How does automation change for prepared Foods and ready-to-eat lines?

Prepared and ready-to-eat lines run many formats and SKUs on the same line, and exposed product needs hygienic handling before it is packaged.

On the exposed side, ready-to-eat products are among the priority applications for TRFCleanflow, which is designed around sanitation, accessibility, product integrity and uptime. The Dry line fits industrialized foods with moderate, infrequent cleaning; the Washdown line fits daily sanitation with chemicals, high pressure and high temperature.

On the packaged side, trays, thermoformed packages, cartons and cups often share one line. TRFRapidpack suits that mix, TRFOnepack suits smaller plants that want forming, packing and sealing in one machine, and recipe-based pallet patterns on TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed follow frequent product changes. When a prepared meal is palletized below 32°F, see frozen food and ice cream.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Which Palletizer and Case Packer Fit a Typical Food Processing Line?

It depends on line speed, product and floor space. Lower-speed, high-mix lines point to TRFCobot, limited space with more speed to TRFCompact, and high-volume or multi-line plants to TRFSpeed. For case packing, many SKUs and delicate items point to TRFRapidpack, integrated forming and sealing to TRFOnepack, and repetitive flexible bags to TRFDroppack. TRF engineering validates each application before quoting.

Can One Automation Partner Supply Palletizing, Case Packing and Conveyors for a Food Plant?

Yes. TRF supplies case packing, conveying and palletizing as one integrated end-of-line solution: TRFRapidpack, TRFOnepack or TRFDroppack pack the cases, TRFIntraflow moves and routes them, and TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed build the pallet. One partner is responsible from layout to startup, and Parts and Service supports TRF-supplied equipment afterward.

What Is the Difference Between a Sanitary Conveyor and a Standard Industrial Conveyor?

A sanitary conveyor carries exposed food and is built to be cleaned: TRFCleanflow uses open-frame construction, reduced horizontal surfaces and food-grade belting as design targets. A standard industrial conveyor such as TRFIntraflow moves packaged units (cases, cartons, trays and totes) between packing, palletizing and the warehouse. Most food plants need both, joined where the sanitation zone ends.

How Do You Choose Between a Robotic, an Integrated and a Gravity Case Packer?

Start with product behavior: a robotic case packer such as TRFRapidpack fits many SKUs, variable product positioning and delicate items. An integrated case packer such as TRFOnepack erects, packs and seals in one machine for smaller lines. A gravity case packer such as TRFDroppack fits repetitive flexible bags that tolerate controlled gravity loading. TRF evaluates all three for each product.

What Is the Difference Between a Dedicated Palletizing Cell and a Centralized Multi-Line System?

A dedicated cell palletizes one line next to its packer. A centralized system receives cases from several lines and builds their pallets in one area. TRFSpeed scales from a dedicated cell to a centralized multi-line system, and TRFIntraflow merges, routes and accumulates the cases so the palletizer receives an organized flow. TRF validates the combined rate for each plant.

How Is Payback Estimated for Food Processing Automation?

Payback depends on the labor hours the system replaces, shifts per day, local labor cost and the installed cost of the system. The estimate follows the method of the A3 (Association for Advancing Automation) robot ROI calculator: the system cost, including 20 years of maintenance at 5% per year, divided by the annual net savings, which is the labor cost of manual palletizing minus the electricity the robot uses. The palletizing ROI calculator applies this method to your location, your shifts and the people who palletize by hand today.
