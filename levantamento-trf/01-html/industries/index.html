The nine TRF platforms available to every industry

Every industry page presents all nine platforms, because the platform is chosen by line setup and speed rather than by sector. They are organized in three product groups: robotic palletizing, case packing and conveyor systems.

Palletizing Platforms

  • TRFCobot: collaborative palletizing for lower-speed, high-mix lines with limited floor space.
  • TRFCompact: an industrial robot in a compact, standardized cell, for more speed and duty cycle without a large footprint.
  • TRFSpeed: high-performance palletizing for high-volume, continuous and multi-line production.
  • TRFSubzero: palletizing for areas below 32°F, in frozen food and ice cream.

Case Packing Platforms

  • TRFRapidpack: flexible robotic top-load packing for high-mix lines and delicate or irregular products.
  • TRFOnepack: case erecting, robotic packing and sealing in one machine.
  • TRFDroppack: gravity case packing for repetitive flexible packages, such as VFFS, IQF and frozen food bags.

Conveying Platforms

  • TRFIntraflow: case intralogistics for packaged goods between packaging, case packing and palletizing.
  • TRFCleanflow: hygienic conveying of exposed food, with a Washdown line and a Dry line.

Beyond the platforms, a custom palletizer covers products, loads or layouts outside the standard ranges, and end-of-line integration connects case packing, conveying and palletizing as one system in any industry.

How each industry uses TRF platforms

Food, Protein and Frozen Food

Food processing, including prepared foods and ready-to-eat lines, can draw on all nine platforms from one partner. For protein processing, TRF supplies sanitary conveying, case handling, case packing and palletizing; primary processing machines stay with their current suppliers. Frozen food and ice cream lines pair TRFDroppack for IQF and frozen food bags with TRFSubzero for palletizing below 32°F.

Bakery, Confectionery, Dairy, Beverage and Pet Food

Bakery and snacks and confectionery are a focus of TRFRapidpack, which packs flow packs, pillow bags, trays and cartons with recipe-based changeovers. Dairy and cheese is a TRFCleanflow priority industry, and TRFDroppack packs grated cheese bags. Beverage and pet food plants use TRFIntraflow for case movement, and TRFDroppack also packs pet food.

Consumer Goods, Pharmaceutical and Industrial

Pharmaceutical and personal care and cosmetics lines with lower speeds and frequent product changes match the TRFCobot profile. Consumer packaged goods and household and cleaning products point to TRFCompact, TRFSpeed, TRFRapidpack, TRFOnepack and TRFIntraflow, while co-packers and e-commerce and distribution operations point to TRFCobot and TRFCompact. Chemicals plants match TRFCompact and TRFSpeed, and paper and packaging manufacturers can use TRFIntraflow for case and bundle movement, confirmed per application.

How do you choose packaging automation for your industry?

Start from your line, not the sector: the industry sets the conditions and the line sets the platform. Screen four variables before you shortlist equipment.

  1. Speed. Cases per minute decides among palletizers, and products per minute among case packers, using the figures in the table above.
  2. Load and product behavior. Case weight sets the palletizing platform. Repetitive flexible bags point to gravity packing, while delicate, variable or mixed products point to robotic packing.
  3. Space and layout. Brownfield plants with tight packaging areas favor compact cells and machines that combine functions, such as TRFOnepack. Multi-line plants favor a centralized palletizing area fed by TRFIntraflow.
  4. Environment. Exposed food and washdown zones point to TRFCleanflow, palletizing areas below 32°F to TRFSubzero, and packaged-goods zones to TRFIntraflow.

When more than one platform could work, choose the one that matches your throughput, production intensity and growth plans, not simply the lowest initial investment.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Which Industries Need Sanitary Conveyors?

Industries that move exposed food or run conveyors in hygienic and washdown zones. TRFCleanflow lists beef, pork, poultry, seafood, frozen foods, ready-to-eat products, and dairy and cheese among its priority industries. Where the item conveyed is a case, carton, tray or tote, the platform is TRFIntraflow instead, and a project that crosses both zones combines the two platforms in one coordinated material-flow system.

Which Industries Palletize Below Freezing?

Frozen food, ice cream, frozen prepared foods and the frozen areas of meat processing plants. For palletizing areas below 32°F, the platform is TRFSubzero, the TRF palletizer built for those areas.

Can One Automation Partner Supply Palletizing, Case Packing and Conveyors?

Yes. TRF supplies case packing, conveying and palletizing as one integrated end-of-line solution: TRFRapidpack, TRFOnepack or TRFDroppack pack the cases, TRFIntraflow moves and routes them, and TRFCobot, TRFCompact, TRFSpeed or TRFSubzero build the pallet. With end-of-line integration, one partner is responsible for engineering and integration from layout to startup.

How Does Automation Help With Labor Shortages in Food Plants?

Automation takes over the repetitive end-of-line tasks that are hardest to staff: grouping and loading products into cases, moving cases by hand and building pallets. Operators move to supervision, quality checks and changeovers, and the line no longer depends on how fast people can lift. Repetitive lifting, bending and twisting also leave the end of the line.

How Do I Estimate the Payback of Automation in My Plant?

For palletizing, use the palletizing ROI calculator. The estimate divides the system cost, including maintenance, by the annual net savings: the labor cost of manual palletizing minus the electricity the robot uses. The result is an initial planning estimate that a TRF Sales Engineer completes with the costs of your project.
