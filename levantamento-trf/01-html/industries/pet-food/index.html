What do pet food plants pack and palletize?

Pet food plants palletize large bags of dry food directly and case pack small bags, pillow bags, pouches, cartons, trays and cups before palletizing the cases. The table maps each format to its usual path and to the page that covers it.

What is a drop packer, and how does gravity packing work?

A drop packer groups bags into the pack pattern and lets them drop into the open case in a controlled way, so no robot picks each bag. TRFDroppack works this way: bags arrive from the bagger, are collated into the count and arrangement the case needs and are loaded from the top. It packs pet food, VFFS packages and similar free-flowing packaged products.

Because the product moves vertically, the machine can keep a compact footprint, and without an end-of-arm tool there are fewer complex components to maintain. The condition is a repetitive pack pattern and a package that tolerates controlled gravity loading. When a pouch needs a specific orientation or the plant changes patterns several times a day, robotic case packing with TRFRapidpack is the closer match; when a smaller line also wants case forming and sealing, TRFOnepack combines the three steps. For a pet food case packer project, TRF needs the bag dimensions and weight, the fill behavior, the target rate, bags per case, the case style and the SKU list.

A custom palletizer is engineered when the bag weight, the rate, the number of lines or the layout goes beyond the standard platforms. With pet food end-of-line integration, the case packer, conveyors and palletizer share one controls concept and one partner is responsible for startup.

What payload does a palletizing robot need for my product?

The robot must carry the bag or case plus the end-of-arm tool, and for multi-unit picks the combined weight. TRF screens its palletizers by load: TRFCobot up to about 22 lb, TRFCompact up to about 44 lb and TRFSpeed up to about 66 lb, always subject to end-of-arm tool and reach validation. Bags beyond that range move the project to a custom palletizer.

Payload is only the first decision for a pet food palletizer. Slip sheets can be added between layers on TRFSpeed when the load requires it. The bag palletizer page covers grippers and patterns in detail.

Frequently asked questions

What Is Slip Sheet Palletizing and When Is It Needed?

Slip sheet palletizing places a thin sheet between pallet layers to separate them and help keep the load stable. Pet food plants use it when bags settle or shift, or when a customer requires it. TRFSpeed handles slip sheets together with automatic pallet handling and pallet discharge, so operators do not have to stop other work to insert sheets by hand.

What Is the Difference Between a Dedicated Palletizing Cell and a Centralized Multi-Line System?

A dedicated cell palletizes one line next to its bagger or case packer. A centralized system receives bags or cases from several lines and builds their pallets in one area, each line keeping its own pallet pattern. TRFSpeed scales from a dedicated cell to a centralized system, and TRFIntraflow merges and accumulates units from each line. TRF validates the combined rate.

How Does a Robotic Palletizer Handle SKU or Pallet Pattern Changeovers?

Through stored recipes. A new bag size, pack pattern or pallet pattern becomes a selection on the operator screen rather than a mechanical rebuild. TRFCobot and TRFCompact support multiple recipes and pallet patterns. Some changes still need parts, such as a different gripper for a new bag format, so the application study lists which SKUs share tooling before purchase.

Can One Automation Partner Supply Palletizing, Case Packing and Conveyors for a Pet Food Plant?

Yes. TRF supplies case packing, conveying and palletizing as one integrated end-of-line solution: TRFDroppack, TRFRapidpack or TRFOnepack pack the cases, TRFIntraflow moves and routes them, and TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed build the pallet. One partner is responsible from layout to startup, and Parts and Service supports TRF-supplied equipment afterward.

How Is Payback Estimated for Pet Food Packaging Automation?

Payback depends on the labor hours the system replaces, shifts per day, local labor cost and the installed cost of the system. The estimate follows the method of the A3 (Association for Advancing Automation) robot ROI calculator: the system cost, including 20 years of maintenance at 5% per year, divided by the annual net savings, which is the labor cost of manual palletizing minus the electricity the robot uses. The palletizing payback calculator applies this method to your location, your shifts and the people who palletize by hand today.
