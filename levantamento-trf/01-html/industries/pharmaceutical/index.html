End-of-line challenges in pharmaceutical plants

The end of a pharmaceutical packaging line is shaped by product variety rather than by volume. Three challenges stand out.

Short Runs and Frequent Product Changes

A packaging suite can run several products, strengths and pack counts in one week, and each one can change the case size and the pallet pattern. Equipment that needs a mechanical rebuild for every change consumes time the schedule does not have, so recipe-based changeovers matter more than peak speed.

Tight Floor Space After Packaging

Packaging suites are built around the primary packaging equipment, and the space left at the end of the line is often a narrow corridor. A traditional robotic cell can be hard to install there, so footprint, operator access and pallet exchange have to be solved together.

Repetitive Lifting by Trained Staff

In many plants, the people stacking cases at the end of the line are the trained operators who also run the packaging equipment. Manual palletizing takes them away from the line and repeats the same lifting, bending and twisting all shift. Automating the pallet keeps qualified staff on supervision, quality checks and changeovers.

What is packed and palletized at the end of a pharmaceutical line?

Finished pharmaceutical products reach the end of the line as cartons, trays, pouches or sealed shipping cases. Cartons and smaller packages go through a pharmaceutical case packer and then to the palletizer; sealed cases go straight to the palletizer. Each format has its own detailed page.

Why does a cobot palletizer fit a high-mix pharmaceutical line?

In pharmaceutical palletizing, a cobot palletizer fits because demand at the end of the line is defined by variety and space, not by throughput. TRFCobot stores multiple recipes, pallet patterns and SKU changes, so a product change is a recipe selection instead of a mechanical rebuild.

Footprint is the second reason. TRFCobot is built for areas where a traditional robotic cell would be difficult to install, which describes many packaging suites. Its standardized architecture and intuitive operation also lower the barrier to a first automation project: operators learn one cell and one routine.

The third reason is the investment profile. TRFCobot is a lower-entry-cost path to robotic palletizing, particularly in one- to three-shift operations. When the rate grows beyond the collaborative range, the application moves to TRFCompact; several suites feeding one palletizing area point to TRFSpeed. The robotic palletizer cost page explains what makes up the installed cost.

Integrated end-of-line automation for pharmaceutical plants

Integrated end-of-line automation means case packing, conveying and palletizing are engineered as one system by one supplier, instead of three machines bought separately and connected by the plant. TRFRapidpack, TRFOnepack or TRFDroppack pack the cases, TRFIntraflow moves and routes them, and TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed build the pallet.

For a pharmaceutical plant, fewer suppliers means fewer interfaces to define and one counterpart when something has to be adjusted. TRFIntraflow includes barcode and vision interfaces, machine communication and reject stations, while systems that print or verify codes stay with their own suppliers. TRF engineering defines the signals exchanged with them for each project.

Case erecting and sealing are supplied inside TRFOnepack or a complete line, never as stand-alone case erectors, and conveyors are engineered as part of a system.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How Many Cases per Minute Can a Robotic Palletizer Handle?

It depends on the platform. TRFCobot covers about 1 to 10 cases per minute, TRFCompact about 5 to 15 and TRFSpeed about 10 to 35. Lower-speed pharmaceutical lines fit the TRFCobot range. These are typical ranges for commercial screening: TRF confirms the final rate for your product, end-of-arm tool, pallet pattern and reach.

What Automation Reduces Labor Dependency in a Pharmaceutical Packaging Line?

Palletizing and case packing are usually the first steps to automate, because they are the most repetitive manual tasks after primary packaging. A cobot palletizer takes over case stacking, a case packer takes over grouping units into cases, and conveying removes manual carrying between them. Operators move to line supervision, quality checks and changeovers.

How Much Floor Space Does a Compact Palletizing Cell Require?

It depends on the pallet size, the number of pallet positions, how full pallets leave the cell and how operators reach it. TRFCobot and TRFCompact are designed as compact cells for plants where space at the end of the line is limited. TRF draws a layout with the cell, the infeed conveyor and operator access during the application review.

What Is the Difference Between an Integrated Case Packer and Separate Erecting, Packing and Sealing Machines?

An integrated case packer erects the case, loads it and seals it in one coordinated machine. TRFOnepack works this way, with one controls platform, fewer interfaces and a smaller footprint than three separate machines connected by conveyors. Separate machines make sense when each step must be sized or replaced independently.

Can a Palletizer Be Integrated With My Existing Conveyor and Wrapping Line?

Yes. TRFIntraflow supplies the accumulation, machine communication and palletizer interfaces that connect a new cell to the conveyors already installed, and TRFSpeed projects can include interfaces to wrapping, labeling and warehouse systems. The scope of each interface is defined during project engineering, so one partner is responsible for how the new cell fits the line.

How Is Payback Estimated for a Robotic Palletizer?

Payback depends on the labor hours the palletizer replaces, shifts per day, local labor cost and the installed cost of the system. The estimate follows the method of the A3 (Association for Advancing Automation) robot ROI calculator: the system cost, including 20 years of maintenance at 5% per year, divided by the annual net savings, which is the labor cost of manual palletizing minus the electricity the robot uses. The payback calculator returns an initial estimate that a TRF Sales Engineer completes with your project costs.
