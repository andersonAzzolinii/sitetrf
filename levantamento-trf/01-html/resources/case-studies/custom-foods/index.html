The challenge: Why palletizing below freezing is hard

The case lists four challenges the plant faced before automation:

  • Productivity declines over time. Manual output falls as exposure to the cold accumulates across a shift.
  • Physical limitations of manual operations. Repetitive lifting is harder to sustain in sub-zero conditions than at ambient temperature.
  • High ergonomic risk. Lifting, twisting and reaching case after case in the cold puts strain on operators.
  • Difficulty maintaining production throughput. When the palletizing station cannot hold rate, the whole line slows down.

The solution: A TRFSubzero system engineered for cold storage

TRF implemented a complete robotic palletizing solution for cold storage. The case describes three elements:

  • A TRFSubzero robotic palletizing system for cold storage applications.
  • A KUKA KR QUANTEC PA Arctic robot, designed for sub-zero operation and engineered to work reliably at extreme temperatures.
  • Components and engineering developed specifically for frozen environments.

What changed at the end of the line

The customer reports three outcomes at Custom Foods:

  • A significant increase in end-of-line throughput.
  • A standardized and stable operation regardless of temperature.
  • Improved employee safety and ergonomics.

The repetitive lifting in sub-zero conditions moves from people to the robot.

Can a robotic palletizer operate below freezing?

Yes, when the cell is engineered for it, as at Custom Foods: a robot designed for sub-zero operation, with components and engineering developed for frozen environments. TRFSubzero is the TRF palletizing system for areas below 32°F, used in frozen food, ice cream, and refrigerated and frozen areas.

For areas at normal temperature, the TRF palletizing family covers lines by speed and case weight:

How do you build the business case for freezer automation?

Build it like any palletizing project, with extra attention to the labor inputs of a cold area. The estimate follows the method of the A3 (Association for Advancing Automation) robot ROI calculator: the system cost, including 20 years of maintenance at 5% per year, divided by the annual net savings, which is the labor cost of manual palletizing minus the electricity the robot uses.

In a freezer, productivity that declines across a shift and throughput lost when the station cannot hold rate belong next to that number. Run your own shifts and labor cost in the palletizing ROI calculator, and read how much a robotic palletizer costs for the investment side.

Cold chain palletizing applies across frozen food and ice cream, protein processing and broader food processing plants with refrigerated or frozen finished goods.

What Payload Does a Palletizing Robot Need for My Product?

The robot must carry the case weight plus the end-of-arm tool, and for multi-case picks, the combined weight of the cases. How a case behaves when cold also affects gripping. TRF validates the final payload with your product, end-of-arm tool, pallet pattern and reach, and the table above shows the typical case weight for each platform.

How Does a Robotic Palletizer Handle SKU or Pallet Pattern Changeovers?

A changeover usually means selecting a stored recipe that holds the case dimensions, pick logic and pallet pattern for the new SKU. When the new case fits the same end-of-arm tool, the change happens mostly in software. TRFCobot and TRFCompact store multiple recipes and pallet patterns, so a SKU change does not mean rebuilding the process.
