Robotic Palletizing / Bundle Palletizer

Bundle palletizer sized to your bundles and line rate.

Keep shrink-wrapped and strapped bundles square from the line to the pallet, without manual lifting.

A bundle palletizer is a robotic system that stacks bundles, such as shrink-wrapped multipacks or strapped stacks of paper and packaging, onto pallets in stable layers, using tooling that supports soft, flexible loads. TRF Industrial Automation builds bundle palletizers on TRFCobot, TRFCompact and TRFSpeed, or as a custom palletizer, and the right model is defined by your line setup and speed.

What is a bundle palletizer?

A bundle palletizer turns a stream of bundles into a shippable pallet load. It picks each bundle from the infeed conveyor, turns it when the pattern calls for it, places it on the pallet and repeats the cycle until the load is complete, following a stored recipe for bundle size, pattern, layer count and tier sheets.

On a production line, a bundle is a group of products held together as one unit without a closed case around it:

  • Film-only shrink bundles, such as bottles or cans grouped and wrapped in shrink film.
  • Pad or tray and shrink bundles, where products stand on a pad or shallow tray inside the film, which gives a firmer base.
  • Strapped or banded bundles of flat goods, such as paper products, paperboard or blanks.

If your product ships in a closed case, see the case palletizer. If trays of product are stacked without film, see the tray palletizer.

Bundles are the shipping unit in beverage, paper and packaging, household and cleaning products and co-packers. The business case starts with labor and ergonomics: stacking heavy multipacks by hand means repeated lifting, bending and twisting at the end of the line, shift after shift.

Why Bundles Are Harder to Palletize Than Cases

A closed case has flat, rigid walls on every side; a bundle often does not. A film-only bundle has an uneven top of caps and shoulders, a bottom that flexes when lifted from the ends and film that slips against the film of the next layer. A strapped bundle is rigid but heavy, and its edges dent if a tool presses in the wrong place. These traits change how the bundle is gripped, how it is turned and which pattern holds together.

How does a robot pick shrink-wrapped bundles without damaging them?

With an end-of-arm tool selected for your bundle and a well-prepared infeed. A film-only bundle generally needs support across its whole base, so the bottom does not sag and the film is not stressed. Heavier multipacks need a tool that holds them firmly while the robot turns them. TRF validates the tool with production bundles.

The infeed matters as much as the tool. Bundles leave the shrink tunnel with warm film that has not fully set, so a section of conveyor between the tunnel and the palletizer lets the film firm up before the pick. On paper and packaging lines, bundles come from a stacker and a strapping station, and the palletizer needs straps tensioned and bundles square. A tool that supports the bundle from below also needs space to withdraw after placement, which is part of the pattern design from the start.

How do you keep a pallet of bundles stable?

A stable bundle pallet comes from the pattern, the sheet plan and the handoff to wrapping, designed together, because film-wrapped layers slide more easily than case layers.

Stability factor Why it matters for bundles How it is handled

Tier sheets Film-on-film contact and heavy layers Sheets between layers, placed manually or automatically

The recipe also controls which face points outward, so labels and the open ends of the shrink film sit where the plant wants them on the finished pallet. Automatic slip sheet handling is part of the TRFSpeed scope; on lower-speed cells, the sheet approach is defined with the application.

How do you choose a bundle palletizer?

Choose by line setup and speed: how many bundles per minute the palletizer must sustain, including peaks after a stop, and how many lines will feed it now and in the coming years. Then confirm with the heaviest bundle, its film, pad or straps, and the floor space.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can One Palletizer Handle Different Bundle Sizes and Pallet Patterns?

Yes, within the validated application. TRF palletizing platforms run multiple recipes and pallet patterns, so the same bundle palletizer can handle several bundle sizes and patterns, selected by the operator at changeover. Each size is validated with its end-of-arm tool and pattern during project engineering, and very different formats, such as film bundles and strapped bundles on one line, may need separate tools.

What Payload Does a Palletizing Robot Need for Heavy Multipacks?

The robot must carry the bundle plus the end-of-arm tool, and for multipick, the combined weight of the bundles. Published case weights are up to about 22 lb for TRFCobot, 44 lb for TRFCompact and 66 lb for TRFSpeed. TRF validates the payload with your heaviest bundle and evaluates a custom palletizer when the bundle is heavier than that range.

Can One Bundle Palletizer Serve Several Bundling Lines?

Yes, when the combined rate and the layout fit. TRFSpeed is built to consolidate the flow of multiple production lines into one coordinated palletizing system, and its modular design lets a project start as a dedicated cell and grow into a centralized palletizing area. Combined rate, bundle mix and conveyor routing are validated in the application review.

Can a Bundle Palletizer Be Integrated With My Shrink Bundler, Conveyors and Wrapper?

Yes. TRF engineers the bundler or strapper outfeed, the cooling and accumulation conveyors, the palletizer, wrapping and labeling as one system. TRFIntraflow provides modules for conveying, accumulation, routing and elevation, and end-of-line integration puts the whole flow under one partner, from layout to startup.

Can a Bundle Palletizer Handle Strapped Paper and Packaging Bundles?

Yes, within the platform ranges or as a custom palletizer. Strapped bundles of paper products, paperboard or blanks are rigid but heavy and sensitive to edge damage, so the end-of-arm tool spreads the pressure and keeps straps intact. When a bundle is heavier or larger than the platform ranges allow, TRF evaluates a custom palletizer after validation.

How Much Does a Bundle Palletizer Cost?

Cost depends on the platform, bundle rate, bundle weight, end-of-arm tool, pallet and sheet handling and conveyor integration. As a reference, list prices are US$ 120,000 for the TRFCobot Dual, US$ 180,000 for the TRFCompact Dual and US$ 350,000 for the TRFSpeed 100, EX Works Lenexa, Kansas, installation not included. The robotic palletizer cost page explains each factor.

What Information Should I Prepare to Size a Bundle Palletizer?

Bring your bundle types, dimensions and weights, film or strap details, the bundle rate at peak, shifts per day, the number of lines, your pallet size and target height, and a drawing of the end of the line. A TRF Sales Engineer uses this data to screen TRFCobot, TRFCompact, TRFSpeed or a custom palletizer, and the payback calculator gives you a first estimate.
