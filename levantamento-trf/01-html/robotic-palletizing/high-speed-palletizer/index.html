What is a high-speed palletizing system, and how is it different from a compact palletizing cell?

A high-speed palletizing system is an automated cell, or a group of connected cells, that builds pallets at the rate of a continuous packaging operation, usually with an industrial robot, automatic pallet handling and conveyors that feed it from one or more production lines. It is the end-of-line station that has to keep up with everything upstream.

Compared with a compact cell, a high-speed system adds the functions that keep it moving at higher rates. Multipick, picking more than one case per cycle, raises effective throughput. Automatic pallet handling keeps empty pallets flowing in and full pallets flowing out. Slip sheets, when the load needs them, are placed in the same sequence. These functions decide whether the typical range holds on your line, so they are validated with each application.

TRFSpeed is built for that reality without forcing every customer into a full custom project. It is a configurable platform: modular engineering reduces project risk, while the configuration keeps the flexibility each production flow requires. A system that starts as a dedicated cell can be extended later, when a new line comes online.

What Is the Difference Between a Dedicated Palletizing Cell and a Centralized Multi-Line System?

A dedicated cell palletizes one line at its own rate. A centralized multi-line system receives cases from several lines, routes them and builds a pallet for each SKU in one palletizing area.

Question Dedicated palletizing cell Centralized multi-line system
Lines served One line Two or more lines consolidated in one area
Conveying Short run from the case packer to the cell Routing, merging and accumulation from each line to the palletizing area
Floor space Space at the end of each line One palletizing area, freeing space along the lines
Growth Add a cell per new line Add a line to the existing system where capacity allows
Typical driver A single line is the bottleneck Several lines, capacity expansion or labor concentrated at the end of line

The choice depends on how many lines you run, how far apart they are, the product mix and how much you expect to grow. TRF reviews these factors in the application study and sizes the system so the palletizer does not become the bottleneck of the plant.

Which applications fit a high-speed palletizer?

A high-speed palletizer fits high-capacity plants that run continuously and plants with several lines to consolidate. Five customer profiles fit TRFSpeed:

  • Mid-sized and large food, CPG and chemical manufacturers, with a strong focus on food processing and cold-chain applications. See also chemical packaging automation and household and cleaning products.
  • High-capacity plants operating two or three shifts, including continuous 24/7 production.
  • Facilities with several production lines that can be consolidated into one palletizing area. See CPG manufacturers and co-packers.
  • Customers planning capacity expansion and concerned about end-of-line bottlenecks.
  • Operations that need deeper end-of-line integration, pallet handling or system customization.

The platform is selected by cases per minute and case weight, so rate, weight and pack format decide the fit for your load. The pages by palletized product, such as the case palletizer and tray palletizer pages, explain the selection for each one.

Frequently asked questions

How Much Does a High-Speed Palletizing System Cost?

The TRFSpeed 100 has a reference list price of US$ 350,000, EX Works Lenexa, Kansas, installation not included. Installation, line integration and options are quoted with your application, because line speed, case weight, the number of lines feeding the cell, pallet handling and layout change the scope. Use the payback calculator on this page to compare it with what manual palletizing costs you today.

How Many Cases per Minute Can a Robotic Palletizer Handle?

It depends on the platform, the case and the pallet pattern. TRFCobot covers about 1 to 10 cases per minute, TRFCompact about 5 to 15 and TRFSpeed about 10 to 35. These are typical ranges for commercial screening: TRF confirms the final rate for your product, end-of-arm tool, pallet pattern, reach and process, including whether multipick is needed to hold the rate.

What Is Slip Sheet Palletizing and When Is It Needed?

Slip sheet palletizing places a thin sheet between layers, under the load or between the products and the pallet. It is used when layers need separation or extra stability, or when the receiving operation requires it. On TRFSpeed, slip sheet placement is one of the pallet handling functions configured for the project, along with automatic pallet feeding and pallet discharge.

Can a Palletizer Be Integrated With My Existing Conveyor and Wrapping Line?

Yes, when the interfaces are defined in the application study: infeed rates and orientation, spacing, signals exchanged between machines and how full pallets travel to the stretch wrapper or the warehouse. TRF engineers these interfaces as part of the project and can supply TRFIntraflow conveyors where the existing line needs accumulation, routing or elevation, so the end of line works as one system.

Can a High-Speed Palletizer Keep the End of My Line From Becoming the Bottleneck?

Yes, when it is sized for your peak rate. TRFSpeed is designed for production environments where the palletizer cannot become the bottleneck: multipick and automatic pallet handling keep the cell moving, and accumulation ahead of the cell absorbs short variations between the lines and the palletizer. TRF engineering sizes the system from the rate of each line, the product mix and the growth you expect.

How Does a Robotic Palletizer Handle SKU or Pallet Pattern Changeovers?

A changeover means selecting the stored recipe for the new SKU, which holds case dimensions, pick logic and pallet pattern. When the new case fits the same end-of-arm tool, the change is mostly in software. On a multi-line TRFSpeed system, recipes also define how cases from different lines are routed and which pallet each SKU builds, so several products can run at the same time.

What Is the Typical Payback Period for Switching From Manual to Robotic Palletizing?

Payback depends on the labor hours the palletizer replaces, shifts per day, local labor cost and the installed cost of the system. The estimate follows the method of the A3 (Association for Advancing Automation) robot ROI calculator: the system cost, including 20 years of maintenance at 5% per year, divided by the annual net savings, which is the labor cost of manual palletizing minus the electricity the robot uses. The calculator on this page applies this method to your location, your shifts and the people who palletize by hand today, and a TRF Sales Engineer completes it with the costs of your project.

What Maintenance Does a Robotic Palletizer Require to Avoid Downtime?

A robotic palletizer needs periodic inspection of the robot, end-of-arm tool, sensors, conveyors and pallet handling, following the manufacturer's schedule, plus replacement of wear parts before they fail. For TRF-supplied equipment, Parts and Service covers technical support, spare parts kept in the United States and the TRFCare preventive maintenance program.

Who Makes High-Speed Robotic Palletizers for Food Manufacturers in the US?

TRF designs, builds, installs and supports robotic palletizing systems for food, CPG and chemical manufacturers across the United States. Its palletizing family covers TRFCobot, TRFCompact, TRFSpeed and TRFSubzero, plus custom palletizers, and the same partner supplies case packing and conveyor systems, so the end of line can be engineered as one project with one point of contact.
