## What Does a Case Packer Do on a Production Line?

A case packer groups primary packages into the pattern the case requires and loads them into a shipping case, the secondary packaging step before palletizing. In a top-load machine, the case is presented open and the product enters from above, placed by a robot or released through a controlled drop.

The sequence is similar on most lines. Products arrive from primary packaging, often unevenly spaced, and the machine collates them by rows, layers or counted batches. The case is loaded, closed and sealed, then conveyed toward palletizing; in an integrated machine, it is erected from a flat blank right before loading.

Two variables decide how hard that sequence is. The first is product behavior: a rigid carton, a flexible pouch that changes shape when picked and a thermoformed pack that must not be pressed are different problems. The second is the mix: two SKUs and one pattern is a different project from many SKUs, several case formats and frequent changeovers.

## Three Case Packing Platforms: Flexibility, Integration and Efficiency

The TRF case packing family is organized by application rather than by machine size. Robotic handling brings flexibility and gentle product control, integration brings fewer machines and interfaces, and gravity brings mechanical simplicity.

### TRFRapidpack: Flexible Robotic Case Packing

[TRFRapidpack](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/robotic-case-packer/) is the flexible robotic platform for high-mix lines and difficult products. Vision-guided or indexed robotic picking handles pouches, pillow bags, flow packs, thermoformed packages, trays, cartons, cups, tubs and other small packaged products that arrive in variable positions. Recipe-based changeovers reduce mechanical adjustments, controlled handling protects delicate or irregular products, and the cell can grow from one robotic module to several.

### TRFOnepack: Erecting, Packing and Sealing in One Machine

[TRFOnepack](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/integrated-case-packer/) combines case erecting, robotic product loading and case sealing in one integrated platform. That means fewer machines, fewer control systems, fewer integration points and a smaller footprint, while keeping the flexibility of robotic loading. It is built for small and mid-sized food and CPG manufacturers replacing manual or semi-automatic secondary packaging.

### TRFDroppack: Gravity Case Packing for Flexible Packages

[TRFDroppack](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/drop-packer/) collates flexible packages and loads them into cases through a controlled drop, for lines that need medium to low throughput without robotic complexity. With no robotic end-of-arm tool and fewer complex components, it suits pillow bags, pouches, VFFS packages, IQF and frozen food bags, grated cheese, rice and grains, pet food and similar free-flowing packaged products.

The decision rules are direct. Position TRFRapidpack when the application has high SKU variability, complex pack patterns, variable product positioning or needs vision-guided handling. Position TRFOnepack when the priority is combining erecting, packing and sealing while reducing equipment interfaces and floor space. Position TRFDroppack when the product is a repetitive flexible package that tolerates controlled gravity loading.

Some lines qualify for more than one platform. Then the tiebreakers are product integrity, the SKUs the machine must cover as your catalog grows, the floor space available and how much of the line you want to buy from one supplier.

## What Is the Difference Between a Case Packer and a Case Erector?

A case packer loads product into the shipping case; a case erector forms the case from a flat blank. At TRF, erecting is part of the packing system rather than a separate machine: TRFOnepack erects, packs and seals in one platform, and case forming and closing are included in [end-of-line integration](https://trfautomation.com/conveyor-systems/end-of-line-integration/) projects. TRF does not supply stand-alone case erectors.

The practical benefit is fewer interfaces. Every separate machine in secondary packaging adds a handoff, a control system and a supplier relationship, so consolidating erecting, packing and sealing reduces the places where the flow can break.

## Information to Prepare Before Choosing a Case Packer

With the inputs below, a TRF Sales Engineer can recommend a platform, a tooling approach and a configuration:

- **Product:** package type, dimensions, weight, rigidity and how it behaves when picked or dropped.
- **Rate:** products per minute per line, peak versus average, and how many lines feed the case packer.
- **Pack pattern and case:** count per case, orientation, layers, case dimensions, erected cases or flat blanks, and sealing method.
- **Mix and changeover:** number of SKUs, changeover frequency and expected catalog growth.
- **Layout and integration:** floor space between primary packaging and palletizing, upstream machines, conveyors and the palletizer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is Top-Load Case Packing, and When Is It the Right Choice?

Top-load case packing loads product into the case from above, by robotic pick-and-place or by a controlled gravity drop. It suits pack patterns built by rows or layers, mixed SKU production and delicate products. All three TRF platforms are top-load machines, and the [top-load case packer page](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/top-load-case-packer/) compares them by application.

### What Is a Drop Packer and How Does Gravity Packing Work?

A drop packer groups packages above the case and releases them through a controlled drop instead of picking them with a robot. TRFDroppack uses this principle for repetitive flexible packages such as pillow bags, pouches, VFFS and IQF bags. With no robotic end-of-arm tool and fewer complex components, the machine stays simple to operate and maintain.

### Can a Robotic Case Packer Handle Pouches, Trays and Thermoformed Packages in the Same Line?

Yes, within the validated application envelope. TRFRapidpack is built for high-mix production and handles pouches, pillow bags, flow packs, thermoformed packages, trays, cartons, cups and tubs with recipe-based changeovers. The limits are the tooling and the pack patterns, which TRF validates against your complete SKU list before the machine is specified.

### How Many Products per Minute Can a Robotic Case Packer Run?

TRFRapidpack is positioned for applications of about 100 to 200 products per minute, TRFOnepack for about 20 to 50 and TRFDroppack for about 20 to 500. These are application ranges used for commercial screening, not nominal machine speeds. Final capacity is validated for your product, pack pattern, tooling and case format.

### Can a Case Packer Handle Frozen or IQF Products?

Yes. IQF product bags and frozen food bags are among the products TRFDroppack handles with controlled gravity loading, and frozen food is a focus industry for TRFRapidpack. On frozen lines, palletizing below 32°F is covered by [TRFSubzero](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/subzero-palletizer/), so case packing and palletizing can be designed together for the same plant.

### How Does a Case Packer Protect Delicate or Irregularly Shaped Products?

With controlled handling. TRFRapidpack picks thermoformed packs, trays, filled pouches and flow-wrapped items robotically, with tooling selected for each product. Vision-guided picking also handles products that arrive in variable positions, protecting product integrity while the line keeps its rate. For repetitive flexible packages that tolerate a controlled drop, TRFDroppack avoids robotic handling altogether.

### Can Case Packing Equipment Integrate With My Existing Palletizer and Conveyors?

Yes. Each TRF case packer can run as a standalone machine or as part of a TRF end-of-line system. [TRFIntraflow](https://trfautomation.com/conveyor-systems/case-conveyor-intralogistics/) conveys, accumulates and routes sealed cases to the palletizer, and the [conveyor systems page](https://trfautomation.com/conveyor-systems/) explains how the flow is engineered. Interfaces with equipment already installed are defined during project engineering.

### What Is the Typical ROI of Switching From Manual to Robotic Case Packing?

There is no single figure. The return comes from the operator hours the case packer removes, multiplied by your loaded labor cost and compared with the installed cost of the system. Throughput that no longer depends on manual speed and controlled handling that protects the product add value beyond labor. The [palletizing ROI calculator](https://trfautomation.com/tools/palletizing-roi-calculator/) applies the same labor logic to the palletizing side.
