## What Is a Carton Packing Machine?

A carton packing machine is the equipment that turns a stream of individual retail cartons into shipping cases ready for the pallet. It collects cartons as they leave the cartoner and the checks after it, groups them into the count and arrangement each customer requires, and lowers that group into an open case so it can be sealed, coded and palletized.

Folding cartons carry the brand at retail: frozen entrees, cereal and snack cartons, candy boxes and household products. They share useful properties for automation. They are rigid and rectangular, they present flat faces a tool can hold, and they stack predictably into layers. What varies from plant to plant is the mix of sizes, the orientation retailers expect and how fast the cartoner discharges.

Because TRFRapidpack and TRFOnepack load through the open top of the case, cartons are placed rather than pushed, which keeps control of orientation and protects the printed faces. The [top load case packer](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/top-load-case-packer/) page explains the format across the family. Carton lines are common in [frozen food and ice cream](https://trfautomation.com/industries/frozen-food-and-ice-cream/), [confectionery](https://trfautomation.com/industries/confectionery/), [CPG](https://trfautomation.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/) and [household and cleaning products](https://trfautomation.com/industries/household-and-cleaning-products/) plants.

## What Is the Difference Between a Carton Packing Machine and a Cartoner?

A cartoner forms the folding carton and puts product into it. A carton packing machine takes cartons that are already formed, filled and closed and packs them into shipping cases for distribution. The two machines sit one after the other: the cartoner serves retail presentation, the case packer serves secondary packaging.

TRF supplies the second step. The case packer has to keep pace with whatever the cartoner discharges, so the interface between them, including line signals, SKU changes and an accumulation buffer that keeps the cartoner running through a case change, is defined during project engineering. Rejects from the checkweigher or code verification should leave the line before the packing area.

## How Are Cartons Packed Into Cases?

Cartons are packed in a pattern that sets the count, orientation and layers in each case, defined per SKU and stored as a recipe. The details that decide a carton project are orientation, collation and surface protection.

- **Orientation.** Cartons can enter the case flat, on edge or standing. Retailers may ask for a specific face toward the opening of a shelf-ready case, and some cartons go on edge so the case holds more units.
- **Collation and counts.** Cartons usually reach the case in groups, built by the robot picking several cartons per cycle, by an infeed that gathers a row or both. A plant often ships the same carton in more than one count.
- **Graphics and squareness.** Tooling and motion are set to avoid marking the print. Cartons that are slightly out of square or bowed after filling behave differently in a tight pattern, so TRF asks for samples from normal production.

One carton packing machine can run several sizes and patterns within the validated application. TRFRapidpack changes products and patterns by recipe, with reduced mechanical adjustments, and TRFOnepack runs multiple products, recipes and packing patterns through robotic loading. Sharing planned carton formats early keeps a new SKU from becoming a mechanical rebuild after startup.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a Carton Packing Machine Need a Vision System?

Only when carton position varies. TRFRapidpack packs with vision-guided delta robots when cartons arrive in a variable flow, or with indexed picking when they arrive in a controlled position, which is common when the cartoner discharges in a fixed index. The infeed design and the number of SKUs decide which approach fits, and TRF defines it in the application review.

### Should Cartons Go Into a Shipping Case or Straight Onto the Pallet?

It depends on the channel. Cartons that ship in cases need a carton packing machine, while display-ready cartons that go directly onto the pallet need a [carton palletizer](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/carton-palletizer/). The two paths use different tooling, patterns and conveying, so decide early. Plants that do both can have the case packer, the conveying and the palletizer engineered by TRF as one project.

### What Is the Difference Between an Integrated Case Packer and Separate Erecting, Packing and Sealing Machines?

An integrated case packer erects the case, loads it and seals it in one coordinated machine, with one controls platform. Separate machines each need their own interfaces, controls and floor space. TRFOnepack is the integrated option, for applications of about 20 to 50 products per minute, and it can start standalone and later join a complete TRF end-of-line system.

### How Many Products per Minute Can a Robotic Case Packer Run?

TRFRapidpack is designed for applications of about 100 to 200 products per minute. TRF publishes application ranges, not machine speeds: actual capacity depends on the carton, the collation, the pick tool, the pack pattern and how cartons leave the cartoner, and TRF engineering validates it for each line before the design is closed.

### Can Case Packing Equipment Integrate With My Existing Palletizer and Conveyors?

Yes. Each TRF case packing platform can run standalone or integrate with conveyors, case handling, palletizing and intralogistics as one end-of-line system. TRFIntraflow routes cases from one or several packing lines to a TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed palletizer, and interfaces with existing equipment are defined during project engineering. See [end-of-line integration](https://trfautomation.com/conveyor-systems/end-of-line-integration/).

### What Is the Difference Between a Case Packer and a Case Erector?

A case erector forms an open case from a flat blank, and a case packer loads products into that case. TRF supplies case erecting only as part of an integrated solution: TRFOnepack erects, packs and seals in one machine, and end-of-line projects with TRFRapidpack include case forming and closing, so one partner answers for the flow from flat case to sealed case.

### What Is the Typical ROI of Switching From Manual to Robotic Case Packing?

There is no typical figure, because payback depends on your plant. The estimate compares the installed cost of the system with the annual savings: operators packing cases per shift, shifts per day, local labor cost, output the cartoner gains when packing stops limiting it, cartons scuffed or crushed by handling and time saved at changeovers. A TRF Sales Engineer builds it with your data.
