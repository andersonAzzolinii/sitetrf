## What Is a Flow Wrap Case Packer?

A flow wrap case packer is secondary packaging equipment that collects flow packs from one or more flow wrappers and loads them into a shipping case through its open top. It receives packages at wrapper speed, arranges them in the count and orientation the case requires and places them in a way that protects the wrap and the product inside.

Every TRF case packer loads from above, and the full case moves on to closing, conveying and palletizing. The [top load case packer](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/top-load-case-packer/) page explains the format for the whole family.

Flow wrapped products reach the end of the line in a fast, continuous stream, and they behave differently from rigid packages:

- **Soft, not rigid.** The film follows the shape of the product, so packages flex when handled and settle when stacked.
- **Seal geometry.** The fin seal along one face and the crimped ends add thickness at specific points, which affects how packages nest.
- **Brittle contents.** Biscuits, wafers, crackers and thin bars break if they are squeezed, dropped or loaded under too many layers.
- **Position at the discharge.** Spacing and orientation can vary when the wrapper accelerates, stops or restarts.

Flow wrap lines are common in [bakery and snacks](https://trfautomation.com/industries/bakery-and-snacks/), [confectionery](https://trfautomation.com/industries/confectionery/) and [food processing](https://trfautomation.com/industries/food-processing/) plants, often with many SKUs on the same wrapper.

## Does a Flow Wrap Case Packer Need Vision-Guided Robotics?

Only when package positions vary. When flow packs leave the wrapper with consistent spacing and orientation, indexed picking is simple: the robot knows where each package will be. When packages arrive rotated, shingled, with variable gaps or merged from more than one wrapper, vision guidance lets the delta robots find each package before picking it. TRFRapidpack supports both approaches.

During the application review, TRF looks at how packages leave each wrapper, how they are conveyed to the packer and what happens to the flow when a wrapper stops. Accumulation between wrapper and packer absorbs short stops, so the case packer keeps a stable rhythm.

The end-of-arm tool is chosen for the package, not for the category. Film, fin seal position, weight, headspace and the orientation required in the case decide how the package is held and whether the robot picks one package or a group at a time. TRF validates the tool with your real flow packs, including packages at the size and weight limits of each SKU.

## How Are Flow Packs Packed Into Cases?

Flow packs are packed in a pattern that sets the count, rows, layers and orientation in each case. The pattern defines the cycle the packer must perform and the case that will be used, so it is decided early in the project.

- **On edge, in rows.** Packages stand on their long edge, side by side, common for bars and cookie packs and easy to unload at the store.
- **Flat, in layers.** Packages lie face up or face down in stacked layers, common for thin or wide flow packs that need support.
- **Shingled.** Packages overlap like roof shingles, which can stabilize soft packages.
- **Alternating fin seals.** Rows rotate so fin seals and crimped ends do not stack on each other, which keeps the layer flat.

Because flow packs compress, the case must let the flaps close without squeezing the layer while still holding the load stable in transport. The number of layers also sets how much weight rests on the bottom row, so fragile products may need fewer layers or a different orientation.

Flow wrapping lines often carry several flavors, seasonal formats and customer-specific cases. TRFRapidpack changes products and patterns by recipe, with reduced mechanical adjustments, so many changeovers become software selections. The project documents which SKUs share a recipe and which, if any, need a format part.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a Flow Pack the Same as a Flow Wrap?

Yes. Flow wrap, flow pack and horizontal form-fill-seal describe the same package: a tube of film formed around the product, sealed along its length and crimped at both ends. Buyers in the US usually say flow wrap or flow wrapped product, and TRF uses flow pack for the same soft, roughly rectangular package. On this page, all of these terms mean one package.

### Can Several Flow Wrappers Feed One Case Packer?

It can, when the combined rate and the layout allow it. TRF reviews how many wrappers feed the packer, how packages arrive, how much accumulation exists and how the flow behaves when one wrapper stops. The infeed, the picking method and the collation are then defined for the combined flow, often with vision guidance when lanes merge.

### How Does a Case Packer Protect Delicate or Irregularly Shaped Products?

By controlling the pick, the placement and the load in the case. The tool holds the package securely without pressing on the product and releases it in position rather than dropping it. TRFRapidpack is designed for controlled handling of delicate, flexible or irregularly shaped products, and TRF checks broken product, seal condition and pattern stability with real flow packs.

### How Many Products per Minute Can a Robotic Case Packer Run?

TRFRapidpack is designed for applications of about 100 to 200 products per minute, TRFOnepack for about 20 to 50 and TRFDroppack for about 20 to 500. TRF publishes application ranges, not machine speeds: actual capacity depends on the flow pack, the pick tool, the pack pattern and the infeed, and TRF engineering validates it for each line.

### Can Case Packing Equipment Integrate With My Existing Palletizer and Conveyors?

Yes. Each TRF case packing platform can run standalone or integrate with conveyors, case handling, palletizing and intralogistics as one end-of-line system. TRFIntraflow carries sealed cases to a TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed palletizer, and interfaces with your wrappers and existing equipment are defined during project engineering. See [end-of-line integration](https://trfautomation.com/conveyor-systems/end-of-line-integration/).

### What Is the Typical ROI of Switching From Manual to Robotic Case Packing?

There is no typical figure, because payback depends on your plant. The estimate compares the installed cost of the system with the annual savings: operators casing packages by hand per shift, shifts per day, local labor cost, wrapper output lost to packing backlogs, broken product and time saved at flavor and format changes. A TRF Sales Engineer builds it with your data.
