## What Does an Industrial Conveyor System Do on a Production Line?

An industrial conveyor system does four jobs at once: transport, flow management, routing and integration with the machines at both ends. The difference between a line that flows and a line that stops is usually in the last three.

Transport carries product between machines, across levels and through work areas. Flow management accumulates product when a downstream machine pauses, meters it so a robot picks at a steady rhythm and creates gaps where a scanner or diverter needs them. Routing merges lines, diverts to a second lane, sorts by SKU or destination and rejects product that fails inspection, while integration links the conveyor to the machines through sensors, PLC and HMI, barcode and vision interfaces.

A conveyor that only transports leaves the line exposed. If a palletizer pauses for a pallet change and there is no accumulation, the case packer upstream has to stop. Those are design questions, not catalog questions, and they are why TRF engineers conveying as a system.

## What Is the Difference Between Accumulation, Merging, Diverting and Sorting?

Each function solves a different flow problem, and the wrong one in the wrong place creates the jam it was meant to prevent. **Accumulation** builds a buffer so upstream equipment keeps running while a downstream machine pauses. **Zero-pressure accumulation** does the same in zones that stop and release individually, so units do not push against each other and release stays predictable.

**Metering** releases units at a controlled rate and **gapping** creates space between them, which matters wherever a scanner, a diverter or a robot picks. **Merging** brings two or more flows into one lane and depends on metering to avoid collisions at the junction.

**Diverting** transfers units out of the main lane, for a second packing station or a reject line, and **sorting** routes units by destination, usually from barcode or vision data. A **reject station** removes flagged units without stopping the flow.

## Information to Prepare Before Requesting a Conveyor Layout

Before a first conversation with a TRF Sales Engineer, it helps to have:

- **Products and units:** cases, cartons, trays, totes or exposed food, with dimensions and typical weight.
- **Flow map:** where product enters and leaves the system, and which machines sit at each end.
- **Rates and shifts:** average rates, peak rates and planned growth.
- **Environment and sanitation:** dry, low-moisture or washdown, and how the area is cleaned today.
- **Layout and existing equipment:** drawings with floor space, levels, columns and aisles, plus the case packer, palletizer, scanner or warehouse system the conveyor connects to.
