## What Is an Accumulation Conveyor System?

An accumulation conveyor system is a section of conveyor that lets product wait, in a controlled way, when the equipment downstream cannot take it yet. Instead of stopping the whole line, cases queue in the accumulation zone until the downstream machine is ready, and then they are released in order.

In TRFIntraflow, accumulation belongs to the Flow Management family, which has four standard modules, each solving a different flow problem. Accumulation creates buffer capacity. Zero-pressure accumulation keeps waiting cases from pressing against each other. Metering releases cases at a controlled rate. Gapping creates the spacing that scanners, diverts and palletizers need.

An engineered accumulating conveyor differs from an improvised buffer because it is sized for the actual rates of the connected machines, controlled by sensors and a PLC, and designed together with the modules around it. A buffer that is not designed with the case packer, the palletizer and the controls in mind rarely performs as expected.

## Why Do Packaging and Palletizing Lines Need Accumulation?

Packaging and palletizing lines need accumulation because the machines on an end of line rarely pause at the same moment, and without a buffer every short stop spreads. The case packer stops to refill its case supply or to change over a product. The palletizer stops for a pallet change, a slip sheet or a full pallet leaving the cell. A checkweigher or a scanner rejects a case and the flow hesitates.

Without a buffer, each of these pauses travels backward through the line: a palletizer that pauses briefly can stop the case packer, which stops the machine that feeds it, and the restart can take longer than the original pause. With a buffer sized for the stops the line has to absorb, upstream machines keep running while the downstream machine recovers. The same logic works in reverse, when a full buffer keeps the palletizer building pallets while the case packer is stopped.

## How Does Zero-Pressure Accumulation Work on a Case Conveyor?

Zero-pressure accumulation divides the conveyor into zones controlled by sensors, and each zone stops independently, so every case waits in its own zone instead of pushing against the case ahead. Cases stop with a defined space between them and restart in the same order, with no build-up of force along the queue.

On a conventional accumulating conveyor, the queue builds pressure: the cases at the tail push on the cases at the head, and the pressure grows with the length of the queue. That can crush lightly built cartons, misalign trays, mark printed cases and cause jams at the release point. Zero-pressure accumulation suits light or thin-walled cartons, trays and printed cases, applications where scanners, labelers or diverts need cases separated, and buffers ahead of a palletizer with frequent stops.

Standard accumulation, without zone control, can be enough for sturdy cases on shorter buffers. TRF selects between them during engineering, based on the case, the length of the buffer and the way the downstream machine receives product, using the application data you share.

## What Is the Difference Between Metering and Gapping?

Metering releases cases at a controlled rate, and gapping creates a defined space between cases, so the machine or device that receives them can handle each case individually. They are separate functions that usually work together, often in this order on a TRFIntraflow line: accumulation to hold, metering to release and gapping to space.

**Metering** matters wherever a stream of cases reaches a point that can only accept so many at a time. At a merge, it keeps two lines from arriving at the same moment. At a palletizer infeed, it presents cases at the rate the palletizer can accept. After an accumulation zone releases, it stops the whole queue from rushing forward at once.

**Gapping** turns a tight, irregular stream into one with consistent spacing. Scanners read a case more reliably when it is separated from its neighbors, a divert needs a gap to move one case without catching the next, and a palletizer works better when each case arrives at a predictable position. The release rate of each metering point is defined per project, from your line rates and case dimensions.

## Where Should Accumulation Go in an End-of-Line System?

Accumulation goes wherever two machines with different stop patterns are connected, and the most common positions are the discharge of the case packer and the infeed of the palletizer. These are the positions a flow review looks at first:

- **After the case packer.** A buffer lets the case packer keep running while a downstream stop clears, and lets the line downstream keep working during a case packer changeover. Cases can come from the [TRFRapidpack robotic case packer](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/robotic-case-packer/), the [TRFOnepack integrated case packer](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/integrated-case-packer/), the [TRFDroppack drop packer](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/drop-packer/) or equipment already installed.
- **Ahead of the palletizer.** This buffer keeps the palletizing cell fed during a pallet change and absorbs the stop when a full pallet leaves. Each palletizer, from [TRFCobot](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/cobot-palletizer/) and [TRFCompact](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/compact-palletizer/) to [TRFSpeed](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/high-speed-palletizer/) and the [custom palletizer](https://trfautomation.com/robotic-palletizing/custom-palletizer/), has its own infeed requirements, defined through a palletizer interface.
- **Before and after a merge.** A short buffer before a merge lets metering release cases at the right moment, and a buffer after it smooths the combined stream. The [merge, divert and sorting modules](https://trfautomation.com/conveyor-systems/merge-divert-and-sorting/) rely on this spacing.
- **At level changes.** Before an incline, decline or elevator, a buffer keeps the vertical transfer from becoming the bottleneck, as described on the [elevation conveyors page](https://trfautomation.com/conveyor-systems/elevation-conveyors/).

Signals such as buffer full, buffer empty and downstream ready are engineered once, in one control concept that covers the sensors, PLC and HMI of the conveyor and the machines at both ends. The buffer can then report how full it is, which is often the first sign of a problem downstream.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Do I Know if My Line Needs an Accumulation Conveyor?

Your line likely needs one if the case packer stops at every pallet change, if cases reach the palletizer in bunches, if operators push or straighten cases at transfers, or if several lines share one palletizing area. A short review of stop causes and durations shows where a buffer will recover the most output, and where the line already has enough.

### How Long Should an Accumulation Buffer Be?

Long enough for the upstream machine to keep running through the longest stop the downstream machine is expected to have, at the rate of the line. There is no fixed length: the buffer is calculated from your line rates, case size and stop patterns during the application review and confirmed against the layout and the space available.

### Can Accumulation Prevent the Case Packer From Stopping When the Palletizer Changes Pallets?

Yes, when the buffer is sized for the pallet change. Cases queue on the accumulation section ahead of the palletizer while the pallet is exchanged, and the case packer keeps discharging. When the palletizer resumes, the buffer releases at a metered rate. TRF engineers the buffer and the signals between the machines together, so the behavior is defined before startup.

### How Does Accumulation Work When Several Lines Feed One Palletizer?

Accumulation and merging decide how the lines take turns. Each line has its own buffer, and metering releases cases onto a shared conveyor at a controlled rate, so no line starves or floods the others. The sharing logic is designed together with the merge modules and the palletizer interface, so a stop on one line does not have to stop the rest.

### Can a Conveyor System Integrate With My Palletizer and Case Packer?

Yes. TRFIntraflow includes machine communication and palletizer interfaces, so the accumulation zones exchange signals with the case packer and the palletizer at both ends. Interfaces with TRF case packers and palletizers, or with equipment already installed, are defined during engineering, and one partner is responsible for the complete flow from layout to startup.
