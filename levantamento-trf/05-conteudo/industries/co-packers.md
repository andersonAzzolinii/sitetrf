## What Should a Co-Packer Look for in End-of-Line Automation?

A co-packer should look for flexibility first, because the product mix belongs to the customers and contracts have horizons. A co-packer sells capacity, while a brand owner optimizes a known portfolio, so both buy the same three steps, case packing, conveying and palletizing, but justify the equipment differently. Brand owners are covered on the [consumer packaged goods](https://trfautomation.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/) page.

In contract packaging automation, a line that runs pouches this quarter may run cartons next, and every contract can bring its own case size, count and pallet pattern. The questions that decide a purchase are how much flexibility a machine buys, how quickly it can be set up for a new product, and whether the investment can be spread across several contracts.

## End-of-Line Challenges in Contract Packaging

The end of the line is where a co-packer's own labor concentrates: erecting cases, loading them, sealing them and building pallets to each customer's specification. Three challenges stand out.

### High SKU Mix and Customer-Owned Specifications

A contract packager may run many products from several customers in the same week, each with its own carton, case count and pallet pattern. Automated lines absorb that variety with recipes and tooling validated for the whole product list, so the application review asks for every SKU, not only the fastest one.

### Short Runs and Frequent Changeovers

In contract packaging, a run can last a few hours, so changeover time is a capacity number. Minutes lost at every change add up faster than any gain in rated speed, and a stored recipe lets any trained operator run the change instead of one experienced person.

### Labor Across Shifts

Co-packers often add a second or third shift to cover peaks, and end-of-line positions are hard to keep filled. Automation reduces dependence on repetitive manual palletizing and moves operators to line setup, quality checks and changeovers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is a Cobot?

A cobot is a collaborative robot, an arm designed for shared workspaces at moderate speeds. In palletizing, a cobot picks each case from the infeed conveyor and places it on the pallet in a stored pattern. TRFCobot puts one in a standardized cell built for co-packers and contract manufacturers with short runs and frequent SKU changes.

### Can a Cobot Palletizer Handle Multiple SKUs and Pallet Patterns?

Yes. Built for high-mix palletizing, TRFCobot stores multiple recipes, pallet patterns and SKU changes for lines of about 1 to 10 cases per minute with cases up to about 22 lb, so each customer's pattern can be stored and recalled. TRF confirms that the end-of-arm tool and reach cover every case in your mix. Higher rates move to TRFCompact or TRFSpeed.

### Can a Robotic Case Packer Handle Pouches, Trays and Thermoformed Packages in the Same Line?

Yes, within its validated range. TRFRapidpack handles pouches, pillow bags, flow packs, thermoformed packages, trays, cartons, cups, tubs and small packaged products, and changes products and patterns through recipes with reduced mechanical adjustments. That breadth is what makes flexible case packing serve several customers on one line. TRF validates the full product list before quoting.

### What Is the Difference Between an Integrated Case Packer and Separate Erecting, Packing and Sealing Machines?

An integrated case packer erects the case, loads it and seals it in one coordinated machine. TRFOnepack works this way, with one controls platform, fewer interfaces and a smaller footprint than three separate machines. For a contract packager, that means one machine to learn and maintain when the line changes customers.

### Can a Robotic Palletizer Operate Below Freezing?

Yes, with a palletizer built for it. TRFSubzero palletizes in frozen areas below 32°F, which fits co-packers that run frozen food or ice cream programs. The Custom Foods case study documents robotic palletizing below freezing. TRF validates the layout and the equipment for each cold application before quoting.

### How Should a Co-Packer Calculate Payback on Packaging Automation?

Start with the labor hours the equipment replaces, shifts per day, local labor cost and the installed cost, including electricity and maintenance. Then calculate against utilization across contracts, not one product, count changeover time recovered, and compare the payback with the length of the contracts behind the line.
