## Does a Case Packer Need Vision-Guided Robotics for High-Mix Production?

Not always. Vision-guided robotic handling pays off when products arrive in variable positions, pack patterns change often or the package is appearance-sensitive, which describes many candy and chocolate lines. Repetitive bags packed in the same pattern all shift may not need it.

TRFRapidpack uses vision-guided or indexed handling, depending on the application, and is designed for controlled handling of delicate, flexible or irregularly shaped products. Glossy, printed wraps show handling marks, so the end-of-arm tool is matched to the wrap and each product is validated with its real package before the pack pattern is fixed. When a bag is repetitive and tolerates a controlled drop, TRFDroppack loads it by gravity, without robotic end-of-arm tooling.

## How Should a Confectionery Plant Size Case Packing and Palletizing?

Size the case packing and palletizing for the peak rate the plant commits to, then choose platforms that scale. A line sized only for an average rate runs at its limit whenever volume rises.

TRFRapidpack grows from a single robotic module to several robots, and palletizing can start with one cell and later consolidate lines into a centralized TRFSpeed area. Reserve the floor space and utilities for the next machine in the first layout. Recipes for pack patterns, counts per case and pallet patterns let the same equipment run different formats.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How Does a Case Packer Protect Delicate or Irregularly Shaped Products?

By controlling how the package is picked, placed and stacked. Robotic picking with an end-of-arm tool matched to the wrap limits pressure on the package, placing rather than dropping avoids scuffs, and a pack pattern matched to the case keeps packages from rubbing in transit. TRFRapidpack is built around controlled handling, and TRF validates each product with real samples.

### How Fast Can a Case Packer Change Over Between SKUs?

When the change is recipe-only, it is a selection at the operator panel: product size, count per case, case format and pallet pattern are stored and recalled. TRFRapidpack changes products and patterns with reduced mechanical adjustments. Some changes still need tooling or guide adjustments, so TRF reviews the full SKU list during the application study and states which changes are recipe-only.

### How Many Products per Minute Can a Robotic Case Packer Run?

TRFRapidpack is designed for applications of about 100 to 200 products per minute on medium- to high-speed lines. Because light confectionery units are rarely limited by payload, the number of picks per minute matters most, and the platform can add robotic modules as volume grows. The final rate is validated for each product, pack pattern and case.

### How Do You Choose Between TRFCobot, TRFCompact and TRFSpeed?

Choose by line speed, case weight and floor space: TRFCobot fits light confectionery cases at lower rates with frequent pattern changes. TRFCompact adds industrial duty cycle and speed in restricted space. TRFSpeed fits plants that consolidate several lines into one palletizing area. TRF validates the choice with your real cases and pallet patterns.

TRF supplies candy packaging automation and chocolate packaging automation as one integrated end-of-line solution: TRFRapidpack, TRFOnepack or TRFDroppack pack the cases, TRFIntraflow moves and routes them, and TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed build the pallet. ### How Is Payback Estimated for Confectionery Automation?

Payback depends on the labor hours the system replaces, shifts per day, local labor cost and the installed cost of the system. The estimate divides the system cost, including maintenance, by the annual net savings: the labor cost of manual palletizing minus the electricity the robot uses.
