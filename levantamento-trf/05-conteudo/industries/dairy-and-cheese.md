## What Features Reduce Cleaning Time on a Hygienic Conveyor?

Open construction, surfaces that drain and parts that come off without tools reduce cleaning time, because the crew can reach, wash and inspect everything in the window between runs. In dairy and cheese, cleaning is production time. TRFCleanflow comes in two lines because dairy plants have two kinds of zones, and the design targets below are confirmed for each project.

| Design target | TRFCleanflow Dry | TRFCleanflow Washdown |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | 304 stainless steel, open frame, reduced horizontal surfaces | 304 stainless sanitary construction, 316 optional, continuous weld, sloped surfaces |
| Drainage | No unnecessary cavities or product accumulation points | Self-draining surfaces, no exposed hollow-frame openings |
| Belting | Food-grade belting, easy-access belt and components | Washdown-compatible food-grade belt, quick-release tension |
| Access for sanitation | Vacuum, brushing and wipe-down access | Tool-less belt lifting or removal where practical, removable guides and wear strips |
| Electrical | IP65 electrical components as a baseline | IP66/IP69K components in direct washdown zones, NEMA 4X control enclosure |

For a dairy plant, the choice follows the sanitation plan, not the product alone: a packaging room cleaned dry and a cheese conversion area washed frequently can sit in the same building and need different construction.

## Can a Gravity Case Packer Handle Grated Cheese Bags?

Yes. TRFDroppack packs grated cheese bags: it collates flexible bags into the pack pattern and loads them into the case with a controlled drop, without robotic end-of-arm tooling. For grated cheese bag packing, the condition is a repetitive pattern and a bag that tolerates controlled gravity loading.

Block cheese, sliced cheese and snack formats usually take the robotic route instead, because orientation matters: TRFRapidpack places thermoformed packs and trays, and TRFOnepack combines erecting, packing and sealing on smaller lines. TRF validates the platform with your real bags and cases.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a Robotic Case Packer Handle Yogurt Cups and Tubs?

Yes. TRFRapidpack handles cups and tubs, and TRFOnepack handles cups. Cup geometry, lid type and case format decide how the package is gripped, placed and protected, so TRF defines the end-of-arm tool and pack pattern with real packages before the design of your dairy case packing line is fixed.

### Can a Conveyor System Be Designed for Both Dry and Washdown Zones in the Same Plant?

Yes. TRFCleanflow has a Dry line for dry and low-moisture zones and a Washdown line for frequent washdown. TRF engineers the transition between zones so the hygienic section ends where the sanitation requirement ends, and TRFIntraflow takes over once product is in a case.

### Can a Robotic Palletizer Operate Below Freezing (32°F)?

Yes, with equipment built for it. When the palletizing area runs below 32°F, as in frozen dairy production, the TRF line points to TRFSubzero. For any other cold room, TRF engineering confirms the platform during the application review. Ice cream has its own page: [frozen food and ice cream](https://trfautomation.com/industries/frozen-food-and-ice-cream/).

### How Do You Choose Between TRFCobot, TRFCompact and TRFSpeed?

Choose by line speed, case weight and floor space: TRFCobot fits smaller dairy lines that still stack pallets by hand and change patterns often. TRFCompact adds industrial duty cycle in a packaging room where floor space is scarce. TRFSpeed fits high-volume plants that consolidate several lines into one palletizing area. TRF validates the choice with your real cases and pallet patterns.

### Can One Automation Partner Supply Palletizing, Case Packing and Conveyors for a Dairy Plant?

Yes. TRF supplies cheese packaging automation and dairy packaging automation as one integrated solution: TRFCleanflow carries exposed product, TRFRapidpack, TRFOnepack or TRFDroppack pack the cases, TRFIntraflow moves them, and TRFCobot, TRFCompact or TRFSpeed build the pallet. One partner is responsible from layout to startup, and [Parts and Service](https://trfautomation.com/parts-and-service/) supports TRF-supplied equipment afterward.

### How Is Payback Estimated for Dairy Packaging Automation?

Payback depends on the labor hours the system replaces, shifts per day, local labor cost and the installed cost of the system. The estimate divides the system cost, including maintenance, by the annual net savings: the labor cost of manual palletizing minus the electricity the robot uses. The [palletizing ROI calculator](https://trfautomation.com/tools/palletizing-roi-calculator/) applies this method to your location, your shifts and the people who palletize by hand today.
