The case covers two of its facilities: a production center that is the company's second busiest in the world, and a distribution center that is one of its ten main global logistics centers. Both move products of many shapes, sizes and weights, from small to large packages, through the same infrastructure.

## The Challenge: Accuracy in a High-Mix Material Flow

The problem was accuracy in the internal flow of materials. In a fully harmonized production and logistics operation, the right product has to reach the right stage at the right moment, and that accuracy supports the performance of the whole operation.

## The Solution: A New Automatic Material Flow

TRF was responsible for designing and installing a completely new automatic solution, and the customer credits it with a significant improvement in productivity. TRF worked in partnership with a specialist in vertical transport, whose lifts serve the production line, and used a modular conveyor platform and roller systems for both facilities.

The modular platform let the customer create solutions for the needs of its products by shape, size and weight. The flow runs from the product separation and packaging area, through the production and assembly line, forward to shipping to the end consumer.

## What the Customer Reports

The customer's distribution center management reports a capacity of 16,000 boxes per day, around 1,200 boxes per hour. With a focused project, the customer reported an increase of around 9% in productivity in the first three months, which allowed orders to be processed faster and reduced processing time for reseller orders inside the distribution center.

The same account explains the choice of supplier: a solution aimed mainly at the safety of employees, which the customer describes as a company value, and a partnership already established at another of its plants.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a Conveyor System Be Customized to My Exact Plant Layout?

Yes. An engineered conveyor system is laid out around your building, your machines and your product mix, using standard modules for conveying, accumulation, diverting, routing, elevation and integration. In the Avon project, a modular conveyor platform let the customer create solutions for products of different shapes, sizes and weights across two facilities, with lifts between levels.

### Can a Conveyor System Integrate With My Palletizer and Case Packer?

Yes. Integration is simplest when conveying, case packing and palletizing are engineered together, with accumulation placed to decouple the machines and controls that coordinate the flow. When the whole path from packaging discharge to shipping is one scope, it falls under end-of-line integration, with one partner responsible for how the machines work together.

### Can a Single Supplier Engineer, Build and Install My Entire Conveyor System?

Yes. TRF Industrial Automation engineers, builds and installs conveyor systems as part of the line, with one partner responsible for engineering, manufacturing, controls, installation and commissioning. The same scope can include case packing and palletizing. In this case, TRF designed and installed the complete automatic flow, working with a vertical transport specialist for the lifts.

### How Is Vertical Transport Handled in an End-of-Line System?

Lifts and elevators move product between levels without manual handling, and they are specified by load, cycle rate and footprint. In an integrated system, they are controlled as part of the same flow as the conveyors, so accumulation and routing account for the time a load spends changing levels. In this case, lifts connect levels inside the production line.
