## How Does the Calculator Estimate Payback?

The calculator follows the method of the A3 (Association for Advancing Automation) Robot System Value Calculator and compares the labor cost of manual palletizing with the cost of owning and operating a robotic system over a 20-year period, the period used by the A3 method:

1. **Manual labor per year** = people per shift × hours per shift × number of shifts × days per week × 52 weeks × hourly labor rate.
2. **Adjusted system price** = reference price × the scale of automation for your workforce (1 to 2 workers per shift: 1x; 3 to 4: 1.5x; 5 to 6: 1.95x; 7 to 9: 2.5x; 10 or more: 3.3x).
3. **Electricity per year** = annual operating hours × your location's electricity rate × a 20 kW reference draw × an electricity multiplier of 1x (1 to 2 workers), 2x (3 to 4), 3x (5 to 6), 4x (7 to 9) or 5x (10 or more).
4. **Total system cost** = adjusted system price + maintenance at 5% of that price per year for 20 years.
5. **Break-even, in years** = total system cost ÷ (manual labor per year minus electricity per year).
6. **20-year labor savings** = manual labor over 20 years minus the robotic system over 20 years (total system cost plus 20 years of electricity).

The method is simple and undiscounted, so anyone can rebuild it in a spreadsheet and carry the annual figures into a net present value model.

## Which Inputs Move Robotic Palletizer Payback the Most?

Test these four inputs one at a time:

- **Shifts.** The investment is paid once while the labor savings repeat on every shift, so payback falls as shifts increase.
- **People per shift.** Count only the people who would leave the task. A realistic figure protects the credibility of your request.
- **Labor rate.** The location reference is an average production wage. Replace it with your actual rate when you know it.
- **System cost.** A reference list price without installation and integration makes payback look shorter than it will be; use Custom to test your quote.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is the Typical Payback Period for a Robotic Palletizer?

There is no single typical figure. Payback depends on the labor hours the palletizer replaces, shifts per day, local labor cost and the cost of the system you buy. The calculator applies the method on this page to your inputs, and a TRF Sales Engineer completes the estimate with the installed scope of your project.

### Where Does the Reference Labor Rate Come From?

The reference hourly rate for each state is a production wage from public U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics data, a base wage without payroll costs. It fills in when you select your state, and you can edit it at any time. Use your own loaded rate, including benefits, when you present the estimate to finance.

### Is the Calculator Result a Quote?

No. The result is an initial planning estimate built from your inputs and reference data. Your final price and payback come from a quote, which a TRF Sales Engineer prepares after validating the platform, tooling, pallet patterns, conveyors, installation and integration for your line.

### Can I Use This Calculator for a Case Packer?

The calculator is built for palletizing, and its platform list covers TRFCobot, TRFCompact and TRFSpeed. The labor logic is similar for case packing: the manual labor removed, compared with the cost of the system. For TRFRapidpack, TRFOnepack or TRFDroppack, talk with a TRF Sales Engineer about your products and rates on the [case packing](https://trfautomation.com/case-packing/) page.

### Why Does the Calculator Ask for a Work Email?

The preview appears as you type. The work email unlocks the full breakdown and lets TRF follow up with a Sales Engineer who starts from your numbers instead of a blank page. Personal email domains are not accepted. TRF uses your email and inputs to send the estimate and follow up about your project, as described in the [Privacy Policy](https://trfautomation.com/privacy-policy/).
